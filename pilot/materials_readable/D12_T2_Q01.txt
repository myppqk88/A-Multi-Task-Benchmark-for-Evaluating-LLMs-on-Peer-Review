==================== 审稿意见 ====================

It would also be good if the authors explained whether the patterns could be taken to be general – or are the results a feature of the regions/countries that selected themselves into the university initiative?


==================== 作者回应 ====================

Although our study focuses mainly on the ERUA regions and countries, our findings can be considered more broadly. We state the reason for this in the Methodological Section, which is that the countries under study were chosen not only because of the ReERUA Project, but also because they represent all key educational/employment regimes in Europe. Specifically, we write:
“The analysis is conducted at the regional level (NUTS-2) and its geographical scope covers five countries: Greece, Bulgaria, France, Germany, and Denmark. These were chosen on the basis that they represent the main educational/employment regimes in Europe: the universalistic of the Nordic countries, the employment-centered of Continental Europe, the sub-protective of the Mediterranean countries, and the post-socialist of Eastern Europe (Pohl & Walther 2007; Farkas, 2011). While both France and Germany fall under the Continental model, they are both analyzed due to their stark differences in terms of their wage structures (Hein & Vogel, 2009) and educational models (O’Reilly et al., 2019). Given the research’s geographical scope covering all main European regimes pertaining to employment and skills, its findings can be contextualized more broadly and provide insight into the EU as a whole.”
The above is also implied in the Introduction and the Discussion.
