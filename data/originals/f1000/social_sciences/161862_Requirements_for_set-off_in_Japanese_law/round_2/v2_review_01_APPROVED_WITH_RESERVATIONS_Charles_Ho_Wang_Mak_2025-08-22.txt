Article: Requirements for set-off in Japanese law
DOI: 10.12688/f1000research.161862.2
Version: 2
Reviewer: Charles Ho Wang Mak
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2025-08-22
Report ID: 394783
============================================================

This article offers a valuable and much-needed account of Japanese set-off law in English. It provides a comprehensive overview of the requirements for set-off under Japanese law, a topic that has not been extensively discussed in international literature. In particular, the article's systematic breakdown of set-off into affirmative and negative requirements is excellent. Your use of specific examples and clear explanations of concepts like "Sousai-Tekijou" (eligibility for set-off) and the distinction between active and passive claims makes the complex topic approachable. The section on set-off in bankruptcy proceedings is also particularly strong.
However, you should consider a few key improvements:
While the abstract provides a good overview, a formal introduction would set the stage, state the paper's aim, and highlight its contribution to the English-language literature.
Your response acknowledges that some passages are difficult to translate directly from Japanese legal texts. I suggest a collaborative effort with a native English speaker with a legal background to smooth out these parts. The goal is not to change the meaning but to improve readability for an international audience. Specific sections that could benefit from this include the third paragraph under 1-1-2 and the first paragraph under 3-4-2.
While you intentionally avoided a full comparative study for the sake of conciseness, a brief, well-structured section in the main body (not just the conclusion) could address the German and French law comparisons. This could also be an opportunity to clarify or revise statements, such as the one about German law's stance on knowledge of assignment (BGB Art. 392 and 406).
If possible, the complexity of set-off, especially in a tripartite relationship or in bankruptcy, would be significantly easier to understand with a few simple diagrams. These visuals could illustrate the flow of claims and the impact of concepts like attachment and assignment.
