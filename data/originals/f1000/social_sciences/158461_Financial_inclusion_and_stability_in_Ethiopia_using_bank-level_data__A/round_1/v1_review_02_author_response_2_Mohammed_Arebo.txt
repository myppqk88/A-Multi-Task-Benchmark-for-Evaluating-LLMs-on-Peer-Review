Author Response to Review 2 (v1)
Author: Mohammed Arebo
Date: 2025-04-15
============================================================

We sincerely appreciate the time and effort you have taken to review our manuscript. Your thoughtful comments have provided valuable guidance, enabling us to strengthen the theoretical, empirical and methodological aspects of the study. Below, we outline the changes made in response to your feedback.
1.    To clarify in the introduction, the two key variables of the study, namely what is meant by financial inclusion and banking stability: these two concepts are too broad and must be defined precisely.
Response: Thank you for your valuable comment.  The introduction has been revised to provide precise definitions of financial inclusion and banking stability.
2.    From the conceptualization of the key variables, it would be more structuring in the literature review to focus on the work related to these two concepts in order to be able to ultimately deduce ideas or hypotheses to study in the empirical part: a segmented literature review like what has been done does not allow us to know what the usefulness of this step is for the future!!
Response: Thank you for this significant comment. We updated the literature review with the most recent studies and segmented it to focus on the key variables: (i) financial inclusion and banking stability, (ii) non-linear relationship between financial inclusion and bank stability, (iii) the moderating effects of macro-prudential regulation. This restructuring allows for clearer hypothesis formulation and empirical relevance.
3.    It would be useful in the empirical part to detail a little more on the stylized facts of the banks studied.
Response: Thank you very much your feedback. Additional details on the stylized facts of the banks studied have been included in the empirical section.
4.    At the level of dependent variables, namely, financial stability, the Z score is not sufficiently significant for stability, it rather designates profitability. However, if the latter is important for stability, it is not sufficient. Why not consider the ratios of the base as a criterion of stability instead of the Z ratio?
Response: Thank you for your insightful comment. While the Z-score may overemphasize profitability, it was used due to the unavailability of risk-related data. We have acknowledged this limitation in the revised manuscript, stating: “Bank stability is measured solely through the Z-score due to data limitations. Future studies should explore alternative risk-based stability measures.”
