Article: It’s complicated: staff experiences of academic integrity at a Mauritian university
DOI: 10.12688/f1000research.167245.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Martin Oliver
Status: approved
Date: 2026-01-06
Report ID: 443820
============================================================

The article provides an analysis of staff views about academic integrity, comparing and contrasting academic and professional perspectives. This is a welcome approach, which expands previous studies. The empirical site is also under-represented in previous research, making this work distinctive.
I really appreciated the care taken in describing the research process, and the clarity about positionally. This was handled in an exemplary way.
Structurally, the order of sections felt a little unusual to me; I would expect positionally to be address in or around the methodology section, and I would normally expect the presentation of themes to be followed by a discussion section relating this to the literature, then conclusions, which would incorporate recommendations. However, all these sections are present in the submitted paper - discussion incorporated with presentation of themes, recommendations preceding conclusions - so I do not think this structure is an issue that needs to be addressed. (Personally, I would also suggest deleting the sentence about the value of research being recognised when translated into practice; I don't think this is needed, and it also doesn't address research that is valued through citation in other research.)
There are also some minor clarifications that would be worth adding. This includes explaining what Bretag's model consists of, when it's first mentioned; and also, explaining the SADC acronym the first time this is used. I was also slightly confused by one sentence: "The academic staff focus group included six participants from all faculty members.” I wasn't sure if the final clause was intended to signal recruitment from across all disciplines at the institution.
