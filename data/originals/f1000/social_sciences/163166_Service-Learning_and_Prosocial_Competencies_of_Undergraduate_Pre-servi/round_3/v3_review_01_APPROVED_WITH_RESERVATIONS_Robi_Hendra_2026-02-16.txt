Article: Service-Learning and Prosocial Competencies of Undergraduate Pre-service Teachers Toward Graduate Employability Skills
DOI: 10.12688/f1000research.163166.3
Version: 3
Reviewer: Robi Hendra
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2026-02-16
Report ID: 454388
============================================================

This work has great potential and relevance. Service-based learning to enhance the prosocial skills of prospective teachers in Nigeria is timely. The mixed-methods sequential explanation approach is also conceptually sound. The current version requires significant changes.
Comments 1
This study lacks instrument validation evidence, a major limitation. The authors claim overall reliability (α = 0.79) without reporting Cronbach's alpha for each subconstruct, content validity, EFA, or CFA. This is important since the multidimensional instrument assesses prosocial competence. Without rigorous construct validation, all quantitative data is suspect.
Comment 2
Inconsistencies remain in the statistical analysis. The authors cite a "t-test of means" but provide the F-statistic. The language and tables differ (e.g., the pre-teamwork score was 2.45 vs. 2.62 in the prior version). The authors don't give effect sizes (Cohen's d) or 95% confidence intervals, making it difficult to judge the changes' practical robustness.
Comment 3
The quantitative sampling method (150 students from five departments) is unclear. Convenience, purposive, or stratified sampling? This ambiguity raises questions about selection bias and findings generalizability. The writers must describe the sampling process and sample demographics.
Comment 4
Little is known about the service learning intervention. Readers don't know the six-month activities, meeting duration and frequency, or reflection component. It is impossible to determine if stated improvements are due to the program without a clear and complete explanation of the intervention.
Comment 5
Only 10 team leaders were chosen for the focus group discussion (FGD), which may increase positive response bias. This qualitative sample is unrepresentative and biased toward engaged students. Expanding the qualitative sample or triangulating with individual interviews is advised.
Comment 6
Brief and opaque qualitative data theme analysis is presented. It doesn't explain inductive/deductive coding, theme generation, inter-coder reliability, or Lincoln & Guba's trustworthiness measurements (credibility, dependability, confirmability). This greatly devalues ​​qualitative findings.
Comment 7
The integration of quantitative and qualitative data is weak. Qualitative data simply "supports" quantitative conclusions, but in a sequential explanatory design, it should explain why and how changes occur, especially in empathy, which is scarcely significant. The authors must present together to improve mixed-methods integration.
Comment 8
Typographical errors sentences, repeated concepts, and inconsistent litter the discussion section. The authors must modify and restructure the discussion to be more brief, critical, and openly address the study's shortcomings (particularly empathy's minor statistical significance) and actual policy consequences.
