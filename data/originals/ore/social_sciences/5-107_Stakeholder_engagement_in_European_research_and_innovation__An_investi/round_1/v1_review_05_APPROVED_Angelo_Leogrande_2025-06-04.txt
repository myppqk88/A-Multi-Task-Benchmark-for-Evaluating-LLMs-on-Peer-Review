Article: Stakeholder engagement in European research and innovation: An investigation into how and why EU R&I projects develop engagement tools
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.19907.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Angelo Leogrande
Status: approved
Date: 2025-06-04
Report ID: 54068
============================================================

The article is well written, however it is possible to identify the following critical issues:
• The article refers to the fact that a qualitative analysis was conducted through interviews. However, the essential characteristics of these interviews are not reported. Even if they are qualitative data, they can still be analyzed through classifications and the like.
• The authors used the Atlas software to analyze the results of the qualitative analysis. However, it is not clear what type of qualitative methodology they used. Whether or not they used machine learning and in what way. Whether they simply limited themselves to a lexicometric analysis of the results obtained from the questionnaires. Or whether they translated the qualitative dimension into a quantitative dimension through the proposed software. It is necessary to better specify in the methodology what was achieved in terms of data analytics, data processing, data mining, data ingestion, data interpretation.
• The authors refer to the fact that they used lines of code. It would be appropriate to make these lines of code public in an appendix specifically prepared at the end of the article, also to allow the possible replicability of the article's results.
• The category of stakeholders and stakeholder management is often referred to. However, in the proposed literature there is no reference to Robert Edward Freeman who invented the very idea of ​​stakeholder theory. It would also be necessary to add a section to the article in which to delve deeper into the idea of ​​stakeholder management and highlight the possibilities of this idea - born in the corporate sphere - to serve the interests of public institutions and especially of the research and innovation policies of the European Union. In this sense, it would also be very useful to jointly analyze the idea of ​​stakeholder theory with the idea of ​​open innovation.
• The introduction is too long and tends to also include the analysis of the literature. It would be appropriate to reorganize the article by dividing the introduction from the analysis of the literature. In the introduction, the research question should be expressed, the motivations that push this article to be original, any gap that one wants to fill in the studies on the topic. In the literature analysis, however, it is absolutely necessary to give space to the detailed treatment of references capable of bringing together the treatment of stakeholder theory in the public sphere in connection with the idea of ​​open innovation and innovation ecosystems inserted in the context of European public finance.
• In table 1, it is necessary to add a column indicating the stakeholders most involved in the proposed research project. In fact, the article often speaks of stakeholders, but they are never defined in detail. Generally, each research project has its own definition of stakeholders. It would be very useful to define stakeholders also to highlight the fact that such projects can also partly differ in the sense of identifying the main stakeholders. For example, the article refers to citizens as stakeholders. Of course, in a broad sense, this may be true. However, when applying the stakeholder management model, we generally mean more limited categories of stakeholders that may concern, for example, company workers or consumers, or the various project partners.
Following these changes, it is possible to orient the article towards publication.
