Point by point responses:

1. Point on a more critical reflection on EU RRI and Missions



In the revised version of the article, we add a reflection in the theory section highlighting the critical literature (RRI and MOIP) on the challenges of engagement led by these concepts as well as a short reflection within the EU context. For example: "Furthermore, more critical perspectives on engagement in transformative R&I point out that mission-led approaches sometimes fail to resolve issues such as stakeholder monotony (cf. Kirchherr et al., 2023), and lack of consideration for geographical diversity (Uyarra et al., 2025)."
We return to this in the discussion by referring to the challenges of engagement in supranational transformative innovation.

2. Point on clarifying how the 'Purpose' stage fits in the project timeline



We see the Purpose stage encompassing activities largely within the project timeline. While the very general ideas of tools can be outlined in the project proposals (e.g. "development of a digital engagement platform for farmers"), this kind of a description still needs to be operationalised through a long process. This process usually includes understanding the needs that the tool should fulfil, the feasible format and functionalities, as well as the content. This is clarified in the text as shown below:
"While a general idea of a tool might be developed before the project as a part of the proposal, the critical conceptualisation of the tool happens at the beginning of the project."

3. Point on clarifying how the concept of 'tools' is used



Following your suggestion, we have added some examples of tools to make the concept of engagement tools more understandable for the reader:
"Practically, these tools can take shape as scripts for multi-stakeholder workshops, digital data sharing platforms, or networking apps."

4. Point on incorporating more examples of tools



We have now adjusted the results at several points to make more explicit references to the types of tools being discussed:
On the other hand, user groups such as citizens were perceived as less knowledgeable, particularly about the feasibility of implementing certain changes "ideas for technical tools like apps and websites that facilitated collaboration."
"When it came to developing tools based on digital platforms," Partners with IT expertise such as coders and web developers often had different views on the feasibility of implementing user feedback compared to project coordinators, or research partners, often leading to discord.
Once the translation was underway or completed, making adjustments to the tools "(whether session outlines, or digital platforms)" also became a major challenge.
For "digital tools", that required users to create accounts, it was possible to collect additional data about tool use.
"While digital tools like apps and websites are vulnerable to becoming unusable due to a lack of maintenance, even methodologies and session outlines for workshops can become inaccessible if their hosting expires or external links in PDFs break over time."

5. Point on referring to
sustainability
innovation



The sustainability-focused innovation references the policy grounding of the projects that are the empirical basis of the article. These projects are based in the European Green Deal and Food2030 frameworks that emphasise sustainability aspects of innovation.
We realise that within the abstract the reference to sustainability might cause confusion so we removed it and refer simply to "innovation". Additionally, we expanded on the introduction to more clearly reflect the gap that the study addresses. More specific overview of this is available in other comments.

6. Point on sentence clarity



We have now adjusted the wording to be more straight forward: "In recent decades, Europe has grappled with several challenges, including geopolitical crises, the climate crisis and biodiversity loss, maintaining global competitiveness, and others."

7. Point on interview questions



We now include the interview guide as an appendix to improve the transparency of our data collection process.

8. Point on additional information in the coding process



In the subsection 'data analysis' it is now explained that we undertook a second coding step following Saldana's axial coding process:
"Subsequent to open coding, we engaged in a second cycle of analysis to group these codes into broader conceptual categories. Drawing on principles of axial coding (Saldaña, 2021), we examined relationships among the codes, explored underlying patterns, and grouped them into higher-order categories. Through this process, it became evident that participants consistently described their activities in chronological sequences, structuring their accounts around distinct stages of tool development and dissemination.Based on this, we identified four phases of the tool development and dissemination process, as described in the consequent section of this paper. The 46 codes that were created during the previous analysis step were then assigned to one or more of the stages."

9. Point on methodological transparency for potential replication



The code tree and interview guide have now been included as appendices to the paper, increasing transparency and allowing for greater replicability.
