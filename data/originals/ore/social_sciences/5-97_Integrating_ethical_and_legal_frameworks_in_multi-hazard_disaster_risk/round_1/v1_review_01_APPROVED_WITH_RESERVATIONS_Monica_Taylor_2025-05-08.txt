Thank you for the opportunity to review this open letter entitled,
“Integrating ethical and legal frameworks in multi hazard disaster risk management.”
As it is an open Letter not a research article, it therefore has a different focus. I provide this feedback based on the criteria and guidelines for reviewing an open letter.
Is the rationale for the Open Letter provided in sufficient detail?
The rationale for the open letter was not fully apparent to me in the abstract or the document itself. The objective of the open letter appears to be to critically evaluate the success of an integrated Decision Support System (DSS) developed by the European MEDiate project. But at various stages when reading the article I felt that it did not provide much of a critical lens, and was instead written in a descriptive and promotional style to explain the benefits of the DSS system. I also wasn't sure about the author's standpoint and whether they had some involvement in the creation of the DSS system that is the subject of the article.
My main critical and constructive feedback relates to the structure of the article. I think it could benefit from a restructure that brings forward the literature review sections, which are provided in the latter part of the paper (part 5). Then a more coherent structure might be: (a) identify the problem or issue under consideration; (b) state a methodology for critical analysis, informed by the literature review, and then (c) apply the literature to the case study (DSS system) to determine whether or not it is meeting the objectives, namely, the integration of ethical and human rights frameworks into multi-hazard DRM. This is where the differing national frameworks and approaches can be introduced, together with key challenges and emerging trends.
2. Does the article adequately reference differing views and opinions?
It was very interesting to read about the different national frameworks in MEDiate testbed countries. This was an enjoyable part of the paper that gives context to its overall objective. However, again to structure, I think introducing the national frameworks after the literature review would foreground the evidence against which the DSS system can be evaluated in relation to the national frameworks provided.
3. Are all factual statements correct, and are statements and arguments made adequately supported by citations?
For a piece of this length that draws upon a large body of disaster risk management literature, it is considerably under-cited. Citations are often not provided where they ought to be.  An example is on page 8 in part 5.2, where the author states, “Studies highlight that many failures in disaster coordination stem from different agencies and systems being unable to seamlessly share data and models.”  A statement such as this requires a citation to point the reader to the evidence (the studies) that it refers to.  At this stage I do not consider that this article satisfies the criteria that all factual statements are correct and adequately supported by citations.
4. Is the Open Letter written in accessible language?
Yes, the language is accessible. There is a repetitive paragraph on Page 3. The second paragraph in Part 2 almost repeats verbatim the preceding paragraph and should be deleted. There also appears to be a typographical error on page 8 where the Covid-19 pandemic sentence says ‘…international organisations advising against
traying
rights through public health measures.’  Is this meant to say betraying?
5. Where applicable, are recommendations and next steps explained clearly for others to follow?
Yes, section 6 offers some suggestions for how MEDiate DSS can become more participatory, and it also identifies future research avenues. Section 6 covers a lot of good analytical ground that could be drawn out earlier in the article, applied to the case study and through the national frameworks, and not left right to the end.
Overall, this is a very interesting article on an important topic that shows promise. With some restructuring it would achieve more clarity and coherence. Closer attention to citation is also required.
Thank you for the opportunity to provide feedback.
