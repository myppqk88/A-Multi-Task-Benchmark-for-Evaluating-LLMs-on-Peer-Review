Dear reviewer 2,
Thank you very much for your review and the helpful feedback!
Regarding your first comment, we have now added a sentence on the frequencies for 5G signals in Germany and Greece in the subchapter “The case of 5G, RF-EMF and health” and one on knowledge of the public about exposure in the subchapter “Previous research on effects of precautionary information on risk perception and trust”. Previous research demonstrates that the public in general knows little details about RF-EMF exposure (e.g., frequencies).
Regarding your second question: As the scope of our study was specifically to investigate the effects of precautionary information with a focus on personal relevance, we didn’t ask for knowledge about media content, institutions, or awareness campaigns in the survey. Therefore, it isn’t possible to correlate the results with this kind of knowledge, even though this could be an interesting topic for future research.
Regarding your third question, there is no literature (we know of) that compares exposure measurements in Germany and Greece. According to experts, this would be very difficult to do, because exposure varies a lot and depends on many factors. As the public usually knows little about field intensity values and possible differences, this is very unlikely to have influenced our results. However, we have added literature showing that in Greece (where risk perception was higher) the actual exposure is usually much lower than the limits recommended for health protection.
Best regards,
Marie Eggeling-Böcker
