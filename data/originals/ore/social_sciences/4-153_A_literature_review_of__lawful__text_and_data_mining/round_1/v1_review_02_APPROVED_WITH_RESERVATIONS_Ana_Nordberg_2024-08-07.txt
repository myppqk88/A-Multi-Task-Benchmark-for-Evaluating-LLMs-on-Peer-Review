============================================================

The paper provides a highly valuable overview of the literature on the topic of  TDM.  Exploring the literature in a meaningful manner and highlighting scholarly work conducted  on this topic.
The following comments and suggestions are submitted for the author’s consideration:
Methodology
The sources included in the review are mostly relevant and the analysis is sound however some aspects of the methodology section would benefit from further development:
The sources selected consist mostly of well-known authors and reputable publication venues. However, the characteristics of journals or other venues of publications either were not considered or are not described. Given the proliferation of low-quality, biased or otherwise unreliable publications, it should be addressed whether or not the study included a qualitative approach to  the selection of sources, and if relevant how.
Table 1 categorizes the literature by topics (most of them of a legal nature), however, some of the sources appear to present other disciplinary perspectives.  It is unclear whether the field of research of the literature analyzed was considered and how such influenced the qualitative analysis of the materials.
A large part of the literature analyzed is legal literature where the jurisdictional aspect is relevant, the methodology section does not clearly state the jurisdictional focus of the study and whether comparative law or other methodologies were used in the qualitative analysis. Instead, the section ‘jurisdictional variance’ provides some information in this regard which could be more detailed.
Arguably, there is a strong possibility that a considerable body of legal literature has been published in the official languages of each jurisdiction, in particular in those countries that have enacted national legislation on TDM that predates Directive (EU) 2019/790 CDSM. However, the literature reviewed only included publications in the English language. If language was a selection factor it should be described and treated as an important methodological limitation or has a subject scope delimitation.
Readability
The text is well written with occasional less clear sentences. Some sentences and paragraphs are excessively long and complex.
The use of the plural ‘they’ to avoid gender pronouns can be confusing. Gender neutrality is important and can be better achieved with the person's name or neutral expressions, such as ‘the author’  or equivalent.
A few footnotes require revision and the entries in the appendix are inconsistently cited, with many being incomplete.
These points can easily be improved with the usual final text review, which is recommended.
Substantive content
1.Section ‘Regulatory frameworks which govern TDM'
Would benefit from a clear structure and a definition of what is understood by “lawful TDM”. The term lawful entails a presumption of compliance with a set of legal rules, thus it should be accompanied by clear specification of the said set of rules (including eventual conditions and exceptions).
2. Section ‘TDM and Intellectual Property Rights’
The section does not address all IPRs, perhaps it would benefit from being more accurately named or at least the type of IPRs considered could be enunciated in the section's introductory sentences.
Some statements in the text are excessively vague or not sufficiently explored/supported. For example, the statement below introduces several points of comparative law that are left ‘hanging in the air’,  and are not explored further in the section.  I would suggest either erasing ( ‘kill the darling’) or further elaborating.
‘Jackson Bertón, who examined the applicable copyright frameworks of the five largest economies of South America in a pre-CDSM era, claimed that even then, the EU was falling behind other more competitive jurisdictions, but ultimately found that South American frameworks were not prepared for digital research techniques.’
3. Section ‘ TDM and Data Protection’
Here the reader faces difficulties with the jurisdictional aspect. Although the information is implied, it should be made more clear what is the  jurisdictional origin and coverage of the legal instruments mentioned (e.g. GDPR). It is not always clear what jurisdiction was considered in the papers analysed.
Some of the papers analysed in this section were written in the early 2000’s. The temporal aspect could be better scrutinized  as to whether their findings remain unchanged by subsequent technological and legal developments.
4. Section ‘TDM and  Fundamental Rights’
What is understood by fundamental rights and what legal framework(s) are considered is not defined. The expressions ‘fundamental rights’, ‘human rights’ and ‘fundamental human rights’ are sometimes used interchangeably making those sentences confusing.
5.Section ‘Generalist approach’
The qualification of the papers analysed under this section as ‘generalist’ is not very convincing. Perhaps the author could better justify their classification, or otherwise include this literature in previous sections.
