Article: A literature review of “lawful” text and data mining.
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.18013.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Thomas Margoni
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2024-07-30
Report ID: 42477
============================================================

This is a very relevant piece of research reviewing the literature in the field of TDM. As such it is important and, within the limits of the initial stage of the research project, original and well developed. A few observations can be found below that may be taken into considerations by the author in the next stages.
"Lawful tdm": not clear what it is, lacks definition.
Literature review: not clear in which disciplinary domains it was conducted. Are you assessing mainly legal sources, or also technical literature (computer science; digital humanities, etc)? There seems to be a need to define better the boundaries of the review and which concepts are assumed.
“Some scholars make a distinction between text mining and data mining.
18
Whilst this distinction may be vital on the technical side of TDM, altering the analysis techniques used, it arguably does not necessarily play a pivotal role in the regulatory frameworks which may apply. Data mining is sometimes seen as a subset of text mining since text mining is the computational process of analysing unstructured messy data, whilst data mining is the computational process of analysing structured data.
19
“
General observation: It is not always clear where you are reviewing current literature and where you are taking a position in the debate. This should be made clearer. As a lit. rev. I would assume the former, but I have doubts here and there.
E.g.,: One could of course argue that text is more copyright focused, while data is less copyright and, if applicable, more sgdr/neighbouring rights with different regulatory impact. Is this something that you are addressing?
Lit rev. may need to be temporally structured: e.g., sources of 2022-2024 are discussed together with sources of 2014 (e.g., around fn 20 – 25 but also in other parts), but the underlying technology and regulatory framework have evolved considerably. Are there category commensurate?
“Bearing in mind the definition of TDM presented above” why not using the CDSM definition?
“One of these solutions, Manteghi claims could be found under Article 35 of the proposed Data Act which “
provides a safe harbour for users needing to access, use or share only databases made of data generated by the use of a product or related service (IoT data)”.
83
“
Unclear. Perhaps clarify that Art. 43 (ex 35) Data Act, only refers to SGDR not copyright.
“On the other hand, Kretschmer
et al.
, who examined machine learning, natural language processing and computer vision for computer moderation as case studies where TDM is vital, found that legal uncertainties like those created by fair use exceptions, can lead to practices exploiting these uncertainties for commercial gain, particularly by large tech corporations.“
Same as above.
General observation: It seems that certain statements are somehow decontextualised from the original source and used in a way that changes the meaning. In the specific example above, it seems that Kretschmer is against fair use for tdm. If I recall it correctly, they are in favour, however, they suggest that legal uncertainty (which can be found in various legal mechanisms including fair use) has, among its consequences, that of favouring larger players who have the financial resources to litigate/resist in court.
It may be necessary to revise that all the excerpts are correctly contextualised in order to ensure that the cited literature is assessed properly.
The part on fundamental right seems a bit rushed in comparison to copyright and DP and to conflate EU/US?
The “generalist approach” category and section is not clear. How is it different from the sections analysed above, particularly copyright? It also seems to rediscuss some of the same sources already analysed.
Conclusions: again it seems you are expressing an opinion about TDM, but can you do that on the basis of a lit. rev.? Perhaps, at this stage you should only focus on what emerges from lit. rev. (which you do in part at the end of the section).
