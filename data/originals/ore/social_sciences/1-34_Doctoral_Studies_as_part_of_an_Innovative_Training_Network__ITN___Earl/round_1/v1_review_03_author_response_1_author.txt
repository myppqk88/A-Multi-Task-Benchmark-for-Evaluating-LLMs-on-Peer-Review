Regarding MSCA-ITNs, there were (still are, I think), three types – the classic, the EJD and the ID. I think that all your data are from the classic type? Thanks a lot for this comment, and we clarified by providing a short sentence in the introduction, stating that while three different MSCA ITNs exist, we focused on the standard kind: “At the time of writing, there exist three different subcategories of ITNs, a standard doctoral training network, as well as European Joint Doctorates and Industrial Doctorates. This work will be focusing on the standard category.”
I understand why it was performed like this but in terms of the purpose of the article and the study, I do not see value in separating the data of the two tinnitus-related ITNs (28 ESRs) and the other ITNS (58 ESRs). As far as I can see the results are very similar and presenting them separately duplicates a lot of text since analysis and explanation are similar. The messages are communicated much more clearly when they are there once with the full dataset – the disaggregated data are on Zenodo for whoever wants them and it is still possible to highlight any specific differences in the text.
This manuscript was initially developed as an endeavor to evaluate the experience of PhD students in the two tinnitus-related MSCA-ITNs, ESIT and TIN-ACT, by the ESRs participating in these two consortia. The evaluation of ESR experiences from various MSCA-ITNs from various disciplines was conducted to assess the agreement between the perspectives of ESR from the tinnitus-related ITNs. This also increased the generalizability of our results, and we could say that irrespective of the research discipline there was a general consensus in much of the ESR experiences.

Thank you very much for pointing this out. Indeed, for ESRs, additionally to support from the consortium, at any point of time in the project (and indeed, also during/after the Midterm Meeting), it was possible to reach out to the Project Officer, an Ombudsperson, or as well National Contact Points, or dedicated departments at their respective host institutions. We refined the following sentence: “Figure 1A shows the impact of ITN-related mobility on the respondents’ academic performance, physical, psychological and overall well-being, with most reporting little to moderate impact. Host institutions and supervisors, ITN management team and EU project team (including Project Officer, Ombudsperson and National Contact Points) supported ESRs with accommodation, language courses and work permits (Figure 1B).”
For instances where ages and similar are used to categorise ESRs, I think that median is more informative than mean.
We think that the mean is appropriate to represent our findings. Interested readers can find the median values in the supplemental material.
The data for the non-ITN PhDs are not shown graphically and it is quite heavy going to read all the numbers as text and then look to see how that compares to the ITN ESRs. Notwithstanding the lower numbers, if they data are worth including, they should be in the same format for direct comparison.
The aim of the current project was to evaluate the PhD-experience of ITN ESRs. Due to the small number of individual PhDs (N=9) associated with one of the ITNs, we consciously refrained from highlighting the results in figures.
The discussion covers the impact of secondments on career choice. I was not completely convinced that this was inconclusive – it seems around 40% in total said that it had an influence. But more importantly, I think that it should be recognised that most of these ESRs still had not completed their PhD when surveyed so it is also possible that there had not been time for the secondment to have had impact? Secondments and their analysis are complicated and I suspect that a deeper analysis is needed to get the nuances. For example, does “affected” the career choice mean that it caused an ESR to reconsider and change their choice? That is not really the aim – it is more to open ESRs to considering other sectors – thus "affecting" could also mean “considered and now understood the other sector but did not change my direction”? There could also be a difference depending on where the secondment was (e.g. maybe those already in industry had made up their mind).
