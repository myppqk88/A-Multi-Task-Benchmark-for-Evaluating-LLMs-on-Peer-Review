Article: Systematic review: locating qualitative academic publications for reviewing tenants’ and landlords’ renting experiences and interaction in the Majority World
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.18234.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Edward Yiu
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2025-01-06
Report ID: 48823
============================================================

This article presents the development of a Critical Interpretative Synthesis (CIS) as a methodological framework for retrieving relevant literature from various databases, exemplified by qualitative studies on private rental housing in the Majority World. It makes a contribution by advancing a Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable (FAIR) approach to literature search. It does not contain any statistical analysis, and thus the question on statistical analysis in the peer review form is irrelevant. It is noted that Version 1 of the manuscript has been reviewed by three scholars, and the author has addressed most of their feedback. This review report pertains to the revised version 2 of the manuscript. I found the author's responses to the reviewers’ comments satisfactory, and the manuscript is well-written.
I offer the following suggestions for further improvement:
Explanation of Less Common Keywords
: Providing remarks or legends to clarify the rationale behind the selection of less common keywords (e.g.,
hmo
,
rooming
,
hous
) in the SCOPUS and Web of Science Boolean strings would enhance transparency and help readers better understand these decisions.
Comparison Table of Search Results
: Including a table that compares search results across SCOPUS, Web of Science, and manual searches would be beneficial. This table could detail:
The total number of references retrieved from each source.
Duplicated references identified across databases.
Misfit references, along with reasons for their exclusion.
Such a table would effectively visualize the impacts and contributions of different databases and search strategies.
These additions would improve the manuscript’s clarity and utility for other researchers replicating or adapting the approach.
