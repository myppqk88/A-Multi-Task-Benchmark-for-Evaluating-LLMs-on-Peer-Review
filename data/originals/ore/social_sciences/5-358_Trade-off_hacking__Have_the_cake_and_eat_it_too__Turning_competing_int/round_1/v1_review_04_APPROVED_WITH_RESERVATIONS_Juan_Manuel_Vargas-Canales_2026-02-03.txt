Trade-off hacking: Have the cake and eat it too? Turning competing interests into win-wins in data-driven food technology
General comments:
The work addresses a real and highly topical problem. It is highly relevant to policy design. However, there are some areas that need improvement.
Abstract
It is not specified how the pilot projects were explicitly analyzed.
Introduction
This section contains statements heavily biased towards justifying the project. It would be preferable for it to be more neutral.
Background
Trade-off hacking: this concept should be expanded upon as much as possible. It would be beneficial to explore the issues further.
Regarding the trade-offs, it would be helpful to explain why there were only three.
Trade-off hacking: vignettes from the DRG4FOOD pilots
This section would be strengthened if it were more critical. Furthermore, it would be advisable to include failures, unresolved tensions, and aspects not yet considered.
Synthesis: Two patterns of trade-off hacking
In this section, it would be advisable to delve deeper into the limitations of each of the patterns. It should be more critical.
Discussion
This section seems too closely aligned with the project objectives. A more critical analysis is needed; otherwise, there is a risk of analytical bias. What would happen without funding? What would happen if an attempt were made to replicate the study? What aspects are most important to consider? What happens outside the contexts of the analysis?
Conclusions
This section is very encouraging. It shows a positive bias. It needs to be more critical.
