Summary of the Manuscript:
this article introduces the Professional Identity and Knowledge (PIK) model, designed to link the historical gap between teacher knowledge (the "profile") and teacher identity in mathematics education. By synthesizing established frameworks; specifically the Mathematics Teacher’s Specialized Knowledge and Wenger’s Communities of Practice. Author argue that professional development is not merely an accumulation of cognitive competencies but a dynamic, situated process of "becoming." The model identifies key psychological and cognitive bridges, such as beliefs and professional noticing, which facilitate the interdependence between what a teacher knows and who a teacher is.
Critical Evaluation and Recommendations:
the manuscript provides a strong  theoretical contribution; however, certain areas require further clarification to ensure scientific rigor:
1. Methodological Transparency:
While the authors frame the study as an integrative synthesis, the transition from the literature review (43 sources) to the specific geometric structure of the PIK model should be more explicitly detailed. Providing a brief description of the thematic coding process would strengthen the validity of the proposed framework.
2. Operationalizing Interdependence:
To move beyond abstraction, the authors should provide concrete examples of how specific knowledge domains (e.g., Knowledge of Topics) influence identity narratives in practice. This would clarify the "bidirectionality" that the model claims to represent.
3. Practical Application:
The manuscript would benefit from discussing how the PIK model can be operationalized as a diagnostic tool within teacher education programs.
Conclusion:
the PIK model is a timely and significant advancement for the field. If the authors address these minor methodological and justificatory points, the article will serve as a foundational resource for understanding the holistic development of mathematics educators. The language is sophisticated and appropriate for an international academic audience.
