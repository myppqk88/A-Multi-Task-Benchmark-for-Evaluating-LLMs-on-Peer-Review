============================================================

I really enjoyed reading this paper. It addresses a relevant though understudied topic of migration studies,
trafficking in itinere,
that deserves further investigation. Irregular migration has often been explored to disentangle the personal and structural causes of migration and the misfortunes of people on the move during their irregular entry into Europe. But the active role and function of smugglers as facilitators of irregular migration across the Mediterranean Sea is often neglected, put in the shadow by other political debates and security concerns. This critical empirically-based analysis zooms instead on the counter effects of securing EU borders, i.e. human insecurity, human immobility and the suspension of human rights protection with the contribution of EU institutions and EUMS.
Some securitization studies draw the attention on the crucial role of various actors that in transit countries are putting international protection in danger (e.g.
Longo F, et al., 2023 [Ref 1]), yet systematic studies are still missing. This paper conducts empirical research that contributes to fill this gap and provides empirically grounded policy recommendations. Comprehensive research is conducted contesting oversimplifications such as the criminalization of NGOs (Cusumano E, et al., 2021 [Ref 2]) that find easily place in the Italian political debates.
In the paper the responsibility of the mismanagement of Mediterranean migration governance is clearly shared among all parties involved, providing ground for the irresponsible stances of the EU politics of externalizing European borders. Criticism addresses the poor human rights standards of EU partners and EU contradictions deriving from its being an organized hypocrisy. Reference to the key literature might be made more explicit, e.g. Brunsson, 1989; Krasner, 1999; Hansen S, et al., 2015 [Ref 3]; Lavenex S, 2018 [Ref 4]; Cusumano, 2019; Cusumano E, et al., 2022 [Ref 5]. Serious concerns are raised re the violation of the international sea regime requesting to aid to people in distress.
The use of data to strengthen the author’s argument is convincing. The paper profits of deep interviews with different actors, providing subjective and institutional information and facts. Policy recommendations provided in the paper are the result of a complex collection of information that renders the distinction between economic and political migrants
The merit of the paper is the nice combination of different research methods and sources. The weakest dimension, as mentioned earlier, is the engagement with the existing literature. Also externalization literature has attracted a lot scholars attention, but few references are provided (e.g. Del Sarto, 2010; Bialasiewicz L, 2012 [Ref 6]; Zaiotti, 2016; Lavenex 2018, Spijkerboer T, 2018 [Ref 7]; Moreno-Lax and Lemberg-Pedersen, 2019; FitzGerald D, 2020 [Ref 8]; Müller P, et al., 2021 [Ref 9]). This reviewer would suggest to expand references, something that can be easily done to strengthen the paper’s quality, anchoring it to the current theoretical debates.
