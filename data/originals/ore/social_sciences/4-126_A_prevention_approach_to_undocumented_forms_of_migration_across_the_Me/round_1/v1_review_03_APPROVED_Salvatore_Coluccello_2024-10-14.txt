============================================================

The recent electoral successes of the far-right Freedom party (FPÖ) in Austria and  Alternative für Deutschland (AfD)  in Germany both backing mass deportation is a stark warning and the spectre of a rising hard right in Europe.  Moreover, an even more Eurocentric, xenophobic and inward-looking Europe is at risk of replacing the hopeful vision that once inspired so many people around the world.
It is therefore like a bit of fresh air and a renewed hope the learn/discover of this interesting Ithaca project funded by the EU’s Horizon’s 2020 research and innovation program.
This paper represents a good effort to investigate the ongoing challenges of irregular migration in the Mediterranean. The authors aim to provide a robust empirical source that could improve the current strategies for the prevention of mixed migration flows and the trafficking of human beings in the Mediterranean. The article also offers an excellent insight into their journey from their countries to Europe/Italy.
The article is significant given that empirical studies on irregular migration to Europe are very topical but also scarce. The article would certainly be of interest to academia in a plethora of different subjects from international migration to sociology, from human rights studies to ethnography.
Using a variety of qualitative methods, the authors demonstrate a number of interesting findings. To such a complicated issue the writers draw some noteworthy conclusions.
The paper also confirms that human smugglers are part of the irregular migration chain. The migrants are sold at every stage of the journey to the local human smugglers and handed over to the next transnational network after they cross the border.
The article also reveals the human smugglers’/traffickers complete disregard for the irregular migrants, their narratives uncovered violence from the law enforcement agencies, the brutalities of the journey, and the continuous fear of ill health or death.
The paper is well written and organised and there is a definite need for a study on the issue of the irregular migration to Europe.
There is, may be an additional comment that the authors might want to consider for the final version of the paper.
The impact of the economic crisis on irregular migrants (and asylum seekers and refugees) has been profound. The informal sector no longer provides sufficient employment for all irregular migrants in the population. Beyond economic survival, the rise in xenophobia has led to a rise in societal risk, including violent attacks against migrants and exclusion. There has also been an apparently lasting change to the fabric of Italian /European societies, notably the xenophobic trajectory of political discourse including the rise of the extreme right and the occasional illiberal enforcement of the rule of law by the security forces.
Overall, it is a very interesting, significant contribution to the field of research.
