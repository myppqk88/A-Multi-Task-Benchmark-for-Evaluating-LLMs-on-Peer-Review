Article: The internal democracy of the crisis parties in Western Europe: a quantitative analysis of the role of digitalization, ideology and populism.
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.21115.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Sorina Soare
Status: approved
Date: 2025-10-14
Report ID: 61265
============================================================

The paper addresses an important and under-explored question: how intra-party democracy functions within new and electorally successful parties in Western Europe. It builds on well-established frameworks  and introduces an innovative generational approach to analyze crisis parties. The combination of the PPDB and POPPA datasets allows for a valuable large-N comparative perspective. The paper has strong empirical potential and can make a meaningful contribution to the literature on party organization and digital-era democracy.
That said, a few aspects could be refined to enhance clarity, coherence, and analytical precision.
The paper rightly notes that existing studies on intra-party democracy in new digital parties are predominantly qualitative and case-based. However, the advantages of adopting a quantitative approach need to be articulated more clearly in the introduction. You could strengthen this point by explicitly explaining how the use of large-N data allows for a systematic testing of the patterns identified in previous qualitative research and what new insights it brings beyond description. The focus on Western parties should also somehow included as an advantage for testing in homogeneous contexts.
The introduction should define IPD more explicitly, as it is a contested and multidimensional concept. It would be helpful to briefly outline what dimensions of IPD are relevant to your study, participation, deliberation, inclusiveness, and decision-making locus,  and why these were prioritized.
This clarification would also distinguish between democracy within parties and democracy by parties, avoiding conceptual stretching.
The theoretical section opens with  an explicit references to left parties. Beginning with a more general reflection on ideological and organizational preferences (rather than directly on left-wing parties) might be beneficial. I also suggest refining phrasing such as “In fact, some new Western European parties…” to “Several new Western European parties especially those on the left have explicitly sought to expand participation and decentralize power.” They look too rough. I also suggest explicitly noting that IPD is not limited to deliberation but also includes decision rules, power distribution, and internal accountability. The end of the literature review should summarize which dimensions of IPD will be tested in the quantitative analysis (e.g., inclusiveness, leader selection, member consultation). This would create a logical bridge between the literature and the empirical design.
The section on theoretical limitations is valuable but could be structured more clearly. It would help to: explicitly connect the identified gaps (small-N bias, focus on leftist cases, lack of longitudinal perspective) with the advantages of the generational approach; show in a more explicit way how this perspective helps to fill those gaps e.g., by capturing how contextual crises shape party formation and internal organization; clarify how “crisis parties” are operationalized and why this temporal framing is analytically meaningful (also caveats). This would turn a methodological note into a stronger theoretical justification.
The hypotheses section would benefit from tighter linkage to the theoretical expectations discussed earlier. At present, the rationale for each hypothesis is described, but the causal logic could be made clearer.
The analytical and discussion sections are strong and well-structured. Still, the paper could benefit from a more explicit dialogue with the initial research gaps identified in the literature review; a more in depth reflection on why crisis parties may not exhibit plebiscitary patterns even when digitalized.
Beyond these elements, this is a promising and well-crafted paper with clear potential for publication. It combines strong empirical grounding with conceptual ambition. Once the argumentation is streamlined and the theoretical framing slightly reinforced, in my opinion it will stand as a valuable contribution to the comparative study of party organization and intra-party democracy.
