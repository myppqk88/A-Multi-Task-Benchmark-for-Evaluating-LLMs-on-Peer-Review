Author Response to Review 3 (v1)
Author: author
Date: 
============================================================

We would like to thank the reviewer for their positive feedback on our work and are very grateful for their constructive comments and suggestions. They most certainly helped to improve the quality of our data note.
Financing sustainable energy projects is a critical issue for expediting the energy transition. This work presents worthy data that could be used in several fields of research and also beyond academia. This data note is presented in a clear and transparent manner. The authors follow fairly all necessary technical and ethical rules for conducting interviews. However, the data note needs some minor revisions to my point of view:
Page 3, Defining the scope, the second and the third paragraphs are a bit unclear and contradictory. In the second paragraph, it is mentioned that “only countries that had conducted at least one renewable energy auction were considered”. However, in the third paragraph, it is mentioned that “At the moment of selecting the focus countries, the following Member States had already conducted auctions…..From these countries, the research team selected the focus countries considering a geographical balance.” However, some countries in these two categories are the same (e.g., Denmark and Germany).
Author Response: We included a more substantiate explanation of the criteria used for selecting the focus countries in the manuscript and adapted the structure of this part to make it clearer.
Please clarify if the interviewees only expressed their organisational views or if they additionally could express their personal views as well in answering the questions.
Author Response: The interviewees did not represent their companies but were rather interviewed as "experts". Some of those even clearly stated that the answers were their personal opinions and not necessarily the ones of their companies. Therefore, we included the following footnote in the manuscript: "In addition, the survey inputs are the personal expert assessments of the interviewees and do not necessarily represent the official views of their organisations."
It is worthwhile to elaborate a bit more on the category of “other” in order to clarify who were categorised under this title. This is not clear.
Author Response: We included a more complete definition for the category "other", as follows: "Under this category, we included among others 1) (independent) consultants and experts, 2) RES associations and chambers, and 3) RES plant operators."
It is worthwhile to add a figure to demonstrate how many of the interviews represented private sectors.
Author Response: We included the following sentence in the manuscript: "Furthermore, almost all of the survey inputs (except 8 out of 206 data points) came from stakeholders from the private sector."
For data validation, the authors used workshops. However, it is not clear who participated in these “internal” workshops. By “internal, "I suspect that only the researchers involved in this project participated. If this is the case, there is a need for clarification on how these workshops, without the participation of other stakeholders or the interviewees, contribute to the data validation
Author Response: One the one hand, we performed an external validation by sending our (aggregated) findings to all the interviewees for feedback. In addition, we performed an internal quality check with all the involved interviewers. We added two subheadings ("External validation" and "Internal quality check") in the manuscript to make it clearer.
