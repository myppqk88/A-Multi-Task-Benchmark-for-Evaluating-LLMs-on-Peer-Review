============================================================

Overall, the previous comments have been taken well into account. One point which has not been addressed adequately is a check of the English language. The development of the test and the validation procedure are now in general clearly described, and mostly well founded. We do have several comments. Most of them are minor editorial suggestions. Yet, some are more fundamental.
Abstract:
Change ‘training sessions’ in ‘training programmes’ (a training is usually more than 1 session).
The following sentence is unclear: “In it, testees choose a practice in line with research integrity to address an issue, justify their choice, and describe how confident they are with their answers.” We suggest changing this sentence, by clarifying that the testees are invited to make a choice between 4 alternative actions, and choose the option that in their view is in line with the principles of RI, as laid down in the ECoC.
Elsewhere also change ‘practice’ in ‘action’.
Introduction:
What does the following sentence mean: “RI and RCR tend to overlap and blur into each other in practice”? Please clarify.
“RI describes therefore the framework, such as the ECoC (2017)”; RI does not describe a framework; the ECoC is a framework for describing principles of RI and good research practices.
“we use the term RCR training to refer to training in the field of RI and RCR”; this is a clear choice, yet, it has not been done everywhere (RI/RCR training is also used).
Status quo and gaps
“RCR training aims to enable participants to become a part of the research community”; the aim of RCR training is not community building but develop knowledge and skills of participants regarding RCR.
The statement “a responsible (future) researcher can argue in favour of practices in line with RI” does cover only one aspect (an important one, and as such rightly targeted by P2I training); more generally, RCR trainings aim to develop awareness, knowledge and skills needed for RCR.
You say: “one practice can simultaneously be both in line and not in line with RI. For example, the practice to publish many articles can on the one hand, be driven by the purpose to get promoted and therefore have enough money to feed one’s family; and on the other hand, could be driven by the purpose to timely inform others about one’s findings. The former argument could lead to a salami publication tactic, whereas the latter does not run the same risk. How somebody justify their practices is crucial.” This raises questions. First, a practice is not right or wrong depending on the motivation alone. A practice can still be an example of salami slicing, and thus not be justified, if the motivation is to inform others. Second, and more fundamentally: if there are two conflicting values at stake (such as career versus informing others), there is often a dilemma involved, meaning that whatever one chooses, harm is done. So there often is no simple right choice. The P2I instrument has a limitation in that it presupposes there is always one right answer, and does not take into account the experiences of researchers regarding dilemmas in their research practice. This is not a problem for the instrument, as long as it is acknowledged that not all aspects of RCR (and its problems) are addressed.
“Participants show how responsible they are by choosing one option on each item representing the ECoC’s topics of RI.” Participants do not show how responsible they are (as responsibility is an aspect of (research) action, not of making a choice), but how knowledgeable they are about principles and good practices outlined in the ECoC.
Methods
“This means that we focussed and limited the P2I questionnaire to what practices testees choose and how they argue for this practice in line with RI.” In this sentence the topic of confidence is missing.
Figure 1 mentions ‘scientific and non-scientific patterns’, this needs correction.
Under Methods, also results are presented. It would be clearer to indicate where results begin by adding a paragraph title: Results. This could be after the Ethics statement. (NB: the subtitle “Results” is positioned much later, but this might better be changed)
Step 1
“To identify research integrity topics in RCR training we followed Roth (2002)”; it is unclear how the (long) quote of Roth helped identifying topics; this quote seems not relevant in step 1.
The references to Jordan, Steneck and Habermas are relevant in clarifying the importance of justifications; yet, they are only mentioned, and the argument for justification is not well elaborated.
The ECoC does not display a discourse in the sense of Habermas, it does provide a frame of reference for identifying good research practices.
Step 2
Information about the content of the focus group discussions is missing. It is therefore unclear how pregiven scenarios could lead to attractive practices in line and not in line with RI. Were the scenarios adapted based on the focus groups results? How were practices not in line developed; were participants asked to provide examples of practices both in line and not in line?
“In a second step, we analysed common justifications”; this is within step 2, so it would be better not to say it in this way, but for instance use “Next, we analysed”
It seems that the answers ‘not in line with RI’ were not thematized in the focus groups, but were the result of analysis by the researchers; this can be formulate more clearly (see comments above); also: how did the researchers come to such evaluations of the discussions in the focus groups? Was the evaluation afterwards mentioned during informed consent of participants? (It is not common to judge answers in focus groups from an external perspective)
The same question can be raised regarding the questionnaires: were respondents informed about the judgment of their answers?
Table 2 presents one justification in line with the ECoC, the others are said not to be in line. Yet, the justification focusing on duty might also be in line with the ECoC. It is also not immediately clear that ‘equal to all’ is against the ECoC.
“we use the term scientific common sense (SCS) when the statement is in line with the RI rules and principles from the ECoC”; it is not clear why this is called ‘common sense’; actually the ECoC aims to do more than codifying common sense.
“We take into account that statements other than our predefined SCS statements could also be correct and expected in specific settings other than our RI-setting.”; how is this taken into account?
Step 3
The title of step 3 does not cover the testing of the instrument, which is part of this step.
“we only selected courses in which participants had not already completed the test.”; the test was not completed by anyone before; maybe you mean: who had not participated in the focus groups or filled in the questionnaire?
“Item cd9 received the same number of answers in line with RI in the first tier and the second tier.”; is this relevant?
“Only in two items 72.73% of the participants choose their answer in line with RI in the second tier, while in the other four items only 54.55% of the participants choose their answer in line with RI in the second tier. For this reason we readjusted the item scenario as well as tier one.” It is unclear why a (low?) number of right answers would require a readjustment of the scenario (and the choice options); also: what exactly did the change entail?
“After revising the pilot test”; do you mean: after revision of the scenario based on the pilot test?
“Nevertheless, this test run exemplified that the complete questionnaire design, as presented in the P2I Questionnaire S, Vers. 1.0 ( Zollitsch
et al.
, 2020b) works.”; what does ‘work’ mean in this context? Feasibility?
After the pre/post test, changes were made; again the question: why does a (low?) number of right answers require a readjustment of the scenario (and the choice options); also: what exactly did the change entail?
“A third expert comment asked to give more than one response option. ”; what is meant here? One right option?
“As there were no indications that other RI and RE experts would come to a fundamentally different conclusion”; what could these indications be? How do you know this?
Results
See earlier comment about the position of the heading ‘Results’
Figure 4 is unreadable, even if we try to enlarge it.
“the P2I questionnaire also contains different independent variables”; the term independent variables is not adequate, they are data about the respondents (which could be used as independent variables in an analysis)
Discussion
“enable participants to become a part of the research community.”; see comment above
“It is therefore suitable for RCR training that focuses on dialogical approaches”; it is unclear why this is mentioned here; also, it will depend on the learning goals of such a training. If these for example focus on virtues, the instrument will not be very helpful.
Limitations
See our comment above: the P2I instrument has a limitation in that it presupposes there is always one right answer, and does not take into account the experiences of researchers regarding dilemmas in their research practice. This is not a problem for the instrument, as long as it is acknowledged that not all aspects of RCR (and its problems) are addressed.
Another limitation is the presupposition that experts know more about RI than (trained) students. We addressed this in our previous review, and the authors agree in their response. We propose mentioning this under limitations.
The need for further validation can be addressed more clearly under limitations. The potential design mentioned in the response to our previous review is interesting, but not fully convincing. How can results from groups discussions be compared to later test results? And would the respondents not be influenced by the previous group discussion? Maybe the other way around (first doing the test individually, afterwards a group discussion) is a better way.
