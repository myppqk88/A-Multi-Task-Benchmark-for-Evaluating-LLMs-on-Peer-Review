Article: Evolving Performance Management Systems in Public Sector Networks: A Longitudinal Case Study of The World Largest Public Network Supporting Smes
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.21281.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Alberto Sardi
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2025-11-20
Report ID: 63284
============================================================

The paper is of considerable interest as it addresses several fundamental aspects of performance measurement. It investigates performance management systems within public sector networks through a longitudinal single-case analysis of the Enterprise Europe Network (EEN), a pan-European network supporting SMEs. The academic literature still shows some gaps on this topic, mainly due to the inherent complexity of conducting research in such large and multifaceted networks.
The research design is well-structured and carefully organized, as is the literature review. However, the theoretical framework and reference models could be further developed to strengthen the discussion and enhance the potential contributions of the study.
Given the complexity of the case under investigation, the findings sometimes appear mainly descriptive or based on the authors’ insights and experience. While this adds value in terms of contextual understanding, it may partially limit the scientific robustness of the work, particularly regarding its generalizability and replicability.
To further improve the study, it would be beneficial to refine some methodological aspects, especially those related to data collection and data analysis. The sources of primary data and the procedures adopted for data gathering could be presented in a more detailed and structured way (e.g., specifying the use of documents, memos, field notes, and observations made during meetings). The data analysis process would also benefit from a more comprehensive explanation to increase methodological transparency and support replicability.
Two final considerations may contribute to further improvement.
First, regarding the topic of the study, in my view the analysis focuses primarily on the performance measurement  – that is, on what to measure – rather than on the performance management, which deals with how to use measurement results (Bititci, 2015; Smith & Bititci, 2017).
Second, the approach adopted appears to be similar to the bottom-up approach proposed by Garengo and Biazzo, particularly in the first two stages described in “Unveiling strategy in SMEs through balanced scorecard implementation: A circular methodology.” Referring to this work could help the authors better rationalize and interpret their findings.
Overall, I congratulate the authors for this interesting and valuable work.
