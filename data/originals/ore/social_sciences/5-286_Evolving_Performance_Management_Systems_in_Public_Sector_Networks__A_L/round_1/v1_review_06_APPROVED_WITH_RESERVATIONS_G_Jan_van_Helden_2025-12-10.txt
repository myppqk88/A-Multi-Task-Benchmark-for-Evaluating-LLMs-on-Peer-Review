I enjoyed reading your paper. It discusses an interesting theme in an eloquent way. Moreover, your paper is well-structured, and your arguments are clearly presented.
Below are some reflections that might inspire you to further improve your paper.
1. The research question is very descriptive, as it is formulated as follows; How do performance management systems in public sector networks evolve?
Because of your literature review, I would have expected a more specific research question that is engaged with the literature and gives guidance to your data analysis.
Some clues might be helpful.
First, a network comprises different actors with diverging tasks and responsibilities. In terms of the PMS it is appropriate to investigate how performance indicators are associated with these diversity of actors and the network as a whole.
Second, the dynamics of a PMS may be contingent upon changes in the governance of the network over time,  and partly interrelated with changes in the environment of the network, such as economic and technological developments in small and medium-size businesses.
Third, the PMS is potentially impacted by power dynamics, in the sense of actors becoming less or more powerful over time.
My suggestion is to think about a framework in which these factors are positioned. This framework can  also be helpful in articulating the contributions to existing knowledge your study makes.
Please mind that these are just suggestions. It is up to you to adopt them or replace them for better ones.
See also point 5 below.
2. The European Enterprise network (EEN) is the case study in this paper. Without further clarification I have doubts about the question whether this is a really interesting network with different actors at various layers. It seems to be an organization with 60 members in various countries, so it is manly an association. What are here the actors: the individual members, the coordinating institutions of EEN, or the representatives of the EU commission. This is all unclear. I am familiar with network PMSs for transportation (train companies, bus companies, governing governments and funding governments), and with the asylum facilitating networks (admission, selection, housing, etc.). Please clarify this.
3. Your data analysis as introduced in section 4 is sophisticated.  This is a strong point of your study. One issue came to my mind when reading about the types of data sources. What I missed was interviews with key actors for unravelling motives behind decisions made about the PMS  or are the studied notes of the network sufficiently informative in this respect? Please clarify.
4. Your findings section is interesting with a clear division between the various phases in the development of the PMS. It was not always clear to me why a PMS was revised from one stage to the other. Maybe you can give a bit more explanation.
5. The paper culminates in the conceptualisation depicted in figure 3. This is an impressive conceptualization with a layered structure, including contextual triggers, a PMS life cycle, and inner-layer building blocks. Although I find this figure impressive, it is not extensively clarified in the paper how the study of underlying documents leads to these outcomes. It looks a bit as a consultancy product: nice to know but not always clear how it can be justified by relevant data sources. The paper claims to use an abduction approach of its data analysis, which is meant to find likely explanations for empirical phenomena, but the data analysis largely remains a black box.
