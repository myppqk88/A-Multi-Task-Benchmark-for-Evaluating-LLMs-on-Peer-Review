We thank Reviewer 3 for their positive evaluation of the manuscript and for the constructive reflections offered, which helped us clarify several elements of the study. Below we address each point in turn.
1. Research question We acknowledge the reviewer’s observation regarding the descriptive nature of the research question. In this study, the formulation was intentional, as the abductive, exploratory design seeks to reconstruct underlying mechanisms of PMS evolution in complex public networks rather than test predefined hypotheses. Following the reviewer’s suggestion, we strengthened the link between the research question, the literature review and the analytical framework, clarifying how the three-layer structure (contextual triggers, lifecycle phases, building blocks) provides theoretical and empirical specificity to the question without altering its exploratory form. Given the abductive purpose, we consider the current formulation appropriate.
2. Nature of the Enterprise Europe Network (EEN) We thank the reviewer for raising this important point. To avoid misunderstanding, we clarified in Section 3 that the EEN is not a small association but a large public network of nearly 600 regional partner organisations across Europe. It is structured through multi-level governance, including the European Commission/EISMEA, national and regional consortia, and cross-cutting governance bodies such as the Representative Body and thematic groups. These actors hold differentiated roles within the network. A short clarification has been added to the manuscripts and responsibilities to make this explicit.
3. Data sources and absence of interviews We appreciate the reviewer’s point regarding motives behind PMS decisions. The primary material used in this study includes research-generated memos from governance arenas such as KPI Working Groups, Steering Committees, Technical Boards and thematic groups settings in which PMS definitions, interpretations and redesign decisions are explicitly debated. These sources therefore provide rich access to actors’ underlying motives and sensemaking processes. While interviews could have added complementary perspectives, we believe the empirical corpus is sufficiently robust for the study’s objectives. A brief clarification of this rationale has been added to Section 3.
4. Explanation of PMS transitions between phases We thank the reviewer for this observation. The revised manuscript already distinguishes between the empirical description of PMS phases (Section 4.1) and the possible explanation of the factors that account for transitions between these phases (Section 4.2). To make this relationship more visible, we added a short bridging sentence indicating that the generic framework captures precisely the triggers and mechanisms that account for phase shifts.
5. Conceptualization in Figure 3 and link to data We appreciate the reviewer’s thoughtful comment. In response, we expanded Table 2 to present the empirical foundations and coding progression from open codes to axial and selective categories. This addition clarifies how the conceptualisation in Figure 3 emerges from the underlying data and strengthens transparency in the abductive reasoning process. Together with the expanded explanation in Section 4.2, we believe the link between the documentary evidence, coding process and the resulting framework is now fully traceable.
