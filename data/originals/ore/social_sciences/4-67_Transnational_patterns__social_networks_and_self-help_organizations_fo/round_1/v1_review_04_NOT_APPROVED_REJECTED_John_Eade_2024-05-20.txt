Article: Transnational patterns, social networks and self-help organizations for migrant women
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.17168.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: John Eade
Status: not approved
Date: 2024-05-20
Report ID: 40103
============================================================

This is a promising paper that tackles the highly relevant issue of developing self-help structures through transnational networks among women who have been forced to migrate. The paper tackles this issue by drawing on interviews with two women in different countries linked to three research questions.
In my opinion, the paper's impact can be improved by the following:
Consider the limitations of the social capital model as exposed in the wider literature. Putnam's approach has been particularly questioned and his perspective differs considerably from Bourdieu's so bringing them together with Granovetter raises important theoretical issues that should be addressed for the sake of clarity. The exploration by Bourdieu of the role played by cultural capital could be useful here, especially as migration requires people to learn the dominant 'rules of the game' in a new country.
The balance between the two case studies needs improving. There is more detail about the Bulgarian context and this weakens the comparison between Italy and Bulgaria and the conclusions drawn from this comparison. More information about the role played by self-help organizations in Italy would be very helpful since the person interviewed appears to have relied more on her personal transnational networks, especially her partner in Sweden with whom she shares a child.
It would be helpful to know more about the relationship between these two women and other migrants (male as well as female) and 'the whole native population' since this plays a crucial role in learning the local 'rules of the game'. There are more indications about how this relationship works in the Bulgarian case study so again more balance is needed here.
In sum, then, a very interesting and promising paper that can be strengthened along the lines outlined above.
