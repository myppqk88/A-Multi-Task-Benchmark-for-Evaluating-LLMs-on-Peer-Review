Summary of the article:
This paper presents an
Open Science Protocol
specifically tailored for the
Integrated Assessment Modeling (IAM)
community, based on a survey of IAM teams. The protocol is designed to improve the
transparency, accessibility, and reusability
of IAM models and outputs by aligning with the
FAIR
(Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) and
TRUST
(Transparency, Responsibility, User focus, Sustainability, Technology) principles. The study highlights current challenges in implementing open science within IAMs—such as inconsistent documentation, limited vetting of outputs, and barriers to sharing data—and offers practical guidance to overcome these. By fostering better communication and usability of models, especially for policymakers, the protocol aims to enhance the
credibility and impact
of IAM-based climate assessments.
Details related to the questions above (where I answered partly):
The paper acknowledges the importance of the FAIR (Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability) and TRUST (Transparency, Responsibility, User focus, Sustainability, and Technology) principles but does not fully explore their practical implementation within Integrated Assessment Models (IAMs). A deeper engagement with recent literature and case studies specific to IAMs and these principles would strengthen both the theoretical foundation and practical relevance.
While the survey design is appropriate for gathering qualitative insights, the methodology section lacks sufficient detail. Key elements such as the full survey instrument, sample size, and selection process are missing, which limits transparency and hampers replicability. Providing this information, along with a clearer description of how responses were analyzed, would enhance the robustness of the conclusions.
The protocol presented is promising, with clearly outlined steps. However, it would benefit from further elaboration, particularly regarding practical challenges encountered, strategies for adaptation across IAM contexts, and concrete examples of successful implementation. Additional data—such as more detailed documentation, case studies, or checklists—would improve the transparency and reusability of the work.
Although the interpretation of survey results is reasonable, it relies heavily on qualitative insights. Incorporating statistical analysis or quantitative data could provide stronger support for the findings. The conclusions align with the presented results but could be made more compelling through the inclusion of real-world applications that demonstrate the protocol’s effectiveness and impact.
Other information related to data/code availability and figure:
- The GitHub repository: https://github.com/klau506/open_science_iams does not detail how to use the R code and with which data.
- The data is deposited in Zenodo as xlsx file which is not an open format. csv file (text file) would be better for reuse.
- Figure 1 is unreadable on my laptop (too small).
- The survey results file (available at
https://zenodo.org/records/13970492
) contains 9 entries, suggesting that fewer than 10 individuals responded to the survey.
Recommendation:
There are a few areas where additional details and refinement could help to strengthen the scientific rigor of your paper. Below are some specific suggestions:
1.
Strengthen the Theoretical Framework
It would be beneficial to gain a deeper understanding of how the FAIR and TRUST  principles can be practically applied to IAMs (in detail because in the paper, the 15 FAIR principles are not addressed with sufficient details). This will help better align the theoretical foundation of your protocol with current best practices in Open Science and ensure its relevance to the IAM community.
2.
Enhance Methodological Transparency
The methodology section could be improved by providing more
detailed information about the survey design
. For instance, explain why the
sample size
and the
selection process
of survey participants were sufficient for this work.
3.
Improve the GitHub Repository
The
GitHub repository
could be enhanced by providing clear
documentation
on how to use the R code and apply it to the provided data. Additionally, I suggest switching the data format from
XLSX
to an
open format like CSV
to facilitate easier reuse and interoperability with other tools.
4.
Enhance Visual Accessibility
Figure 1 i
s not easily readable on standard devices. I recommend increasing the
resolution
and
size
of the figures to improve accessibility, ensuring that they are clearly visible to a broader audience.
5.
Increase Survey Sample Size
Given that the survey currently includes fewer than 10 responses, I suggest reaching out to more IAM teams to
increase the sample size
. A larger sample would improve the
representativeness
of the findings. It would also be helpful to acknowledge the potential limitations posed by the small sample size and how these might affect the interpretation of your results.
