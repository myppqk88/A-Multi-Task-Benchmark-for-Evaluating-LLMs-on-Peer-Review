Thank you for your detailed comments and clarifications in response to my first review. And sincere apologies for the very long delay in completing this second round review.
The purpose and focus are now very clear and the literature review section on Social Innovation captures an impressive amount of information in a relatively succinct section. The variety of new collaborative workspaces comes through clearly and it is logical to assume that the nature of social innovation in each case cold be quite different – both in terms of social organisation and in terms of social forms of participation.
I am not a big fan of assemblage theory, and personally I might prefer to look at functionality and networks as two separate aspects of the CWS.  However, the way that you are able to use this as a strong thread throughout the analysis of the findings suggests that you have hit upon an approach that does add to our understanding of CWS, their different features and their different and overlaying networks of connections. I particularly like the way that you bring the discussion back to social innovation and use the assemblages approach to help explain how social innovation can (and in these types of situations perhaps should?) be a collective activity.
Having previously commented that the contribution of the paper was a little “underwhelming”, I find the deeper theorization in the paper demonstrates how the research is contributing to larger debates in rural development, collaborative workspaces and social innovation literature. The data remains a strength and the use of quotations an explanation of the cases are strong features of the paper that reassure me of the robustness of the claims made. It will be interesting to see how the CWS landscape, particularly, in rural areas, continues to evolve but this paper certainly provides a solid basis for future researchers to refer to when analysing the next phase of evolution in collaborative working.
