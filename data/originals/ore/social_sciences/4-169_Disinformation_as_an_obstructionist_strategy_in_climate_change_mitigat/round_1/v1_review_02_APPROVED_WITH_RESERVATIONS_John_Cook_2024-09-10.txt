Article: Disinformation as an obstructionist strategy in climate change mitigation: a review of the scientific literature for a systemic understanding of the phenomenon
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.18180.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: John Cook
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2024-09-10
Report ID: 43280
============================================================

This study conducted a systematic literature review of papers about climate obstruction, finding an emphasis on immunizing and fact-checking but few studies on the organizational structures supporting disinformation.
Overall, I find this is an interesting study that makes a useful contribution. The point about the need for more systemic approaches to disinformation is well made. However, I find the hypotheses and their partial confirmation unsatisfying. It seems hand-wavy and ill-defined on whether the literature review answers the hypotheses in a robust way. The hypotheses use phrases like “provides a deeper”, “better able to”. Deeper or better compared to what? How does the literature review determine whether certain studies are better than others? In the discussion section, justification of partial confirmation needs to be explained better. Or if it’s not possible to rigorously confirm or disconfirm hypotheses of this nature, perhaps they are better off framed as research questions.
I have some minor comments on the text as well:
Introduction:
You state that "Systematic disinformation... has a profound impact on decision-making, public trust and the effectiveness of policies and the actions they promote". What's the citation for this?
Just a comment: the paper leans heavily on Almiron & Moreno (2022). Excellent to offer more perspective from non-WEIRD countries but pity this paper isn’t available in English as I’d like to read it!
On page 5: what is the “E)” (after the five areas of corrupting the scientific field)?
Page 6: I find the paragraph about Al Gore confusing. After reading it several times, I think you’re talking about a denialist review of Al Gore’s book but it’s not clear – I suggest clarifying the text, perhaps by mentioning who wrote the review or more context around the review early in the paragraph.
Page 6: The paragraph about the Chris Horner book – I’d rewrite the sentence that uses the word “which” twice to be more readable.
Page 6: Is the neoconservative line “inescapably” linked or “inextricably” linked to Trump?
Page 6: The transition to talking about the Intelligence Community report is abrupt, doesn’t really seem to fit in the flow of the text. Seems that paragraph could be deleted with no disruption to the manuscript.
Page 9: The tense shifts from past tense to present tense halfway through describing the methodology. Is this intentional?
Page 11: Use of “Ambito”, do you want this to be English?
Page 12: The tense now shifts to future tense. Recommend consistency of past tense throughout the methodology section.
Page 13: Should the subtitle “Identification of studies via other methods” be formatted like other subheadings?
Page 16: I question the characterization of the phrase “Research by McBeth,
Lybecker and Sargent (2022), in line with the results of Benegal and Scrugg (2018), questions the predictive power of political ideology for climate attitudes.” The citations don’t question the predictive power of ideology but show that certain contexts can reduce the influence of ideology in causing rejection of climate messages. Political ideology is still predictive but can be mitigated. This characterization is brought up again on page 17/18 when summarising Benegal and Scruggs (2018).
Page 18: I’d reword “The authors saw positive effects in the use of vaccination messages” to “inoculation messages” as inoculation messages are not called vaccination messages in the psychological literature. The term vaccination or vaccine is used again several times in the same and subsequent paragraphs and should be changed to inoculation.
