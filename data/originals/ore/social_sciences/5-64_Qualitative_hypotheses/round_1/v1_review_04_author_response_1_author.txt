Thank you for engaging with the manuscript in such depth and providing constructive suggestions, which helped to improve the work. I have revised the structure and content of the essay significantly; however, in order to maintain the encyclopedic format and keep the text informative for a wide range of readers, some of the solutions are light and would deserve further discussion in a separate context. Below, I respond to the main comments one by one and elaborate on some of those topics.
“
That raises the question about what would be so special about QH’s that they need to be set apart from AH’s?
”  I agree the original framing was not clear. I have now further specified the identity of QHs in relation to related concepts, and highlighted its role as a tool rather than a concept in the research process.
“
When the author moves into discussion use (why usage?), function and limitations, the essay is missing examples.
”  I agree that an example was needed to clarify usage. I have added a new section with an example.
“
Is it only the strategic adoption of quantitative language to continue to participate in its bureaucratic innovations (such as preregistration and RR’s)?
”  As a Wittgenstein at heart, I do believe it matters how we play the language game but in this case, it feels participation in innovations is even more relevant. I have further highlighted the QH as a “tool” and specifically one that has come to exist along with technical innnovation in preregs and RRs.
“
how much of a QH is tacit expectation and how much of it is the result of a decent literature review and/or pilot study (which of course needs to be referenced. I am quite curious about the boundary between the two, according to the author. It resonates a little bit with thinking on concepts in the 17
th
century, where the origin and nature of concepts was a central issue in the debate
”  This is a large and highly intersting question, which would deserve separate discussion outside the essay. Although I’ve now addressed the distinction between literature reviews and QHs, there remains an open question
how
e.g. positionality affects our expectations/QHs (i.e. to what degree it’s possible to distinguish inherent expectations from others, and how it matters).
“
Interestingly, where the author draws multiple parallels to positionality, in the limitation section the collective dimension of QHs is left as a problem or concern whereas in terms of positionality, this matter has received a lot of attention under the banner of collaborative positionality
”  I've revised this part. Some other time, I’d like to challenge the idea of ‘collaborative positionality’ a bit. Similarly to the hypotheses at hand, I see collaborative positionality to be ‘positionality’ only in a metaphorical sense; it lacks the very essenence of positionality (direct engagement with reseracher subjectivity) and thus serves a function that’s similar on the surface but different in essence.
“
I would argue that it can be a metaphor, which assists us in tapping into the value of some of the protocolized thinking from qualitative epistemologies to the benefit of qualitative studies. Metaphors may be quite distinct from concepts.
”  This was very insightful and helped me to better understand QHs. I hadn’t read primary sources of Blumenberg earlier; I now read one book (cited) and non-conceptuality seems to tap on similar points to Lakoff & Johnson and to some limited extent Goodman. For this short essay (not least because detailed theoretical scrutiny would take a lot of time), I decided to keep the reference to metaphor brief; nonetheless, the wider impact of this insight can be read throughout the text.
“It would benefit a lot from a more consistent comparison with existing work on positionality, focusing specifically on what it does that positionality cannot.”  This has been added.
Thank you again for all valuable feedback, which significanitly helped to improve the work.
