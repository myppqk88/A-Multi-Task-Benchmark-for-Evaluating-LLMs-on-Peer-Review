To the Editorial Office
Subject
: Final Assessment of Revised Manuscript “Divergence and Aggregation of ESG Ratings: A Survey”
Dear Editors,
I am writing to convey my final judgment regarding the revised (second version) manuscript entitled
“Divergence and Aggregation of ESG Ratings: A Survey”
by Arianna Agosto and Alessandra Tanda. After carefully reviewing this amended version, I am pleased to recommend the acceptance of the paper for indexing in its current form.
Indeed, the authors have undertaken a commendable effort to address my (previously written, composing the first paper's review) comments and substantially improve the manuscript. In particular, the authors have significantly strengthened the introduction by offering a clearer articulation of the motivation behind the study and the central contributions it makes to the literature on ESG score divergence and aggregation. Moreover, a new, dedicated section outlines the methodology adopted to select and screen the literature under review. The latter enhances the transparency and reproducibility of the research process. I would also like to underline that Section 5 has been expanded to offer a more thorough discussion of the metrics used to evaluate divergence among ESG ratings. The authors provide a clear exposition of correlation-based methods, such as Pearson and Spearman coefficients, and have incorporated recent empirical studies applying unsupervised learning methods like Principal Component Analysis (PCA). Analogously, They have directly acknowledged the persistent absence of a standardised framework for evaluating ESG aggregation methods, hence addressing a critical limitation in the current literature. It is also worth to mention that the concluding section has been extended to propose several promising avenues for future investigation. These include the use of clustering techniques for ESG profile classification and the exploration of interpretability tools, such as Shapley values, to evaluate aggregation weights. Importantly, the authors critically assess the trade-offs between methodological complexity and practical utility.
Summing up, the paper now offers a timely and complete contribution to a field of increasing practical and theoretical importance. The Authors systematically review a broad and up-to-date body of literature and succeed in synthesizing a wide range of methodological approaches for ESG score aggregation, moreover, by highlighting methodological discrepancies across ESG providers and proposing pathways for methodological convergence, the paper has also increased its relevance for both academic researchers and market practitioners.
For all what above, I recommend the indexing of the submitted, amended version of the article without further revision.
Sincerely,
