Dear Krithi Pushpanathan, Thank you very much for your review; it was very helpful. Below please find our responses to your points, where we have done our best to address each one.
“The authors assert that "limitations such as ‘bullshitting’ and hallucinations in early AI developments (Zhao et al., 2023) are no longer a primary concern." However, recent research (e.g., Steyvers et al., 2025) continues to demonstrate that hallucinations remain a significant issue and that users tend to overestimate the accuracy of LLM-generated responses. Given these findings, I would caution against the claim that hallucinations are no longer a primary concern. (see: Steyvers, M., Tejeda, H., Kumar, A. et al. What large language models know and what people think they know. Nat Mach Intell
7
, 221–231 (2025).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00976-7
)”
Author Response:
Thank you for this comment. By stating that hallucinations are no longer a primary concern, we meant to suggest that efforts have been made to manage this issue since the introduction of early chatbots, using various measures. To highlight this point better, we changed the wording and added an additional reference. At the same time, we agree with you that accuracy issues still persist, even with these measures in place. We followed your recommendation and revised the statement to provide more explanation. Please kindly see if our changes in the text reflect your point.
“Section C in Methods, Criteria for Analysis, provides a clear overview of the evaluation framework and methodology. However, it does not specify who conducted the qualitative assessment of Copilot’s responses. Were these evaluations performed by domain experts, researchers, or practitioners in P/CVE? Additionally, how many evaluators were involved? If multiple evaluators participated, were any measures taken to ensure consistency, such as inter-rater reliability assessments? Providing this information would enhance the transparency and credibility of the evaluation process”.
Author Response:
Thank you very much for this useful comment. Following your recommendation, in section A, we clarified that we are domain experts (evaluation in P/CVE) - please see whether this brings more clarity. In addition, it was specified already in the earlier version that: “This study drew upon the authors’ extensive interactions with European and non-European practitioners and policymakers in the field of P/CVE, providing insight into their perspectives on evidence-based evaluation and the needs of the field.” Our methodology was based on our earlier publications on evaluation in P/CVE, which also demonstrates our expertise. From the beginning of this paper, we have been careful not to call our study an “evaluation” but rather referred to it as “mapping” and “review.” This is important because our aim was not to conduct a multidimensional evaluation, which would require a more holistic approach with experts from different domains. Instead, our goal was to provide a snapshot of existing research possibilities and share our initial observations for our taregt audience: practitioners, policymaker and researchers in P/CVE.
“The Findings section presents key results, but given that LLMs evolve continuously through manual updates and user interactions, it is unlikely that identical responses will be obtained in future replications of this study. To facilitate longitudinal research and ensure transparency, I strongly recommend that the authors provide the full set of raw data (i.e., Copilot’s responses alongside the corresponding evaluations) as a supplementary table. This would allow future researchers to assess improvements in LLM performance over time and strengthen the reproducibility of the study’s findings”.
Author Response:
Thank you for this comment. Indeed, providing the full datasets aligns with sustainable research practices. As per the Open Research Europe requirements, we initially have made our full datasets (prompts + the chatbots’ responses) available in open access to support the principle of reproducibility. These datasets were mentioned in the paper and listed in the references section.
Since we referred to these published datasets, we found it redundant to duplicate them inside our paper. Please see our datasets here:
