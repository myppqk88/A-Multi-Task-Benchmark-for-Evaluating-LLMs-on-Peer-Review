By\nemploying network analysis, centrality measures, and community\ndetection, we demonstrated how to identify pivotal organizations, monitor their influence over time, and delineate the\nevolution of collaborations fostered by Horizon 2020 and\nHorizon Europe projects.\nSpecifically, the analysis shows that Horizon grants in hydrogen sector projects have prompted the emergence of communities that have significantly evolved over the years. Initially\ncharacterized by small, rapidly changing groups, these groups\ngradually shifted towards the formation of larger, more stable\npartnerships, particularly in the domain of hydrogen valleys.\nThe methodology presented in this paper, particularly the use\nof centrality measures, community detection algorithms and\ntemporal network analysis, offers opportunities to improve the\nvisualizations in the Horizon Dashboard43. In addition, policy makers at European level\ncan use the methodology to gain insights into the evolution\nof communities, to assess their openness to newcomers and\nthe stability of collaboration between industrial and academic\npartners. From an alternative perspective, project coordinators within industrial or research organisations may use the\nmethodology presented in this paper to identify potential\npartners who may be influential in facilitating future\ncollaborations.\nFuture work will focus on several key areas.
