This is not an intuitive\n\n275\n\nmeasure of temporal stability, and obviously is not comparable to the proportion of Type-D and\n\n276\n\nthe proportion of Non-D who retain their status. Other studies report the proportion of temporal\n\n277\n\nstability of Type-D classification overall, combining Type-D membership and Non-D\n\n278\n\nmembership (e.g. Pelle et al., 2008). Since Type-D individuals are about 1 in 4 or 1 in 5, the a-\n\n279\n\npriori probability of remaining Type-D is much lower than the a-priori probability of remaining\n\n280\n\nNon-D, thus overall temporal stability is not an informative measure for the stability of the at-\n\n281\n\nrisk-group Type-D.\n\n\n\n282\n\nIn one of the studies it was possible to retrieve the relevant numbers for the temporal\n\n283\n\nstability of Type-D classification as defined here. Dannemann et al. (2010) found that 14 of the\n\n284\n\n33 pre-surgery Type-D CVD patients, or 42.4%, remained Type-D post- surgery, and 77 of the\n\n285\n\n93 Non-D CVD patients, or 82.8% remained Non-D post- surgery.\n\n286\n\nCompared to this clinical sample, the current study showed high temporal stability, as it\n\n287\n\nextends over a considerably longer time frame, and as more than half of the individuals\n\n288\n\noriginally identified as Type-D remained Type-D. However, the temporal stability of Type-D\n\n289\n\nclassification was significantly lower than that achieved for using the sum of the subscale scores\n\n290\n\nor their product as a cutoff point. In particular, the product of the subscale scores had several\n\n291\n\nadvantages as a criterion: it showed a highly significant overlap with the classical Type-D\n\n292\n\nclassification, as well as having higher temporal stability.\n\n293\n\nCoyne and de Voogd (2012) claimed that Type-D classification has not been borne out by\n\n294\n\nempirical research even though the component traits are of obvious importance, and suggest\n\n295\n\nusing alternative approaches to scoring the Type-Dness, based on the quasi-continuous subscale\n\n296\n\nscores, rather than using the Denollet (2005) rule of 10 or more on both sub-scales.\n\n297\n\nSubsequently, (Denollet, Pedersen, Vrints & Conraad, 2013), conducted a five-year longitudinal\n\n298\n\nstudy of a series of over 500 CVD patients showed that Type-D did better at predicting major\n\n299\n\ncardiac events than did its component traits, making the OR for any major cardiac event 1.74,\n\n300\n\nand for cardiac death 2.35. This impressive study showed very strong \"positive\" results for Type-\n\n301\n\nD's predictive validity, but did not address the question of the temporal stability. Was it the\n\n302\n\nType-D status at the beginning of the study that had this major effect? Was the effect different\n\n303\n\nfor those who remained stably Type-D throughout the five years of the follow-up? If Type-D\n\n304\n\nstatus is less than 50% temporally stable in CVD patients, as seen in the Dannemann et al.\n\n\n\n305\n\n(2010) study, at what point does being Type-D exert its influence? Should being Type-D be\n\n306\n\nconstrued of as a state of heightened risk, or as a trait of individuals at elevated risk?\n\n307\n\nThe current study contributes to existing research on the temporal stability of Type-D by\n\n308\n\nexamining a community sample and not a CVD patient sample, by using a battery of well\n\n309\n\nvalidated personality measures, and by having a relatively long inter-test interval. It suggests an\n\n310\n\nalternative criterion for defining Type-D, based on the product of the DS14 subscales, which\n\n311\n\nprovides better temporal stability in a non-patient sample. However, the results of the study\n\n312\n\nshould be viewed with the study limitations in mind, i.e. they may not generalize well to CVD\n\n313\n\npatients, especially those before and after a traumatic cardiac event or a major intervention.\n\n314\n\nThese life-threatening experiences may have a dynamic of their own. Further research is needed\n\n315\n\nto clarify this point. However, since Type-D is designed to predict risk, its temporal stability is of\n\n316\n\nprime importance, and the question of when it should be measured, or which of subsequent\n\n317\n\nmeasurements is crucial to understanding the behavior and medical risk of CVD patients,\n\n318\n\nrequires further research.\n\n319\n320\n\n\n\n321\n322\n\nREFERENCES\nBagby, R.M., Parker, J.D.A., & Taylor, G.J.
