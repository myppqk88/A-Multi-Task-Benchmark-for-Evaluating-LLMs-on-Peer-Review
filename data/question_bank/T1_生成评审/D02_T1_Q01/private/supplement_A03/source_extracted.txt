DATA FILES
RAW SURVEY DATA
The survey data is either available as a ‘.sav’ file, compatible with SPSS and other statistical tools,
or as ‘.csv’ for maximum compatibility. The .csv file format was chosen to make the data as openly
accessible as possible, since both XLSX (Excel) as well as SAV (SPSS) files are not standard open
data formats and require specialised or paid software to open and process. Both versions hold the
same data.
While the SAV version can simply be opened in compatible software, the CSV variant is made up
of two files:
•
•

GRRIP_researcher_survey_data_CSV.csv
from heron referred to as the “survey data”
GRRIP_researcher_survey_structure.csv
from heron referred to as the “survey coding structure”

The survey data file contains all questions and data of the survey, including qualitative data. Each
column has a unique variable name header that is reflected in the survey coding structure. The
properties of each file are as follows:

The accompanying survey coding structure file lists all variables of the survey data file and how
the numerical values are coded (e.g., “1” = “Yes”). The survey coding structure file lists 3 layers
of variable grouping depth:
•

•

Variable Group
o Variables referring to the same question are grouped through a shared variable
group name. For example, multiple choice questions have a unique column
(variable) within the survey data file for each option, since an arbitrary number
and combination may be presented. All columns of a given multiple choice
question share the same variable group.
Variable
o The variable name used in the survey data file to uniquely identify each data
column. Variable names may repeat to group all response options to a single
question. For example, the different options of a radio (single choice) question are

VARIABLE NAMING CONVENTION
All variable groups, variables and variable options follow a common naming convention. Variable
groups are always suffixed with a “_G”. Variable options are always identical to their
corresponding variable name, suffixed with “_OX”, where X is an incrementing number counting
the number of options for each variable. In some cases, variable options may seem redundant
where a variable might only have a single option. However, the naming convention was strictly
followed to avoid inconsistencies across the survey coding structure file.
•

Example:
o Variable Group: education_G
o Variable: education_arts_and_humanities
o Variable Option: education_arts_and_humanities_O1

VARIABLE TYPES
The “Type” column of the survey coding structure file specifies the type of each variable as
follows:
•
•
•
•
•
•
•
•

Text: Simple text string for open ended questions or “other” responses. This is also used
for nationality or country, where the number of possible items is extremely large. These
are instead encoded as text (e.g., using ISO format for countries such as IE for Ireland).
Radio: Single choice responses.
Multiple Choice: Multiple choice responses.
Checkbox: Checkbox style items, reserved for “Prefer not to say” (-97) toggles of openended text and number fields.
Integer: Whole number responses
Likert Scale: Likert scale responses. These are virtually identical to radio, or single choice
responses.
Likert Scale (Reverse): Reverse coded Likert scale responses, where the high value (e.g.,
7 – “Strongly Agree”) would indicate a difference to the desired or “good” response.
Example: “Not every human needs to eat” -> “Strongly Agree”
Semantic Differential: Semantic differential questions are answered on a scale (in this
case 7 points: -3 to 3). At each end of the scale an adjective is shown, and the respondent
selects the point on the scale closest to how they feel/think. Contrary to traditional Likert
Scales, only the outer most values have a label (i.e., -3 and 3). This variable type is therefore
similar to the integer type, as any value from -3 to 3 may be found, where -3 always refers
to the left (usually negative) and 3 to the right (usually positive) end of the scale. Example:
For the scale [Unimportant - Important] -3 would mean “Unimportant” and 3 “Important”.
Responses from -2 to 2 would gradually represent a higher/lower perceived level of
importance.

SURVEY SPECIFICATION
RESEARCHER SURVEY
Informed Consent

Participation in this survey
You have been invited to participate in this survey because you work in a research performing or research
funding organisation in the marine and maritime sector which is taking part in the GRRIP (Grounding RRI
Practices in research performing organisations) project. We are conducting this survey as part of auditing
the policies and practices of the five marine & maritime organisations involved in GRRIP. We want to hear
your perspectives on these policies and practices.

About the GRRIP project
GRRIP is an EU-funded project (grrip.eu) that aims to embed sustainable RRI (responsible research and
innovation) practices in the marine & maritime sector through institutional and cultural change. GRRIP will
embed sustainable RRI practices in these five marine & maritime organisations: The Centre for Marine and
Renewable Energy (MaREI), University College Cork, Ireland Plataforma Oceánica de Canarias (Plocan),
Gran Canaria WavEC Offshore Renewables, Lisbon, Portugal Institut Universitaire Mer Littoral U.Nantes
(IUML), France Swansea University, Wales.

How much time is needed to participate?
This survey takes about 20 minutes to complete. We will ask you a set of demographic questions to
understand your background. We will then ask you to tell us the extent to which you agree with various
behaviours related to RRI, and how far you see these reflected in your marine & maritime sector
organisation.

Who are we?
We are a consortium of researchers working on the GRRIP project (see grrip.eu/about-theproject/partners). If you have any questions regarding the overall project, please email grrip.eu@gmail.com.
This survey is designed and administered by one of GRRIP’s partners, ICoRSA. If you have questions
about this survey, you can contact survey@qualiaanalytics.org or Dr Eric Jensen at e.jensen@icorsa.org.

What happens to the information you provide?
Please be assured that your responses will be kept confidential. The information you provide will be used
for progressing tasks in GRRIP and will be shared with GRRIP partners. We will not publish these survey
data with information which identifies you personally. You have the right to withdraw from this survey at any
point during your completion of responses. Furthermore, you have the right to skip any survey question for
any reason. However, once you have submitted the survey, you can no longer withdraw your responses.
This project has received funding from the European Union’s Horizon 2020 research and innovation
program under grant agreement No 820283.

Agreement to Participate
Please read the following statements: I confirm I am 18 years of age or older. I understand that my
responses to the following survey will be securely and confidentially stored. I understand the information I
provide about myself is confidential. I understand that my participation is voluntary, and I can withdraw at
any time. I agree I have received adequate information about my participation in this research and
understand what will happen to the information I provide.
