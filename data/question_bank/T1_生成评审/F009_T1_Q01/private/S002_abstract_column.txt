Discussion

238

The current study was conducted to assess pharmacy students' views on AI. Student pharmacists

239

were generally hopeful toward AI. The findings indicate a generally positive attitude toward

240

artificial intelligence (AI) among pharmacy students, with strong support for its development and

241

recognition of its relevance in their field. The statement "To what extent do you support the

242

development of artificial intelligence in your field of specialization?" received the highest

243

Weighted Average (WA) of 3.86, a Median of 4.0, and an Interquartile Range (IQR) of 3.0–5.0,

244

highlighting substantial enthusiasm for advancing AI in pharmacy. Similarly, the statement "I

245

believe that AI will have an impact on my job" scored a Median of 4.0, with a narrower IQR of

246

3.0–4.0 and a WA of 3.33, reflecting optimism about the potential influence of AI on professional

247

roles. Additionally, students expressed moderate agreement on the importance of foundational

PeerJ reviewing PDF | (2025:08:124161:0:2:CHECK 8 Sep 2025)

Manuscript to be reviewed
248

AI knowledge, underscoring an acknowledgment of its growing significance in pharmacy

249

education and practice. These results suggest that while students are enthusiastic about AI's

250

integration and its career implications, there may still be room to strengthen their understanding

251

of foundational AI concepts to fully prepare them for its practical applications.

252

However, they expressed caution regarding the management of AI in the field and reported

253

limited awareness of the ethical consideration involved.

254

A similar mixed feeling was expressed by Canadian healthcare students, including pharmacists,

255

where they were optimistic towards utilizing AI in their clinical practice, but they were worried

256

that AI will result replacing healthcare professionals. (Teng et al., 2022). A recent commentary

257

suggested a reassuring statement; “AI will not replace you, a person using AI will” and

258

encouraged academic institutions to offer informational and training workshops to their students

259

and staff. (Cain et al., 2023) equipping pharmacy students and academics with the knowledge

260

and skills required to use AI effectively, will prepare them to be better positioned to survive in an

261

AI-driven world rather reducing the risk lose their roles as pharmacists and consequently losing

262

their jobs.

263

Pharmacy students from 12 different countries including Saudi Arabia, conveyed positive views

264

towards incorporation of AI in daily pharmacy practice. However, they expressed lacking

265

confidence in their AI knowledge and showed enthusiasm for integrating AI education as part of

266

their pharmacy degree program. Ethical and legal concerns were also highlighted by pharmacy

267

students. (Allam et al., 2024)other studies raised the same concerns regarding ethical and legal

268

aspects of using AI in pharmacy curricula and pharmacy practice. Studies suggested establishing

PeerJ reviewing PDF | (2025:08:124161:0:2:CHECK 8 Sep 2025)

Manuscript to be reviewed
269

ethical guidelines and legal framework that control AI-driven recommendations. (Hasan et al.,

270

2024b) (Mortlock & Lucas, 2024) (Chalasani et al., 2023) (Hasan et al., 2024a)

271
272

In the current study, pharmacy students were supportive of integrating AI into pharmacy

273

program curricula. Their top-rated learning objectives involved understanding and interpreting

274

AI generated results, gaining awareness of ethical consideration of using AI in clinical practice,

275

comprehend how basic technology process the work, expressing how technology functions in a

276

way that others can grasp. Other objectives involved; Being able to assess the appropriateness

277

of utilizing AI in certain clinical contexts, acquire the terminology to effectively communicate with

278

engineers and developers. A previous study reported sharing the same top-ranked objectives of

279

AI incorporation into pharmacy degree program. For that reason, designing an AI course should

280

consider meeting the highest-rated objectives. (Allam et al., 2024)

281

Similar findings were expressed in a recent multicounty study that highlighted the importance of

282

incorporation of AI-based courses into pharmacy program and continuing medical education.

283

They emphasized the significance of early exposure to AI to advance knowledge and skills

284

required in real-world clinical scenarios. (Allam et al., 2024)

285

286

To address the identified gaps, pharmacy curricula should integrate focused modules on AI ethics,

287

covering critical areas such as data privacy, algorithmic bias, accountability in AI decision-making,

288
