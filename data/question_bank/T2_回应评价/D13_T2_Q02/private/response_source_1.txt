I would like to see the actual normal stats – Shapiro Wilk for all the measures reported along with the skew and kurtosis.
Thank you for your suggestion. Although the ShapiroWilk test is a commonly used method to assess normality, it can become overly sensitive with large sample sizes, detecting trivial deviations from normality that have little practical impact on parametric analyses (Ghasemi & Zahediasl, 2012), So we prefer not to choose this indicator when dealing with larger samples.

Ghasemi A, Zahediasl S. Normality tests for statistical analysis: a guide for non-statisticians. Int J Endocrinol Metab. 2012 Spring;10(2):486-9. doi: 10.5812/ijem.3505. Epub 2012 Apr 20. PMID: 23843808; PMCID: PMC3693611.

Using a Bonferroni style is not needed if the actual P values are reported.
Thank you very much for your thoughtful comment. We acknowledge that reporting exact p-values can be sufficient in many cases and that a Bonferroni correction may not always be strictly necessary. However, in our manuscript we performed multiple correlation analyses simultaneously, and thus opted for a conservative approach to control the overall Type I error rate at 5%. By using the Bonferroni-adjusted threshold (0.05/6 = 0.008), we help readers interpret the statistical significance of these tests in a consistent and transparent manner. Thank you again for your suggestion and for helping us refine our presentation of the results.
