Editors’ comment:
The survey need to be described very well in order to facilitate repeatability. It is mandatory to indicate if the survey was validated or not and whether it was used before, include appropriate references for this.
This particular survey has not been validated, although it is modelled on the Australian National Health Survey, with physical activity questions based on the IPAQ (SF) (Craig et al. 2003). This is described in lines 70 to 73 of the review PDF. Reference has been made to the IPAQ-SF in the Survey description section, lines 104 to 114 of the review PDF. The survey was piloted among staff and then students prior to administration; this statement has been added to line 114 of the review PDF. This survey has been administered to first year students every year since 2015 and was adapted to include the COVID-19-specific questions. A detailed description of the survey questions is provided in the methods section, and the response tables (Tables 1-4) provide an indication of the questions that were asked, and responses provided. Others wishing to replicate the survey could do so from those tables. Beyond this, the survey was provided to the reviewers as a supplementary file & we will make it available for readers, as a supplementary file. This statement has been added to line 114 of the review PDF. 

Reviewer 1:
The authors aimed to describe some changes (before/during the COVID-19 outbreak) among pharmacy and medical students. I would like to congratulate the authors for their effort. However, I believe that the research does not apply for an original manuscript submission, mainly due to its lack of novelty. The authors present results that are expected and have already been described in previous literature. I do not consider that they add more to the current evidence. I recommend the authors to consider present their results as a letter to the editor.

Thank you for the positive feedback. This study represents, to our knowledge, the only Australian study to survey the same cohort of students pre-and-mid pandemic, with the majority of published studies being cross-sectional (Defeyter et al. 2020, Odriozola-González et al. 2020, Dodd et al. 2021) or comparing data to that of previous student cohorts (Gallo et al. 2020). There are a few exceptions to this, such as the study by Li & colleagues (2020); however, their study did not report other factors beyond psychological distress. We have revisited the published literature to include support from studies that have been published since our paper was originally submitted and have assessed the same cohort at more than one timepoint, such as Savage et al. 2021 & Bertrand et al. 2021. The reporting of pre-and-mid pandemic data, collected at more than one timepoint from the same cohort, is what we believe makes this manuscript novel and worthy of publication. We have stated this more explicitly in the revised version, lines 166 to 168 of the review PDF ‘We believe that this may be one of only a few studies that compares data collected pre-and-mid pandemic in the same cohort of students, and the only one in Australia, where lockdown duration has not been as prolonged as that in the northern hemisphere.’. 

Reviewer 2:
Well done on a timely and relevant article. In addition, the manuscript is clearly written in professional, unambiguous language.

Experimental design
It would be interesting to know how lockdown due to COVID19 has influenced student's marks (results), and if it really has an impact on their university performance.

Yes, we agree that this would be a useful outcome and have suggested that this be a focus of future longitudinal research. Our preliminary investigation of grades during this period indicates that there has been no impact on grades to date. What we have found to be interesting is that informal discussions with many of our current students suggest ongoing fatigue stemming from the events of 2020; we will continue to monitor grades with respect to this concept, with the intention of reporting longer-term outcomes.

More detail is needed on the survey, including a reference. Was it validated?

Please see the response to the comment from the Academic Editor. 


Validity of the findings
It cannot be concluded that there have been no changes in food behaviour or energy intake as it is done in the discussion. Maybe It could be worth noting other international studies (e. g. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12061807) that inform about there have been relevant changes of food intake among young people as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic.

We do not conclude that there have been no changes in food behaviour, but rather that there is no evidence for it in our cohort, which we believe is a reasonable assertion based on the outcomes of the statistical analysis. The study cited (Gallo et al. 2020) does find a change in food behaviours, albeit that the Gallo et al. study makes comparison to students from earlier cohorts, rather than within-person change assessed in our study. We have included findings from Bertrand et al. (2021) who demonstrate the negative impact of COVID-19 on the diets of university students not living with their parents, line 185 in the review PDF: ‘…and a Canadian study has provided evidence that university students not living with their parents had poorer diets during COVID (Bertrand et al., 2021).’

Thank you for the provision of the link to the paper reporting changes in food intake; however, the 10-to-19 years age range in that paper is below the age range of the university students in our cohort.

The open question was answered by 9 students, 10%, very few to get a conclusion.

We agree that this is a small number of responses and have acknowledged this as a limitation in the paper (lines 202 to 204 in the review PDF), in that these perceptions cannot be generalised to the student population. That stated, this does not invalidate the perceptions of the students who did complete the question. The information is provided to describe the experiences of students, rather than to make a conclusive and generalised determination of the impact of COVID-19 on students’ lifestyle.
