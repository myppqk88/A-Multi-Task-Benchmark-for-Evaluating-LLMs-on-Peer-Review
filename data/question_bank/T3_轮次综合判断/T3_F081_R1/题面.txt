There are many photos in a “romantic style” that\nhighlight a tender and close connection between the couple in\na warm light. These depictions aim to depict and highlight an\nemotional quality of the moment. Also many photos are taken\nduring the ceremony, be it traditionally religious, an alternative\nrite, or a civil wedding. The multitude of photos or the\nextensive footage of the ceremony highlights this part of the\nwedding in the couple’s memories. Even in the conversation with\nthe couples the ceremony has often been extensively discussed.\nFinally, it is notable that the couples who only owned digital\nphotos of their wedding apologized for this. They\nshow the kissing couple, the couple in the sunset, in a fancy\ncar, or after the ceremony in front of the registrar’s office, the\nchurch doors, or wherever the ceremony took place. Specifically,\nthe ceremony is densely photographed in standardized\nimages like the exchange of the rings, signing of the wedding\ncontract, or the “victory photo” of the couple after the\nwedding. It shows that the depiction\nitself reinforces a moment’s positive value and provides it’s\nsticking power. The photographic style and quantity of specific moments influences in what way the rite de passage is\nremembered. Additionally in the memory space of reception, as\none couple explicitly mentioned, the wedding media allow the\ncouples to see and experience moments of the wedding day\nthey missed out. The couples often mentioned that they felt a\nlot of tension and the day went by very quickly. In this case, the\nwedding media facilitate the couple to not only remember but\nalso experience the ceremony or the party from an insider\nobserver perspective. In this way, media enlarge the\nwedding experience and in the words of Belting “achieve\npermanence”. They transcend the rite of passage to transform it\ninto a consistent value that in the reception is experienced as\nconfirming and self-assuring. The media themselves reinforce the couple’s wedding promises and their reception allows\nviewers to repeat and revive the emotional quality. The couple created the wedding narrative together and adapted their different experiences to each other in order to create one memory.\nIt is remarkable how many similarities between the couples’\nmemories could be observed independent of religious, cultural,\nage, and educational background as already mentioned. This\nhomogenization becomes part of a collective memory of the\nrite of passage that connects the individuals.       "R2": "Thanks for the opportunity to review this study, which is a very high-quality contribution to what\nremains a curiously understudied practice. Weddings retain such a vaulted status across cultures,\nand their memorialisation in photographic and videographic forms has only become increasingly\ncomplex and professionally curated, creating mementos that have incredible appeal. And yet,\nthere is little research on the hopes and motivations in producing these affecting tokens of\nmemory, which often hold such importance in idealized life narratives. It was a therefore a\npleasure to read this study, and I look forward to seeing the final version.\nEchoing the prior reviewers, my suggestions relate primarily to strengthening the literature review\nand the analytical findings.\nThe author states:\n‘The research question scrutinizes weddings from a contemporary perspective with a focus on the\nmedia that are produced during the wedding. These wedding media include photos and videos\nthat are produced professionally, semi-professionally, or non-professionally. About this specific\ntopic, no scholarly literature has been published so far.’\nI must confess I was surprised to read this and even a little personally hurt. As another reviewer\nhas duly noted, there is a small but significant corpus of material specifically on media – both\nphotography and videography – that is produced in memorializing weddings. None of these\n\n\n\n\nstudies appear to be cited, and the analysis is oddly conspicuous for their absence. Of course,\nsome pragmatic circumscribing is always necessary in a literature review, particularly when\ndealing with an expansive topic (such as the cultural meanings attached to wedding rituals).\nHowever, to not acknowledge prior studies that investigate the precise empirical focus of your\nown study results in a striking void.\nThis study, to my knowledge, is the first to use photo and video-elicitation methods to discuss\nwedding media with the key actors captured in such events. This is an immensely valuable\ncontribution to the field. However, this originality and benefit is actually diluted and obscured by\nnot acknowledging what has come before. All of the studies below analyze wedding\nphotography/videography in some form, and merit inclusion in the literature review:[ Bezner\nL.2002 (Ref 1), Lewis C.1997(Ref 2), Lewis C.1998 (Ref 3), Schwarz O.2010 (Ref 4), Strano M. 2006\n(Ref 5 ), Wade M, et.al.,2019 (Ref 6), Social Beings, Future Belongings 2019 (Ref 7), Walsh M, et.al.,\n2020 (Ref 8), Humble Á, et.al., 2008 (Ref 9)]\nI also suspect that engaging with these studies will help with the theoretical framing and analysis\nof the findings. While the study is highly original and praiseworthy, many of the points raised are\nechoed in previous work. In particular, regarding the paper’s ‘four memory spaces of production’,\nthere is much prior analysis on production (especially how photographers and videographers\napproach their work in creating something that is uniquely affecting but also adheres to expected\nconventions) and representation (e.g. critical analyses of how rigidly traditional tropes are\nreproduced in wedding mementos).\nSimilarly, some of the findings, while very interesting, tend to describe but leave any analysis\nimplicit. Why does a tangible wedding album seem to hold greater value than a digital one\n(p.13)? Why do couples take an increasingly proactive and co-creative role in curating their albums\nand narratives (pp.13-14)? Why does the ceremony occupy such an outsized role in the\nmemoralization of the wedding (p.14). Why, do you think, were husbands particularly active in\nclarifying symbolic or procedural aspects of the ritual, but were otherwise relatively uninvolved,\nleaving their wives to primarily continue to carry the ‘wedding work’ of articulating the central\nnarrative (p.15)? The twenty\nseven conversations with couples from Germany, Italy and\nSwitzerland confirmed that wedding photos and videos are\nhighly standardized representations. The memory of their\nproduction and reception experiences shows surprising similarities between the couples, independent of the couple’s\nage, year and country of wedding, sexual orientation, or\nreligious-cultural background. The conversations revealed\nhow the mediatisation of wedding memories achieve “sticking\npower” through emotions. In the concluding section the\nfollowing most significant results from the spaces of memory\nand their relation to each other are reviewed. The thematic areas\ncover the following topics: (1) The memory of the photo\nsession with the photographer during the wedding day; (2) the\nphotos of the ceremony and their reception; (3) how the\nmedia enlarge the wedding experience; (4) the homogenization of memory (and standardisation of emotions); (5) gender\ndifferences in the memory space of reception.\n(1) In the memory space of production, the importance of\ntaking the pictures was striking and how much time many\ncouples invested or at least took some time alone with the\nphotographer. These photos often have a “romantic style” that\nhighlight a tender and close connection between the couple in\na warm light. These depictions aim to highlight an emotional\nquality of the moment. They show the kissing couple, the\ncouple in the sunset, in a fancy car, in a park with lots of\nnatural surroundings or after the ceremony in front of the\nregistrar’s office, the church doors, or wherever the ceremony\ntook place. The wedding photo session is often experienced\nas a special event with the photographer and becomes an\nemotional and even intimate part of the rite of passage that is\ncaptured in the different photo motifs. Nevertheless, these\nposed couple photos are often less appreciated than the memory\nof their making.\n\n87\n\n62:4 ¶ 75 in VIW_11_IT_290922_V_ts.docx (see video interview VIW_11_\nIT_290922).\n88\n\nDavid Corpuz, “The Wedding Video and the Persistence of the Marriage\nModel, Romantic Love Myth and Heteronormativity in Filipino Same-Sex\nRelationships,” Manila: Far Eastern University, 2019, 5; Kimport, “Remaking\nthe White Wedding?,” 881.\n\n89\n\nThe original reads “prothèse oculaire”. The couples often\nmentioned that they felt a lot of tension during the day and\nthat the day went by very quickly. In this case, the wedding\nmedia enables the couple to not only remember but also\nexperience the ceremony or the party from an insider observer\nperspective. It\nseemed that gay couples are equally invested in the pictures, and\nthe images of the wedding itself and the reception, was something very emotional and of high importance in a different\nway than in the case of heterosexual couples. The spaces significantly interact, not only reinforcing but also extending emotions with additional experiences in the spaces of production and reception. Additionally,\nthe wedding images themselves form and even intensify the\nmeaning of the rite of passage and add an emotional charge\nthat in turn keeps memory alive. The conversations about the\nmemory of the weddings guided by the pictures not only scrutinises how the media become a defining part of cultural\npractices like a rite of passage. But the research also shows\nhow photos and videos may define our memories of cultural\npractices.\nThe focus on married couples from different cultural backgrounds, gender, and sexual orientations is at the same time\na limitation. The two main commonalities between the couples were firstly, the photos or videos of their wedding, and\nsecondly, that all the couples are still married.
