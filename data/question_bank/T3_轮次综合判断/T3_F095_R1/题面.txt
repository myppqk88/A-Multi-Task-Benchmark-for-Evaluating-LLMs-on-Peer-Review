They observed (ibid., p. 1063) that “the\nobligatory point of passage of the production team as translator of journalistic needs into technological developments\nhindered opportunities for innovative ideas to flourish”, as\n“breaking news reporters felt their ideas were neglected,\nand web developers limited themselves to following instructions from the online editor”. According to them (ibid.,\np. 1068), this made online journalists feel “powerless in the\ndecision-making process” while technologists viewed their\ncolleagues’ needs as a lack of skills. (2013) looked at interactions and power\nrelationships between well-established actors (i.e., journalists), new actors (i.e., technological tools, convergence and\nparticipation) and former intermediaries with an increasingly\nimportant role to play (i.e., audiences), in order to find out,\namong others, whether the dominant journalistic culture\nadapts to or contributes to shaping new technological\naffordances. Although\nthis review was by no means exhaustive as other publications on ANT and automated journalism fell outside my search\ncriteria6, it was still representative of a research gap worth of\nour attention. ANT certainly comes with its own limitations\n(see Ryfe, 2022), such as being too oblivious of any overarching social order and loosing track of the bigger picture\n(see Benson, 2017; Couldry, 2020), but it can meanwhile be\nused to give a “bottom-up” account of how automated news\ndirectly contributes to shaping the world, thus yielding agency.\nDistinguishing mediators from intermediaries then appears all\nthe more necessary.\n\nResults\n\nAs described in my methodology, I will conduct here a\ncross-national exploratory study using Hallin and Mancini’s\nmedia system typology (2004) to strategically select news\n\n6: For instance, Wu, Tandoc and Salmon (2019) used ANT to deconstruct\nthe idea of seeing “automated journalism” as text generation only, arguing that media practitioners rather viewed it (ibid., p. 1453) as “anything\nfrom the machine aggregating and funnelling of content, to data scraping and auto-publication of stories”. In consequence, I was not able to see how\nautomated news was being used with my own eyes (although\na virtual walkthrough was conducted with BBC): this resulted\nin me walking a fine line between findings I could directly\ndocument, like an automated news dashboard that is available\nonline, and others that were reported or not directly visible to\nme, like details of a computer script. Even though I did my best\n\nto verify all of these elements, they are still exposed to the\ntype of fallibility that goes with human interpretation. This is\nwhy I speak of an exploratory study, since more triangulation\nwould be required to generalise these findings.\nOne last limitation is about not being able to set in stone\nwhat remains essentially a field in flux, where new technical\nbreakthroughs or ways of implementing automated journalism\ncould be happening as I am writing these lines. For example,\na couple of years ago, most NLG companies appeared to be\nexternal content providers only, in charge of creating automated news products in place of media companies, but then\nstarted offering self-editing tools as well, as in the case of\nAutomated Insights (see Mullin, 2015). This fast-paced evolution of automated news products makes it difficult to analyse\nthem based on development types (i.e., external content\nproviders, in-house, third-party self-editing tools); however, this\ncould be done once this is stabilised enough.\nOther than this, possible research avenues include using\nANT to determine whether, in the ongoing assemblage of an\n“automated journalism actor-network”, news organisations\nor platforms act as spokespersons, especially as it may turn\ninto a macro actor able to restructure media production as a\nwhole. To a certain extent, platforms can be seen as already\ngaining the upper hand as recent text summarisation efforts\nto create quizzes, polls or summaries—which are somehow\nrelated to automated news—appear to be quite tailored to\nfitting social media content.
