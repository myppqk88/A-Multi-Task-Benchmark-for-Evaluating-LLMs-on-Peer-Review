Challenges to engaging people in such a climate\nwere added to by individuals who did not want to know how\nmuch they were being tracked online. Additionally, CSI-COP\nspecific online activities did not entail enjoying nature through\nsports and outdoor play, actions which were disallowed during the pandemic restrictions. CSI-COP resolved force majeure\nchallenges through proactive adjustments to the severe changes\nby creating a free interactive informal learning resource\n(MOOC), translating it into multiple languages for learning in\nown-time; adapting Greenpeace’s ‘Big Plastic Count’ for an\nonline ‘Big Cookie Count’ event and transforming the MOOC\ninto free-to-attend webinars organised by partners in their local\nlanguage.\nCSI-COP demonstrated that it not only investigated compliance of the GDPR in websites and apps, but the project also\nset in place GDPR compliant processes to build trust with its\nengaged public and citizen scientists. The project innovated a\nsearchable Repository of the websites and apps investigated\nby CSI-COP’s citizen scientists and researchers. Two policy\nbriefs were produced to assist the EU with monitoring GDPR\ncompliance. The change\nin behaviour among the citizen scientists included applying\ntheir new understanding of the rights to data protection\naccorded in the EU’s GDPR, and their new practical skills, to\nbetter protect theirs and their young family members’ online\nprivacy.\nThe value and benefit of citizen science, and the contributions\nfrom an informed, scientifically literate public to the production\nof new knowledge (extent of online tracking and compliance\nof the GDPR in the case of CSI-COP), was evident through\nreceiving an industry award ‘Best Innovative Privacy Project’\nin the inaugural PICCASO Privacy awards.
