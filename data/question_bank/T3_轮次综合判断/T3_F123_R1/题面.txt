      "text": "Abstract\nThe individual development plan (IDP) is a career planning tool that\nassists PhD trainees in self-assessing skills, exploring career paths,\ndeveloping short- and long-term career goals, and creating action\nplans to achieve those goals. The National Institutes of Health and\nmany academic institutions have created policies that mandate\ncompletion of the IDP by both graduate students and postdoctoral\nresearchers. Despite these policies, little information exists regarding\nhow well the tool is used and whether it is useful to the career\ndevelopment of PhD trainees. Herein, we present data from a multiinstitutional, online survey on the use and effectiveness of the IDP\namong a group of 183 postdoctoral researchers. The overall IDP\ncompletion rate was 54% and 38% of IDP users reported that the tool\nwas helpful to their career development. Positive relationships with\none’s advisor, confidence regarding completing training, one’s\nconfidence about their post-training career, and a positive experience\nwith institutional career development resources are associated with\nrespondents’ perception that the IDP is useful for their career\ndevelopment. We suggest that there is a need to further understand\nthe nuanced use and effectiveness of the IDP in general, to determine\nhow to execute the use of the tool to maximize trainees’ career\ndevelopment, and to generally enhance the career development\nsupport for PhD trainees.\n\n\n\nIntroduction\nThe Individual Development Plan was first introduced by the\nU.S. Federation of American Societies for Experimental Biology\nin 2002, and in 2014 the National Institutes of Health implemented a policy requiring the reporting of the tool’s use by\ngraduate students and postdoctoral researchers in grant progress\nreports1–3. Also in 2014, 19% of postdoctoral researchers\nwere shown to use the IDP with 71% of those users finding it\nvaluable4. We recently conducted a multi-institutional study on\nthe use and effectiveness of the IDP in 663 PhD students and\nfound that 57% completed an IDP and 51% of users reported it\nbeing useful to their career development5. The effectiveness of\nthe tool was associated with a positive relationship with mentors,\nconfidence regarding career plans, and positive interactions\nwith institutional career development resources5. More research\nis needed to further characterize the IDP among all PhD trainees and to determine if there are ways to maximize the tool’s\nimpact on trainees’ career development5,6.\n\nMethods\nThese data were collected as part of a broader health and\nwellbeing online, survey-based study of graduate students and\npostdoctoral researchers in the spring and early summer of 2016\n(March to June). With regard to the use and effectiveness of the\nIDP, the data collection procedures, survey methodology, data\nanalysis and statistical methods, and study limitations have been\ndescribed previously5. Briefly, the work was approved by the\nUniversity of Kentucky (protocol 15-1080-P2H) and University\nof Texas Health San Antonio (protocol HSC20160025X) institutional review boards. Respondents read a cover page and\nanonymously consented to the study by engaging the online\nsurvey. The survey was distributed via social media and direct\nemail. Eligibility criteria included being a current postdoctoral\nresearcher in the life/biological/medical or physical/applied\nsciences at a U.S. institution. Subjects responded to the IDP\nquestions using the five-point Likert scale strongly agree, agree,\nneutral, disagree and strongly disagree. For data analysis,\nstrongly agree and agree responses were grouped together as an\n\nagree category and neutral, disagree, and strongly disagree were\ngrouped together into a does not agree category. One-way frequencies were calculated (Supplementary File 2) and the Pearson\nchi-square test was used to assess the univariate associations\nbetween the survey variables and the outcome “I Find the IDP\nProcess Helpful to my Career Development” only among the\nrespondents who completed an IDP (Supplementary File 3).\nAll summaries and statistical analysis were performed in SAS 9.4.\n\nResults\nAmong 183 total postdoctoral respondents, 45.4% reported being\nrequired to complete an IDP, 27.5% reported completing the tool\nwith their PI/advisor, and 33.9% completed the IDP, at some\npoint, without discussing it with their PI/advisor (Figure 1 and\nSupplementary File 2). In total, 54.1% of respondents actually\ncompleted the IDP with or without their advisor (Supplementary\nFile 2 and Supplementary File 3). Further, 24.3% of all respondents reported being able to have an honest conversation with\ntheir PI/advisor in the context of the IDP process and 22.4%\nof all respondents found the IDP helpful to their career development (Figure 1 and Supplementary File 2).
