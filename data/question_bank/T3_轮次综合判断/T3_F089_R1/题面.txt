However, only 24%\nof participants reported using apps, social media, or websites\nfor their health. Despite comprising a minority within the surveyed population, the significance of this subset cannot be\nunderstated. And whilst the study is descriptive in nature, it\noffers valuable data on digital health engagement among adolescents in Vanuatu, a population largely absent from existing\nliterature. While reports often suggest a digital divide between\nurban and rural areas, the results from our study challenge this\nnotion. Our current findings demonstrate that of the participants who reported having used digital technology for\ntheir health indicated similar responses across both urban\nand rural areas, suggesting that with access, there may not\nbe a digital divide.\nThe analysis of findings from these adolescents reporting the\nuse of digital technologies for their health holds importance\nas it provides insights into the health information-seeking\nbehaviours of adolescents in Vanuatu. Understanding the preferences identified by this group serves as a pivotal point for\ntargeted strategies to assist them with making appropriate\nhealth decisions. Tailoring educational initiatives, and resources\nto align with the needs and preferences of this population is\nrequired for effective engagement of digital and online health\ntools and positive health outcomes.\n\nInfluencing factors of social media use for healthrelated purposes by adolescents in Vanuatu\nAs children move from primary to secondary levels in school,\ntablets and mobile phones are the preferred device (Rich\net al., 2020). The type of device used plays a crucial role in\nshaping how adolescents in Vanuatu engage with health-related\n\ndigital content. Smartphones are likely the most commonly used\ndevices due to their portability and multifunctionality, allowing adolescents to easily watch videos, interact with online\ncommunities and access social media and other health apps.\nIn this study, the participants used their mobile phones mostly\nfor music-related activities and less likely to search for information or use social media. Computers and tablets, while less\nportable, offer a larger screen that enhances the length and\nviewing experience of content consumption. Completing homework was the second most reported activity to be conducted\non these devices in the current study, following music-related\nactivities. These entertainment and education-oriented activities\nappear to take priority over proactive health-information seeking and may help explain the low levels of engagement with\nusing digital technologies for health-related information and\nengagement in our study. This is similar to findings from recent\nresearch (Black Dog Institute, 2024; Rich et al., 2020). Listening to music (78%) and watching videos (72%) were in the top\nthree activities (following socialising on social media, 85%)\nconducted on their smartphone or tablet by young people\naged 11–18 years in South West England (Rich et al., 2020).\nThe device type can also affect the format of content that adolescents prefer. Short videos and quick updates particularly on\nsocial media platforms are more suited to smartphones, while\nwebsites and documents might be better viewed on tablets or\ncomputers. Whilst previous research found 71.48% of 270\nhealth-related webpages were mobile-friendly, only 15.93% of\nthem were designed in a way to optimise readability (Cheng &\nDunn, 2017). The dissemination of health information through\nmobile phones, however, have been shown to be a healthpromotion strategy that is useful and convenient (Cilliers et al.,\n2017), with young people reported mobile phone use for seeking out information, advice and learning about health and\nillness, mental health and wellbeing, and physical fitness\n(Rich et al., 2020).\nPrevious studies have shown that online search engines\nand websites were the most common tool to use to actively\n\n\nsearch for health information (Freeman et al., 2018; Gazibara\net al., 2024). In this current study, when asked about what\nonline platforms they used to search for health information,\nthe use of search engines was more popular to search for health\ninformation in the urban location compared with the rural\nlocation, where rural participants preferred to search health\ninformation through social media platforms. Social media\nplatforms, including Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube, were\nalso identified as the favourite to use for health-related activities and discussing health topics as identified by adolescents\nfrom both urban and rural areas. This aligns with current literature that adolescents from other regions use social media\nplatforms to access health-related information, including topics\nsuch as healthy lifestyle behaviour (nutrition, physical activity,\nweight management, sleep), mental health, sexual health, substance use, and specific medical conditions (Armstrong et al.,\n2021; Rich et al., 2020). Popular platforms including\nFacebook, Twitter (now known as X), and YouTube act as\naccessible reservoirs of diverse knowledge, and serves as a space\nfor peer interaction and social support, empowering adolescents with important health information (Moreno et al., 2018;\nRich et al., 2020). Fostering a sense of community engagement, social media enables adolescents to share personal health\nexperiences, practices and solutions within a digitally interconnected space. These findings have leveraged the potential\nsocial media has as a platform to delivering health interventions, with positive findings (Alvarez-Jimenez et al., 2020;\nAmon et al., 2022).\nThese findings were revealed by only a quarter of the total\nparticipants, however, this may be influenced by multifaceted\nfactors that are characteristic of the Pacific Islands. Whilst\nthere were no specific differences in use between rural\nand urban locations, the limited accessibility of internet\ninfrastructure, coupled with socioeconomic disparities may\nsignificantly impact the opportunity to use digital technologies\nfor these purposes. In addition, the amalgamation of traditional\ncultural norms with the current digital landscape shapes the\nacceptance and credibility of health information that is\ndisseminated through these online platforms. The interactive\nnature of these platforms encourages open dialogues, enhancing\nhealth literacy and promoting discussions around taboo health\ntopics in a culturally sensitive manner (Chen, 2023).\nUnderstanding these multifaceted and complex connections is\ncrucial for a comprehensive understanding of adolescent use of\nsocial media for health-related purposes within the Vanuatu\ncontext.\n\nCultural sensitivity\nCulture may affect health by shaping and influencing habits\nand behaviours related to precursors of health, development\nof disease, and by controlling their physical environment\n(Fleisher et al., 2013). Interpretations of health and illness may\nvary with different cultural groups and ultimately inform why\nsome cultural groups may choose to adopt or not adopt\npreventative health behaviours. Thus, health education materials\nneed to be culturally appropriate. An important component of\nculture is language, and the impact of language on minority\nsubgroups differs based on social and demographic\n\ncharacteristics. A lack of understanding of written English\nor French is a barrier that can cause misunderstandings that\nlead to inappropriate health decisions (Sauni & Neal, 2012).\nThe rapid dissemination of potentially culturally insensitive\ninformation is of concern. Vanuatu is culturally diverse which\namplifies the susceptibility to misinterpretation of healthrelated content available online, and the lack of digital health\nresources in local languages can limit the reach and\neffectiveness of health initiatives.\n\nRisks\nMisinformation. Whilst social media serves as a means to\naccess health information by adolescents in Vanuatu, it is\nunregulated which presents potential risks of misinformation (Suarez-Lledo & Alvarez-Galvez, 2021; Swire-Thompson\n& Lazer, 2020). Taba et al. (2022) explored the relationship\nbetween adolescents’ self-efficacy and digital health literacy,\nemphasising the critical role these factors play in navigating\nonline health information. Thus, while adolescents increasingly\nturn to social media for health-related information, their\nability to discern credible sources from misinformation is\noften limited by their digital health literacy skills, emphasising\nthe risk of misinformation, particularly in contexts like\nVanuatu, where digital literacy education may be less prevalent. While participants in our study reported the use of TikTok\nas one of their favourites to use for health, Merga’s (2024) systematic review cautions that the platform’s entertainment-driven\ncontent and inconsistent credibility may pose risks to adolescent digital health literacy, particularly when health advice is\ndelivered by influencers rather than qualified professionals.\nAdolescents with higher self-efficacy and better digital health\nliteracy are more adept at evaluating the trustworthiness and\nrelevance of online health content, thereby reducing their\nsusceptibility to misinformation. Therefore, there is a need\nfor targeted educational interventions and policies to enhance\ndigital health literacy among adolescents, ensuring they can\nsafely and effectively use social media for health information.\nExposure. There is evidence of passive health information\nexchange via social media (Raeside et al., 2022). Findings from\nthis current study show that adolescents in Vanuatu engage\nin several other activities on their devices. Thus, even if\nan adolescent is not actively searching for health-related\ninformation, their presence on online websites and social\nmedia leaves them open to a variety of unsolicited unhealthy\npromotional content. This exposure can significantly influence\nadolescents’ health behaviours and perceptions. Potvin Kent\net al. (2024) found that youth exposed to digital marketing of\nunhealthy foods, including fast food and sugary drinks, were\nmore likely to consume these foods and more frequently. The\nconstant exposure to promotional content can lead to increased\nconsumption of unhealthy foods, contributing to poor dietary\nhabits and related health issues. This highlights the importance of developing digital health literacy skills to help\nadolescents critically evaluate the online health information\nthey encounter, whether intentionally sought or not. According to Oddone and Merga (2024) there is a role school library\nprofessionals and educators can take in the development of\n\n\nadolescents’ critical evaluation skills, which include questioning\nsource credibility, recognising misinformation, and identifying fake expertise, to mitigate the risks. Without adequate digital\nliteracy skills, adolescents are more susceptible to the persuasive tactics of digital marketing, which can exacerbate unhealthy\nbehaviours and the risk of misinformation. Instead, equipping adolescents with these skills can empower them to safely\nnavigate digital health spaces and responsibly make informed\ndecisions about their wellbeing.\n\nThe role of policy and education\nPolicy and education play a crucial role in shaping adolescents’ use of social media for health-related purposes. There is a\nrequired focus on equipping adolescents with the digital literacy skills needed to critically evaluate health information\nencountered online, consequently reducing the risk of\nmisinformation (Taba et al., 2022; Wendt et al., 2023).\nEducational interventions, both within school settings and\nthrough community-based programs, play a significant part.\nFor example, integrating digital literacy into the school\ncurriculum can help students develop the necessary competencies to navigate the digital landscape effectively, whilst also\nproviding them with free connectivity access. Also providing\nschool staff such as onsite school nurses with such skills as\nadditional resources. Additionally, community workshops and\ncampaigns led by non-government organisations can provide\npractical guidance on identifying credible sources and\nunderstanding the potential impacts of social media on health\nbehaviours. By fostering a supportive environment that\nencourages informed and responsible social media use, these\npolicy and educational efforts can significantly influence\nadolescents’ health outcomes. Further still, ongoing evaluation\nand adaptation of these programs are essential to address the\nevolving digital habits and health needs of adolescents in\nVanuatu.\nLimitations and future research directions\nThis study serves as an important direction to determine the\nonline activities conducted by adolescents to access health\ninformation and communicate about health topics, between\nurban and rural areas in Vanuatu. Future research could explore\ncross-cultural comparisons of digital health behaviours among\nadolescents across Pacific Island nations, highlighting both\nshared challenges and culturally distinct approaches. A limitation of this current study is the low number of participants\nwho completed the open-ended questions, which may affect the\ngeneralisability of the findings. Future research with a greater\nnumber of participant responses would provide a more comprehensive understanding by identifying more online platforms, apps, and specific websites and yield more detailed\ninsights into the patterns and preferences of adolescents in this\ncontext, further identifying any differences between the\nurban and rural settings, and would also ensure that the sample is more representative of the broader population. With a\ngreater number of participants, attention to gendered experiences using digital technologies for health, particularly around\nsensitive health topics, may also reveal nuanced vulnerabilities and coping strategies. To address these challenges, developing community-led interventions that center around cultural\nrelevance, and adolescent agency may help build more inclusive\n\nand responsive digital health ecosystems, which involve an\ninterconnected network of people, platforms, and policies that\nfacilitate health communication and access through digital\ntechnologies.\nThe scarcity of region-specific studies underscores the importance of our contribution, offering preliminary insights into a\npopulation that remains underrepresented in digital health\nand youth engagement research. We advocate for future studies to expand the evidence base in this area, particularly those\nthat explore culturally nuanced perspectives and age-specific\nexperiences across Pacific Island communities.\n\nConclusion\n\nAdolescents are prominent users of information promoted\nthrough digital technology, however, there exists a noticeable\ngap in research concerning their specific information needs and\npatterns of use, particularly within the Vanuatu context. This\nstudy contributes to the growing body of research on adolescent digital health practices by examining how young people\nin Vanuatu navigate online platforms to seek health information and discuss health topics. With a multitude of information\nsources available to them, it is important for researchers, healthcare providers, and educators to gain insight into how adolescents in Vanuatu use online technology to fulfill their health\ninformation needs. Our current findings show that of the digital tools used by adolescents, there was a preference for the\nuse of social media, namely Facebook, TikTok and YouTube,\nby adolescents residing in both urban and rural locations\nin Vanuatu to access health information and discuss health\nissues. These findings may be instrumental in informing decisions regarding the integration of digital health literacy\nresources into educational curricula, thereby contributing to the\nadvancement of knowledge and skills within this population.\nThus, while these findings may represent a numerical minority, their usage patterns and behaviours in engaging with digital health platforms underscore their significance in shaping\ntailored initiatives and furthering our understanding of digital health trends among adolescents in Vanuatu. Future exploration in this topic could explore cross-cultural comparisons\nof digital health behaviours among adolescents across Pacific\nIsland nations, highlighting both shared challenges and opportunities for innovative health engagement strategies that are\nculturally grounded.\n\nEthics and consent\n\nThis study was a part of the European Family Farming,\nLifestyle and Health in the Pacific (FALAH) project (n° 873185)\n(Fotsing & Galy, 2019).
