Status: approved
Date: 2025-10-30

============================================================

I am grateful to have the opportunity to review the paper
“The internal democracy of the crisis parties in Western Europe: a quantitative analysis of the role of digitalization, ideology and populism.”
The paper makes a relevant and timely contribution to the fields of party digitalization and intra-party democracy. It innovatively combines the authors’ own generational approach—defining “crisis parties” as a specific cohort emerging between the 2008 economic crisis and the pandemic—with existing large-N surveys (PPDB and POPPA) to quantitatively examine the interplay between digitalization, ideology, and populism in shaping intra-party democracy across Western Europe.
Overall, I recommend
acceptance with minor revisions
, mainly to enhance clarity, narrative flow, and stylistic consistency. Below I elaborate my comments following the journal’s evaluation criteria.
Clarity and engagement with the literature
The paper is clearly written and empirically grounded, and it engages extensively with two major streams of literature: (1) research on new and digital parties, and (2) studies on intra-party democracy in Western Europe. The review of literature is impressive and comprehensive. Nonetheless, the section
“Internal democracy in new parties of Western Europe: the existing evidence”
could benefit from a more synthetic narrative that connects the various strands of scholarship rather than listing them sequentially.
A suggested way forward is to guide the reader through a storyline:
new parties claim to enhance internal democracy through digital tools;
institutionalization and electoral pressures generate centralization;
tensions arise between participatory ideals and competitive imperatives.
This structure would highlight the paradox the paper investigates and make clearer how the authors’ quantitative approach contributes to resolving it.
Stylistically, the authors should avoid colloquial expressions such as “fortunately, data on some of these new parties…”, and ensure consistency in tone. Some signposting would also help, especially to emphasize that the literature simultaneously addresses internal democracy
and
digitalization. For instance, when noting that left-wing parties are more attentive to internal democracy, it could be clarified that the digital parties most often studied (Podemos, La France Insoumise) cluster on that side of the ideological spectrum or at least show distance from right-wing parties (Pirate Parties, Five Stars Movement).
Theoretical framework
The section on
“The generational approach and modes of internal democracy”
is conceptually sound but would benefit from a clearer articulation of its analytical core. Currently, the “generational approach” and the Von dem Berge & Poguntke IPD framework are juxtaposed rather than integrated. The reader would better appreciate the contribution if the authors explicitly stated how the generational approach provides the
population
of cases and the IPD model provides the
analytical lens
.
This framework should be signposted from the introduction, as it justifies both case selection and variable operationalization. Minor language edits are also needed (e.g., “provides a
good
cohort” → “provides a suitable cohort”; “shed more light” → “shed light”).
Study design and technical soundness
The study design is appropriate, technically solid, and well aligned with the paper’s objectives. The decision to combine PPDB and POPPA data allows for robust cross-validation between organizational indicators and expert assessments.
However, I would suggest the following adjustments:
Rename the section
“Hypothesis”
to
“Hypotheses”
, and reformulate them for clarity.
H1a:
Crisis parties—especially left-wing and highly digitalized—are expected to be more plebiscitary than older parties.
H1b:
Older parties—especially on the left—are expected to be more assembly-based.
H2a:
Populism mediates the relationship between crisis-party status and internal democracy.
H2b:
Left-wing populist crisis parties are expected to be more democratic than right-wing populist ones.
This decomposition would better reflect the paper’s analytical distinctions and facilitate the interpretation of results.
Some methodological details (e.g., missing cases, coding limitations) could be moved to an appendix, as they currently interrupt the flow of the main text.
Avoid informal phrasing such as “we bet on the Bronet and Borge’s digitalization index.” Instead, state that the authors
draw on their previously validated digitalization index
.
Transparency and replicability
The data sources are clearly described and publicly available, ensuring replicability. The authors make effective use of open datasets (PPDB and POPPA) and properly acknowledge their limitations. It would nonetheless help to explicitly list the variables included in the models, perhaps in a table, to enhance clarity.
Statistical analysis and interpretation
The statistical procedures (descriptive, bivariate, and bootstrapped OLS regressions) are appropriate and carefully executed. Yet, the interpretation of results would benefit from greater precision. For instance:
At times, hypotheses are discussed as if they were rejected solely based on descriptive statistics; the logic of inference should be clearly distinguished from descriptive observations.
The finding that digitalization significantly predicts both AIPD and PIPD should be integrated back into the theoretical argument, highlighting that digitalization—not ideology or cohort—is the key explanatory factor. This insight deserves stronger emphasis throughout the paper.
Discussion and conclusions
The discussion effectively summarizes the findings, but its narrative could be sharpened. The authors convincingly show that:
Crisis parties are
not
more plebiscitary than older ones;
Digitalization—not cohort or ideology—best predicts intra-party democracy; and
Populism negatively affects internal democracy, with left-wing populist crisis parties remaining more democratic than their right-wing counterparts.
This is a valuable contribution that nuances the dominant assumption of digital parties being inherently plebiscitary. However, to strengthen the contribution, I suggest:
Framing the discussion more explicitly around the
contrast with prior qualitative literature
(e.g., Gerbaudo, Barberà & Rodríguez-Teruel, Deseriis).
Avoiding vague formulations such as “on a considerable number of political parties,” and instead using “on a large-N sample of Western European parties.”
Clarifying how the findings advance the understanding of digitalization as a democratizing—or not—force within party organization.
Style and clarity
The manuscript is overall well structured but requires stylistic editing for conciseness and academic tone. Recurrent colloquial expressions (“fortunately,” “we bet on,” “with all these data”) should be replaced with formal equivalents. Some sentences could be simplified for readability, and small grammatical issues (articles, plurals, transitions) should be corrected.
Final recommendation
The article offers a significant and empirically rich contribution to debates on intra-party democracy and digital transformation. It combines innovative conceptualization with robust quantitative evidence. The revisions suggested here are minor and mainly aimed at enhancing readability, narrative coherence, and stylistic precision.
