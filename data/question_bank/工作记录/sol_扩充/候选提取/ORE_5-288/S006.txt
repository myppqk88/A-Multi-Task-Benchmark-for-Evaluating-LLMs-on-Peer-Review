Article: The internal democracy of the crisis parties in Western Europe: a quantitative analysis of the role of digitalization, ideology and populism.

Reviewer: Marco Lisi
Status: approved

============================================================

This paper addresses the important topic of internal democracy in new parties, testing whether newer organizations differ in their degree of intra-party democracy compared to older parties. The study employs comparative datasets and appropriate statistical methods to examine these questions. While the research makes a valuable contribution, several aspects could be strengthened to enhance clarity and analytical rigor.
Theoretical Framework and Literature Review
My first recommendation concerns the presentation of theoretical arguments. The literature review would benefit from greater coherence and structure. Currently, it does not clearly articulate the main analytical dimensions or debates in the field. I suggest reorganizing this section to address the key theoretical themes systematically rather than presenting a fragmented overview. The discussion of new versus old parties should similarly be restructured to provide a more focused analytical narrative rather than dispersed observations.
I recommend treating the 'theoretical framework' section and the 'hypothesis' section together, as the current separation creates artificial boundaries. The theoretical framework primarily introduces the concept of 'crisis parties' from previous literature rather than developing an integrated analytical framework for the study.
Hypotheses and Research Design
Second, I suggest reformulating Hypothesis 1. The current formulation conflates two distinct propositions: one concerning new parties and another concerning older parties. These should be distinguished as separate hypotheses since they rely on different dependent variables and theoretical mechanisms.
Third, the operationalization of 'crisis parties' requires further development. Specifically: What electoral threshold is used to classify parties as 'crisis parties'? How does the PPDB dataset criteria align with this conceptualization? A more explicit discussion of case selection and the complete list of parties classified as 'crisis parties' should be provided in the main text or methodological appendix rather than only in supplementary material, to ensure transparency and replicability.
Presentation of Results
Page 14 references Table 3, but the actual table presented is numbered as Table 4. Please verify table numbering throughout the manuscript to ensure consistency.
Regarding Table 5: I recommend reconsidering the presentation of regression results based on a very small sample (N=39). With such limited observations, the statistical power is questionable and results may be unstable. As an alternative, you might consider adding an interaction term between 'crisis parties' and populism to the main analysis, which would allow you to test the conditional relationship while maintaining adequate sample size. If you retain the separate analysis, please discuss the limitations explicitly and consider reporting measures of model fit and diagnostics to assess reliability.
Minor Suggestions
Overall, the paper tackles a relevant and timely research question with appropriate data and methods. Addressing these structural and analytical refinements will significantly strengthen the contribution and enhance the manuscript's clarity and rigor.
