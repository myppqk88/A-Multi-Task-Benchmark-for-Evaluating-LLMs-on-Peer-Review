Article: Force majeure impact on citizen science: Perspective from an EU funded project
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.19184.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Michael Jopling
Status: approved
Date: 2025-04-04
Report ID: 52271
============================================================

This case study report outlines the challenges of undertaking a citizen science project, aimed at training citizen scientists to participate in a research project examining online tracking and data privacy, during the COVID-19 pandemic and how they were overcome. The report is impressively honest about the researchers' self-professed naivety in not having expected the move online to affect the project as much as it did, given its online focus. The report outlines the methods used and the levels of participation achieved and concludes with convincing implications for large-scale citizen science activities, especially during such force majeure events.
Inevitably, the report begs a number of questions, such as how representative were the anonymised comments collected in Box 1? How did project leaders explain the relatively low rates of both MOOC completion and "conversion" of participants into becoming citizen scientists? And how far can the results can be applied to citizen science activities more widely? In fact, more consideration of the limitations of the research would have been welcome, but this should not detract from an interesting and important project examining key aspects of data privacy.
