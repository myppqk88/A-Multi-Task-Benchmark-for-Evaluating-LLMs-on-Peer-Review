Article: Stakeholder engagement in European research and innovation: An investigation into how and why EU R&I projects develop engagement tools
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.19907.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Johannes Starkbaum
Status: approved
Date: 2025-05-28
Report ID: 54067
============================================================

The article by Gudek et al. explores how tools supporting stakeholder engagement are developed, deployed, and sustained within EU R&I projects. It critically engages with the practical implementation of these and how they relate to the broader R&I structures. Some points to consider:
Critical perspective with EU R&I (introduction)
: While the article shortly addresses the “projectified” nature of European R&I in the introduction, I miss a more critical perspective on EU ambitions for stakeholder engagement. There exist many publications that engage critically e.g. with RRI, its conceptualization, implementation etc. The same goes for Mission Orientation. The article would benefit from integrating some reflection on the R&I policy level at this stage, especially as you later engage in criticism of how R&I is organized on a broader scale (discussion).
Definition of tools (introduction)
: The definition of engagement tools in the introduction is quite vague, referring to “actionable guidelines and practical processes”. This makes sense given the empirical span of aspects/tools covered in this study, but yet it would be good to give some concrete examples and in which phases of projects they are planned and employed.
Concrete examples (results)
: In line with the point above, it would help the reader to provide more concrete examples of engagement tools in the results section. Often, it is unclear if arguments mainly refer to things like workshops, policy briefs or art interventions, which makes a difference.
Interview questions (methods section)
: Some information on the rough topics of the interview questions would help to make data collection more transparent
Coding (methods section)
: You depart from grounded theory and the article of Saldaña 2021 to describe your open coding. Did you stop at this stage or have further steps of integration, like axial or selective coding, been employed? Consider adding some more information about how you developed a red threat or categories, i.e. broader topics emerging from single codes.
Timeframe (introduction, results)
: You speak of “tools developed and deployed
within
these projects” (p.4). But aren’t many activities you refer to in proposal phases not the project as such? One could argue that the proposal is part of the project, but it might be good to clarify. This is something which is also relevant for the results section, e.g. purpose section (which might mainly refer to proposal phase?).
Project-focused and user-focused approach (discussion
): The use of these ideal typical types of practices for tool development and application are interesting and cover an important aspect to consider for the topic of the study
Minor issues:
P2: You write that “This study contributes to the broader discourse on sustainability innovation…”. To me, it is not clear why the term sustainability is prominently referred to in this context as I don’t see this as a main ambition of this article.
P4: “…ranging from food security and the climate crisis to maintaining global competitiveness” reads as if the Eu would tackle these only to maintain competitiveness, which is a key aspect but not the only driver.
Disclosure of (potential) conflicts of interest
More than ten years ago, I worked with one of the authors on a research project but since then we haven’t had any contact. I see no conflict of interest for this review.
