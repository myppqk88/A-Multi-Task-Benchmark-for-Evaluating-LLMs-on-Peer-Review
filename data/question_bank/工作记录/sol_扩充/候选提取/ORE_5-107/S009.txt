Article: Stakeholder engagement in European research and innovation: An investigation into how and why EU R&I projects develop engagement tools
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.19907.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Krzysztof Goniewicz
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2025-06-02
Report ID: 54296
============================================================

Your article presents a rich and well-structured qualitative investigation into how EU R&I projects develop stakeholder engagement tools. I found the phased model (Purpose, Prototyping, Praxis, Post-project continuity) and the typology of project-focused vs. user-focused approaches both original and valuable. At the same time, I have a few questions and suggestions that may help sharpen your analysis and strengthen your conclusions:
Can you clarify why the study focuses primarily on agri-food and bioeconomy projects? I understand the relevance of Food2030, but how do you see your findings applying to other sectors of Horizon Europe?
Could some of your conclusions be sector-specific?
You briefly mention generalisability in the limitations, but I suggest introducing this question earlier, perhaps in the Methods or Discussion — to help readers frame the scope.
The distinction between project-focused and user-focused trajectories is a useful analytical lens. At the same time, I find the boundary between these categories occasionally too rigid. Several examples in your data suggest hybrid practices.
Could you expand your framework to reflect a spectrum or continuum rather than a binary? Would a typology with intermediary cases  better reflect the realities of Horizon Europe project dynamics?
You refer to theoretical sampling and mention saturation after 10–11 interviews. Can you explain how saturation was assessed across such a diverse sample? Did you observe differences in tool development logics between bioeconomy and agri-food projects? Between digital tools and analogue ones?
A brief matrix or summary of project types, tools, and their assigned approach would enhance transparency.
You raise important issues about whose voices shape engagement tools. I’d like to see this developed further. When certain groups are excluded from co-creation, what are the consequences? Do tools risk reproducing institutional logics rather than transforming them?
Can you engage more with literature on co-optation and participation fatigue?
The analysis of post-project challenges is one of the strongest sections. You highlight multiple strategies for sustaining tools, but I wonder: Which ones have actually worked? Are there examples where uptake by municipalities, ministries, or commercial partners has occurred?
Could you reflect more critically on the tension between open science ideals and commercialisation logic? When a tool becomes a product, how does this affect inclusivity and access?
Given the qualitative nature of the work, I understand the restrictions on data sharing. Still, would you consider including your full coding tree, code definitions, or a sample coded excerpt as supplementary material?
Minor points
The Abstract could specify the number of projects and interviews for clarity.
A schematic diagram illustrating the two approaches across the four phases might make your conceptual contribution more accessible.
In the Discussion, when referring to Horizon Europe’s administrative burdens, can you be more specific? Are there procedural or reporting rules that especially limit co-creative practices?
Conclusion
This is a timely and methodologically sound paper that contributes new insight to the literature on stakeholder engagement in EU R&I. Addressing the questions above could help refine your conceptual model, clarify your empirical base, and enhance the practical relevance of your findings for project coordinators, funders, and policymakers.
