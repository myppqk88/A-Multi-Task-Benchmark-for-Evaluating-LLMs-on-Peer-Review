Comment #2:
Introduction.
The introduction needs to be captivating and focus on a substantiation of the chosen research interest, focus or problem. The researchers need to identify a gap in existing research about the research interest, focus or problem in the study, as implied in the title. Studying “Assessment of the use of Open Educational Resources at five European Library and Information Science”- you need to put a reader on a clear picture of what studies have been conducted in LIS context based on OER. In so doing you will be in the position to identify existing gaps.  Researchers may succeed in substantiating the existence of the chosen research problem (i.e. the existing gap in research) through valid evidence obtained from recent literature. Briefly outline of the problem exposing the reader to the problem you want to investigate. Here you deliberate on the phenomenon under investigation looking at what the literature says about it. In this section, you need to provide a global picture of what is happening under the topic that is being studied. For example, when writing about OER in LIS sectors ; you need to provide a discussion globally for example, choose at least 4 countries from the West including Asia (UeSA, UK, China, Africa, Brazil,  India etc.). Thereafter discuss what is happening at the national levels (countries) including locally (the area where your study will be conducted).
Response:
Some improvements have been made in the section “Main actors, context and scenarios of OER” to reinforce our view, expressed in that section, that the literature about OER use is uneven and with little-investigated empirical effects of use of OER (see Otto et al, 2021). Furthermore, it has to be considered that our work contributes to the literature with a case study not previously done about use of OER during the pandemic at LIS European institutions, through qualitative fieldwork using surveys and focus groups. It is not intended to be an exhaustive state of the art in the form of a literature review, as that would be another type of article. We believe that the Introduction builds on a sufficient body of previous work to show that there are few studies that have carried out concrete evaluations of the use of OER in the university environment, considering the actual opinions of teachers and students. The literature presented in the Introduction is complemented by that used in the Discussion of the results sections. In total, 54 references have been analysed, most of them specifically linked to the field of OER in university education during the pandemic. We think most of the things requested in this comment by the reviewer can be traced in our text devoted to discussing the results, and also in the Introduction.

Comment #7:
Findings.
In this section, you provided a lot of quotations. You need to narrate your results. We want to hear your own voice when presenting results rather than imposing quotations. Presenting findings guided by the theoretical framework or conceptual framework always creates a better logic in this calibre of research.
​​​Response:
: The outputs of our work, for the sake of the clarity of our text, are presented in two consecutive and related sections. First, “Results” as a reasoned inventory of the main situations and experiences with OER derived from actual quotations captured at the interviews and focus groups with the participants. Every set of quotations is framed with a narrative comment and a headline of the issue/idea involved. Second, in the “Discussion” section we go deeper in the presentation of finding with our voice, but with the contrast with other works from the literature on the issue. In the “Results” section, we followed our agnostic and inductive approach to read and interpret the transcriptions to give the voice to the participants. In the “Discussion” section, it was the time for us as researchers to use our own voice to present results.
