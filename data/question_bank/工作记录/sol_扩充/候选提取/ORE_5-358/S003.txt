General comments:
The article is a coherent and valuable contribution to the discussion on digital ethics in the agri-food sector. It effectively combines a high level of theory with practical examples of implementation, making it useful for both researchers and software developers. A strong point of the study is its departure from theorizing about ethics in favor of showing how values can become the basis for technical creativity.
Abstract:
The abstract is complete and contains all the key elements required of a scientific abstract.
Introduction:
The introduction is well structured, with the author moving smoothly from the broad technological context to specific ethical dilemmas, and then to a concrete methodology and analytical framework.
Background:
The background to the article is a well-constructed analytical tool that allows the reader to understand why and how the pilot projects studied attempted to overcome traditional technological limitations.
Hacking compromises: excerpts from the DRG4FOOD pilot:
The section is structured around three key dilemmas, allowing for a clear comparison of different approaches to the same problem. The author does not limit himself to describing the projects, but uses them as “micro-case studies” to illustrate specific strategies. The selection of eight pilots ensures adequate technological diversity – from mobile applications to blockchain systems in the supply chain. The text accurately identifies the spectrum of interventions, which is one of the strongest points of this section. The author maintains the necessary objectivity, avoiding claims that all dilemmas have been completely eliminated. Despite its high analytical value, the text is based mainly on qualitative and narrative descriptions. It lacks hard data or harmonized indicators (metrics) that would allow for an objective comparison of the effectiveness of individual “hacks.” The analysis focuses on the declared strategies from the incubation period, which, without long-term research, does not provide complete certainty as to the ultimate effectiveness of these solutions when used on a large scale.
Synthesis: Two patterns of trade-off hacking:
The section entitled “Synthesis: Two patterns of trade-off hacking” provides a theoretical and practical summary of the analysis, giving the work a systematic character. Here, the author moves away from describing individual projects in favor of creating a universal framework for classifying strategies for resolving project conflicts. The analysis remains at the qualitative level. Although the author has created a solid theoretical framework, it lacks more precise criteria for choosing between a technological and a managerial pattern depending on, for example, the size of the organization or the degree of technological maturity of the project.
Discussion:
This section constitutes the analytical layer of the work, going beyond the description of specific cases towards a synthesis of conclusions for the entire food technology sector. Despite the high quality of the text, I see areas that could be explored in greater depth to enhance the scientific value of this section. The discussion remains at a narrative and qualitative level. There is a lack of specific indicators that a developer could use to measure how much better a “hacked” compromise is than a standard solution. The author mentions the risk of “bottlenecks” (e.g., in human verification), but the discussion lacks a broader analysis of how solutions such as human-in-the-loop or co-design will fare in the transition from pilot phase to mass market scale. Although “low-margin markets” are mentioned, the discussion could have analyzed in more detail how small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) operating outside EU funds can realistically finance costly technological “hacks” (e.g., ZKP cryptography). The argument is very strongly rooted in the European reality. It would be an improvement to address whether these models are applicable in jurisdictions with different data protection standards or lower trust in institutions.
Conclusions:
The conclusions section is a logical conclusion to the article. It effectively combines a high level of theory (digital ethics) with practical conclusions for developers and decision-makers, emphasizing that building trust in technology is a continuous and demanding process. Despite its high quality, there are some areas in the conclusions that could be explored in greater depth. The author mentions the costs in terms of complexity, but does not indicate how organizations should assess whether a given “hack” is economically viable in the long term. The conclusions focus on paths to success. There is a lack of emphasis on the boundary conditions that make trade-off hacking doomed to failure, which would be a valuable warning for practitioners.
