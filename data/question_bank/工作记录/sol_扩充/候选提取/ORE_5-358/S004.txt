Article: Trade-off hacking: Have the cake and eat it too? Turning competing interests into win-wins in data-driven food technology
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.21771.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Charis M Galanakis
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2026-02-03
Report ID: 65045
============================================================

This study aims to reconceptualise ethical and functional trade-offs in data-driven food technologies through the lens of “trade-off hacking,” shifting from zero-sum compromises to innovation-oriented, user-centric solutions. By analysing eight Horizon Europe DRG4FOOD pilot projects, it identifies recurring tensions (privacy versus personalisation, performance versus explainability, and security versus user experience). It systematically examines how they are addressed in practice. The main outcome is a typology of reproducible design patterns that distinguishes technology-driven from governance-driven resolutions and demonstrates how trust, responsibility, and performance can be mutually reinforcing. The study is conceptually original, empirically grounded, and offers a compelling, well-argued contribution to responsible digital innovation and is highly commendable.
Clarify the methodological approach more explicitly, including case selection criteria, data sources, and analytical steps, to strengthen transparency and replicability and to help readers better assess the robustness and transferability of the findings across contexts.
Provide a more apparent justification for focusing on only three trade-offs, discussing whether other relevant tensions were considered or excluded, and how this choice may shape interpretations and limit the generalisability of conclusions in broader food systems.
Expand the discussion of limitations by more critically reflecting on potential biases introduced by the responsible incubator setting, funding incentives, and coaching support, and how these factors might affect applicability outside Horizon Europe programmes and markets.
Include more concrete comparative analysis across the pilot cases, possibly through a synthesis table or metrics, to better demonstrate the relative effectiveness of different strategies and avoid an overly descriptive, narrative-driven assessment of outcomes and impacts observed.
Add clearer implications for practitioners by translating identified patterns into actionable design guidance or decision criteria, helping developers understand when to prioritise technological solutions versus governance-driven approaches under real-world constraints, including scale, cost, and maturity levels.
