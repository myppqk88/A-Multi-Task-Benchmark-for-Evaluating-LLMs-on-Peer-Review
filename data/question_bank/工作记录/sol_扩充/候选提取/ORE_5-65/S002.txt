Article: Copilot in service: Exploring the potential of the large language model-based chatbots for fostering evaluation culture in preventing and countering violent extremism
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.19612.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Krithi Pushpanathan
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2025-03-25
Report ID: 51988
============================================================

This manuscript makes a significant and timely contribution to the ongoing discourse on utilizing large language models (LLMs) for preventing and countering violent extremism (P/CVE). Through the qualitative analysis of its responses to 50 pre-designed prompts, this study evaluates Copilot as a recommender system, providing valuable insights into its potential to enhance evidence-based policy and practice within the P/CVE field. Overall, the manuscript is well-written and contributes meaningfully to the ongoing conversation on AI-assisted decision-making in P/CVE. That said, I have a few minor suggestions that could further refine the manuscript:
The authors assert that "limitations such as ‘bullshitting’ and hallucinations in early AI developments (Zhao et al., 2023) are no longer a primary concern." However, recent research (e.g., Steyvers et al., 2025) continues to demonstrate that hallucinations remain a significant issue and that users tend to overestimate the accuracy of LLM-generated responses. Given these findings, I would caution against the claim that hallucinations are no longer a primary concern. (see: Steyvers, M., Tejeda, H., Kumar, A.
et al.
What large language models know and what people think they know.
Nat Mach Intell
7
, 221–231 (2025).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00976-7
)
Section C in Methods, Criteria for Analysis, provides a clear overview of the evaluation framework and methodology. However, it does not specify who conducted the qualitative assessment of Copilot’s responses. Were these evaluations performed by domain experts, researchers, or practitioners in P/CVE? Additionally, how many evaluators were involved? If multiple evaluators participated, were any measures taken to ensure consistency, such as inter-rater reliability assessments? Providing this information would enhance the transparency and credibility of the evaluation process.
The Findings section presents key results, but given that LLMs evolve continuously through manual updates and user interactions, it is unlikely that identical responses will be obtained in future replications of this study. To facilitate longitudinal research and ensure transparency, I strongly recommend that the authors provide the full set of raw data (i.e., Copilot’s responses alongside the corresponding evaluations) as a supplementary table. This would allow future researchers to assess improvements in LLM performance over time and strengthen the reproducibility of the study’s findings.
