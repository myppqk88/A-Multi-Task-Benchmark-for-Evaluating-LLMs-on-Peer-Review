Dear Reviewer, Prof. Jorge Guerra: We sincerely thank you for the time and care devoted to reading our manuscript and for your constructive and encouraging assessment. We particularly appreciate your recognition of the PIK model as a timely theoretical contribution and of the manuscript’s overall scientific standard. Below, we respond point-by-point to your recommendations, indicating where the revisions were implemented in the revised manuscript.
1. Methodological transparency: clarifying the transition from the corpus to the geometric structure of the PIK model
We fully agree that the transition from the reviewed literature (43 sources) to the model’s specific structure needed to be more explicit. In response, we strengthened the methodological account by adding a dedicated subsection describing the thematic coding workflow and how it informed model construction.
Added a detailed “Thematic coding and model construction” subsection (Section 2.3
): We now specify the unit of analysis (“conceptual claims”), the two-cycle coding process, the role of iterative refinement, and the explicit transition from thematic coding to relational modelling.
Added and referenced a flow diagram (Figure 1):
Figure 1 summarizes the coding and modelling workflow visually, making the methodological progression from corpus delimitation to relational modelling transparent.
These additions were intended to strengthen the validity and transparency of the proposed framework by showing, step-by-step, how thematic coding outcomes were reorganised into the model’s relational architecture rather than presenting the geometry as a purely authorial design choice.
2. Operationalising interdependence: providing concrete examples of bidirectionality
We agree that “bidirectionality” benefits from being illustrated through concrete, practice-grounded examples that clarify how knowledge domains and identity narratives can co-evolve.
Added a new subsection “Illustrative bidirectional links in practice” (Section 6.7):
This subsection provides two short scenarios to exemplify (i) how Knowledge of Topics (KoT) may contribute to identity narratives/positionings via bridging constructs (e.g., professional noticing and epistemic orientations), and (ii) how identity narratives/positionings may shape the activation and prioritisation of knowledge resources via bridging constructs (e.g., self-regulation and situational awareness).
We also explicitly frame these scenarios as conceptual exemplifications rather than empirical cases, in line with the integrative-theoretical nature of the review, while still making the proposed interdependence more concrete and interpretable.
3. Practical application: operationalising PIK as a diagnostic tool in teacher education
We appreciate the suggestion to move beyond abstraction by indicating how PIK can be used as a diagnostic lens in teacher education programmes. We addressed this by adding an explicit “use-case” in the conclusions.
Added “PIK as diagnostic tool in teacher education” (Section 7.2):
We describe how programmes can collect evidence across three complementary layers—(i) professional knowledge and competencies, (ii) identity indicators, and (iii) bridging constructs—and how these sources can be used formatively to identify alignments and tensions and guide targeted interventions (e.g., mentoring, guided rehearsal, design-based cycles).
This addition is meant to clarify how the model can be operationalised in realistic teacher education settings using low-burden artefacts commonly employed in programmes (e.g., annotated lesson plans, video-based noticing tasks, structured reflective prompts, portfolio entries).   Finally, we would like to thank you again for your supportive evaluation (comprehensiveness, citation support, accessibility, and appropriateness of conclusions). Your recommendations helped us improve the manuscript’s methodological transparency and strengthen the connection between the integrative synthesis and the model’s structure and potential use in teacher education.
