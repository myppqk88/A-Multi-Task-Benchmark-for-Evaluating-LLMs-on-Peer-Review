============================================================

I found this paper to be highly valuable for other researchers and even those in charge of planning and developing curriculum for teacher educators. However, there is need to pay attention to few method and analytical approaches in the paper to ensure clarity and applicability of its findings.
In the abstract under the background, the word "unprepared" seems inappropriate. It is the degree that supposed to prepare them, not the workforce. It could read: "Many graduates are discouraged about the labor market...." The statement should be reviewed in first paragraph of the introduction. It seems that the student selected for the focus group discussion were selected because of their position during the course. It might be more purposive instead of convenient, except there are many of them, from which 10 were selected as they are available.
The authors should check some typographical errors in the manuscript, such as paragraph sentence five in paragraph six of the introduction.
I don't think it is necessary to have a title on a single paragraph: Service-learning in teacher education.
Is the description of the sample not too narrow for easy identification? You do not need to mention the university.
Was there a factor analysis that showed five factors? The reliability reported is the overall I suppose.
If you use some software for both quantitative and qualitative analysis they could be mentioned under methods even if there are no citations for them.
I have not seen any discussion about the intervention introduced in this study. It is necessary to mention it, its timeline/duration, and other necessary information for the reader's understanding.
For table 1, what was used to decide the remark? If its the threshold, the mean for communication was greater than 2.50.
