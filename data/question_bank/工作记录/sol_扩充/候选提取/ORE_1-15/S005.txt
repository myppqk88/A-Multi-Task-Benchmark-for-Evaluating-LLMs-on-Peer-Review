The research article by van Alebeek
et al
. reports a single experiment with healthy volunteers who completed behavioral assessment using a mobile approach-avoidance task (AAT) with task-relevant instructions together with an implicit association test (IAT) and several self-report measures of food craving and eating behavior. The goal of the research was to investigate whether faster approach to food in the AAT was dependent on block-order in a block design with increased number of instruction switches. The study furthermore investigates the validity of the results gathered from the AAT and reports mixed results: The authors observed some associations with self-reported eating behavior and there was no association with the IAT, suggesting discriminant validity. Individual desire ratings of the food stimuli were collected a week before behavioral testing but did not predict approach or avoid reaction times. Furthermore, the authors argue that including more switches between the task-relevant classification dimensions mitigated a previously observed effect of block order.
Overall, the motivation and results from the study are well described and the manuscript has apparently been improved from the previous review cycles. Limitations of the study, particularly the very low sample size for correlational analyses, are acknowledged and the manuscript is very clear. Although their statistical approach to interpret a null effect could be more convincing (e.g., by using Bayesian analysis or equivalence test, or comparing the frequent-switch version of this submission with one of their previous data sets), their conclusion of a mitigated block order effect seems appropriate. I do not have additional comments and approve this version for indexing.
