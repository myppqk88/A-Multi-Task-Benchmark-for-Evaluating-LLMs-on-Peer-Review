============================================================

The article titled "Disinformation as an Obstructionist Strategy in Climate Change Mitigation" provides a comprehensive review of the scientific literature on climate change misinformation, aiming to understand the systemic nature of disinformation and its impact on public perception and policy. The study clearly states its rationale and objectives and the methodology is detailed enough to allow for replication (it employs a systematic literature review using the PRISMA 2020 model). The statistical analysis is appropriate, and the conclusions are well-supported by the results, emphasizing the need for global and comparative research to combat misinformation effectively. The systematic review identifies several key findings regarding the spread and impact of climate disinformation. Firstly, it highlights that technological solutions like fact-checking and misinformation immunization are prevalent, yet there is a lack of in-depth research on the operational structures that sustain systematic disinformation. The review underscores the necessity for more comprehensive and globally coordinated studies, emphasizing the financial, economic, and political roots of disinformation. It calls for better-funded research that spans multiple countries to effectively address the complex web of disinformation. I fully concur with its main findings and was pleased to see them in the discussion section, for one of the main things I identified while reading the reviewed studies is how few of them addressed the political economy of media and technology platforms. I was very happy to see that the authors also observed this. The review does not capture the work of critical media literacy, which has addressed this issue, but there are few studies that address this (ecomedia literacy is one area of media literacy that addresses the crossover between fake news and climate disinformation)
1,2
. Despite this, I don't think it impacts the overall conclusions. It would be good to have a clear definition at the very beginning that explains the difference between misinformation and disinformation. I would argue that the former is unintentional and the latter is intentional. Beyond this, I believe this is a valuable study that will advance our understanding of how to address climate disinformation. The study's findings are crucial for policymakers, scientists, and the public to develop strategies that promote accurate climate information and support effective climate action.
