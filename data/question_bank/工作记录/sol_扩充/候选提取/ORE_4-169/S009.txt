Article: Disinformation as an obstructionist strategy in climate change mitigation: a review of the scientific literature for a systemic understanding of the phenomenon
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.18180.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Ana Delicado
Status: approved
Date: 2024-10-22
Report ID: 44781
============================================================

The article provides a comprehensive literature review on climate disinformation and obstructionist strategies related to climate change mitigation. It has undergone several rounds of review, and overall, the study is based on a sound methodology, with clear and well-supported conclusions based on the results presented.
However, I have a concern regarding the treatment of the concepts of "disinformation" and "misinformation." In response to previous reviewers, the authors introduced a distinction between these terms, referencing definitions from the European Community. They also note that there is a lack of conceptual clarity in the existing literature on these terms. Specifically, they state:
“On the other hand, when reviewing the scientific literature, we can see that time and again an extensive and undifferentiated use of concepts is made, leading to greater confusion about the nature of the phenomenon at a systemic level. This can be observed with the terms disinformation and misinformation, which are often used indiscriminately without referring to or attributing the malicious nature of disinformation.”
Despite making this critique, the article itself tends to use these terms interchangeably. At times, the distinction between "disinformation" and "misinformation" seems well-justified and aligns with what is being stated in the article. However, in other instances, the usage lacks clarity. This inconsistency is noticeable from the very beginning. While the title highlights disinformation, the abstract begins with:
“This study examines the scientific misinformation about climate change, in particular obstructionist strategies”
While this does not significantly affect the overall analysis or conclusions, greater conceptual clarity would strengthen the paper. I recommend that the authors either review and adjust the usage of these terms or introduce a statement explaining how the two concepts are used in the context of the article. This would resolve potential confusion and enhance the paper's conceptual rigor.
On a minor note, there is a misplaced parenthesis in "based on an earlier analysis by (
Ong & Glantz, 2000
)".
