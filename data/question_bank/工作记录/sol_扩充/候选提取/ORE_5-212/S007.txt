Article: Logic model-based performance management systems for export promotion agencies
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.20545.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Jose M Sanchez
Status: approved
Date: 2025-10-06
Report ID: 61328
============================================================

The manuscript presents relevant and timely research, addressing a significant issue in the management of Export Promotion Agencies: the implementation of effective Performance Management Systems. The study addresses a topic of great practical and theoretical importance that has been scarcely explored in the academic literature. The proposal to use the Logic Model as a strategic tool is well-founded. The study is well structured, the methodology is appropriate for the exploratory objectives, and the results are presented clearly.
The abstract is clear, concise, and complete. It presents the background, methodology, key results, and main conclusions of the study.
The introduction effectively establishes the context and relevance of the problem. The authors clearly identify the crucial role of Export Promotion Agencies, as well as the challenges inherent in measuring their performance. The argument regarding the use of the Logic Model in this specific context is convincing. The research questions are well formulated and logically guide the development of the study.
The qualitative multiple case study methodology is well designed and executed, providing rich and contextualised findings. The choice of a multiple, qualitative, exploratory case study is entirely appropriate for the nature of the research. It enables a deep understanding of organisational practices and challenges in their real context. The selection of cases is a strong point, as it offers a rich and diverse perspective on the phenomenon. The description of the data collection and analysis process is transparent and systematic, which reinforces the credibility of the findings.
The presentation of the results, structured around the components of the Logical Model is logical and easy to follow. The comparative analysis between the different agencies is insightful, highlighting the variability. The discussion section effectively interprets the results, connecting them to the existing literature on performance management in the public sector. The discussion of implications for future research is valuable and opens up new avenues of study. The article represents a valuable contribution to both academic literature and professional practice in the field of export promotion. Its publication is recommended, subject to consideration of some minor revisions to strengthen its impact further.
Although some limitations of the Logic Model are mentioned, further exploration could be made of how these limitations manifested in the cases studied and what strategies the agencies could implement to mitigate them.
A brief discussion of how the Spanish institutional context might influence the findings (compared to other political or economic systems) would enhance the transferability of the results.
Incorporating the perspective of beneficiary SMEs into future research could provide a comprehensive 360° view of the effectiveness of these performance management systems.
In summary, this is a solid and well-executed research that deserves to be widely disseminated. My congratulations to the authors for their excellent contribution.
