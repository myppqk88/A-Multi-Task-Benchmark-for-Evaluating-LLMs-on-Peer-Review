Thank you for taking the time to review the article! I appreciate your input.
I offer responses to your comments below.
Reviewer comment: There is a lack of literature review, particularly on the factors that make academic integrity challenging in Higher Education Institutions. Some of the references are outdated. References to the African context are somehow insufficient.
Author response: Thank you – I added a paragraph on academic integrity challenges in higher education institutions, with a specific focus on the African context. Specifically, the following:

Reviewer comment: The qualitative data interpretation lacks discussion, and it seems too narrative. The use of I makes the study too personal and sometimes the researcher is too judgmental or emotional in the statements (for example, the use of ..I appreciated...).
Author response: I acknowledge the concern. However, the use of first-person voice in this qualitative report was intentional and methodologically grounded. In qualitative inquiry, the researcher is an integral part of knowledge production, and transparent first-person writing can strengthen reflexivity by making the researcher’s positioning, interpretive role, and analytic decisions visible rather than implied.
I applied the Lichtman (2014) guidance that confirms the use of a reflexive stance (which is often referred to as “confessional” notes) to enhance transparency and credibility in reporting.
To address the concern about the manuscript reading as “too personal,” I moved the researcher’s position statement closed to the methodology to clarify how my role, as the researcher and the researcher instrument, was managed to support trustworthiness. I also double checked that the use of the first person narrative style is consistent throughout the article.
Lichtman, M. (2014). Qualitative research—a reflexive stance. In Qualitative research—a reflexive stance (pp. 27-52). SAGE Publications, Inc., https://doi.org/10.4135/9781544307756.n2
Reviewer comment: There is a lack of ethical consideration as the research site is identified in the section under Discussion.
Author response: I note your comment on the identification of the research site. While the institution provided permission to be identified in the research, I now opted to refer to it in generic terms to ensure anonymity.
