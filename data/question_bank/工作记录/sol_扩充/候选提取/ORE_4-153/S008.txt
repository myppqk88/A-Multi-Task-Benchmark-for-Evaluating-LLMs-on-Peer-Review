Thank you for your valuable feedback. I have carefully considered each of your comments and made revisions accordingly to improve the clarity and strength of the paper. Below, I provide a point-by-point response to your suggestions.
"Lawful TDM" is unclear, lacks definition:
I have now briefly defined “lawful TDM” as “TDM which would be considered compliant with the applicable regulatory frameworks” in the relevant section, ensuring that this term is clearer for the reader.
Literature review: disciplinary domains unclear:
I have clarified in the introduction that no disciplinary boundaries were imposed in the selection criteria for the literature reviewed, to reflect the interdisciplinary nature of TDM. This literature spans legal, technical, and policy domains. This revision aims to better communicate the broad scope of the review.
Clarification of position vs. review:
I have made clearer distinctions between my own interpretations and the positions found in the literature. Where I offer commentary, it is now more clearly framed as a response or evaluation of the literature, rather than presenting it as a standalone position. This is particularly important for a literature review, and I appreciate your observation in this regard.
Temporal structuring of the literature:
Although I have chosen not to temporally structure the review, I have made it clearer when older sources are discussed alongside newer ones. I have provided explicit justification for the inclusion of these older sources, explaining why their contributions remain relevant despite technological and regulatory developments.
Use of CDSM definition:
I have revised the relevant section to incorporate the CDSM definition of TDM, as you suggested. This revision ensures greater coherence with existing EU legal frameworks.
Clarification regarding Article 43 (formerly Article 35) of the Data Act:
I have clarified that Article 43 (formerly 35) of the Data Act refers specifically to SGDR, not copyright, as you rightly pointed out.
Contextualisation of Kretschmer et al.'s position on fair use:
I have revisited the discussion of Kretschmer et al.'s work to ensure that it is accurately contextualised. The revision better reflects their nuanced position on fair use, highlighting their concern about legal uncertainty benefiting larger players, while also ensuring that their general support for fair use is not misrepresented.
Fundamental rights section and conflation of US/EU perspectives:
I have revised the fundamental rights section to avoid conflating EU and US perspectives. The revision now presents these frameworks more distinctly.
Generalist approach and redundancy in the review:
I have restructured the “generalist approach” section to avoid redundancy, ensuring that it builds upon prior sections rather than repeating the same sources or arguments. I have clarified how it differs from the copyright-focused discussions, making the overall structure of the paper more coherent.
Conclusions:
I have revised the conclusion to ensure that it focuses strictly on summarising the key findings from the literature review. I have removed any personal opinions or evaluative commentary that could be construed as going beyond the scope of a literature review.

 

Thank you once again for your insightful comments, which have contributed significantly to refining the paper.
