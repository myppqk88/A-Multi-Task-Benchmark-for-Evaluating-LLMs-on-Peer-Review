============================================================

It has been a great pleasure to read this article which I think provides an important methodological contribution and presents a series of interesting analyses that will be of great value to further work in the field. The article aptly outlines “machine vision situation” as a theoretical concept and demonstrates its usefulness as an analytical tool which allows for the integration of distant and close reading methods in fruitful ways. It is of particular interest to see how the method works across literature, games and art. This scope allows for valuable considerations regarding how to address the presence of machine vision technologies in a text/artwork/game and focusing on its implications for the narrative/story world/aesthetics across different fictional genres.
The authors have addressed the concerns raised by the previous reviewers and the article now stands as a thorough study that is innovative and thought-provoking both methodologically, theoretically and analytically.
One minor typo:
"The police, the algorithm, or its programmers?" is repeated twice in the introduction.
