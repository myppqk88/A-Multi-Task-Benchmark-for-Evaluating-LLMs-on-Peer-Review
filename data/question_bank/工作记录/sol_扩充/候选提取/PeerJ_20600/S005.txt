Aim: The current study was conducted to assess pharmacy students outlook towards AI,

pharmacy education, and pharmacy practice. Methods : The study used a cross-sectional,
self-administered, anonymous online questionnaire. The study was conducted at the
college of pharmacy in Saudi Arabia Participants. Year 4 and year 5 undergraduate

pharmacy students were chosen as the study population Results : A total of 133 pharmacy
students completed the survey (response rate: 82%). The mean Likert score for

perceptions of AI use in pharmacy education and practice was 3.43 ± 0.6, while the mean
score for incorporation of AI in pharmacy education programs was 3.55 ± 0.78. Students
demonstrated generally positive attitudes toward AI, supporting its development and
integration into pharmacy curricula, though ethical awareness remained moderate.

Discussion and conclusion : Most students were supportive of the development of AI in the

Ethical and legal consideration of AI used was raised by participants. They were supportive

involved understanding and interpreting AI generated results, gaining awareness of ethical
consideration of using AI in clinical practice, comprehend how basic technology process
the work, expressing how technology functions in a way that others can grasp.

Introduction

Digitalization of health has been an important aspect of healthcare that was given attention by The World

33

Health Organization (WHO). (Al-Kahtani et al., 2022) Countries were encouraged to develop and adopt

34

digital health to ensure effective delivery of healthcare services through technology. Saudi Arabia

35

36

promoting e-health and digital solutions to improve health accessibility and quality. It also aimed at

37

increasing the numbers of healthcare practitioners, and improving healthcare infrastructure. Achieving the

38

program goals and overcoming the identified challenges require implementing innovative initiatives such

39

as Artificial intelligence (AI) . (Alasiri & Mohammed, 2022)

40

AI is defined as “the capacity of machines to carry out tasks that ordinarily require human intelligence,

41

including sensing, thinking, learning, and decision-making”.(Saeed et al., 2023)

42

The dramatic increase in the utilization of AI in healthcare require healthcare disciplines students to be

43

aware of its principles. (Nikitha et al., 2024) Previous literature suggested that health profession students

Data collection form

99

The data collection tool was adapted from a previous study with same purpose (Teng et al., 2022)

100

The questionnaire consisted of four section. The first section collected students demographics including

101

age, gender, year of study and background information about future career intentions and general outlook

102

on artificial intelligence. The second section, asking about the use of artificial intelligence in pharmacy

103

education and pharmacy practice. It contained 6 items which used a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1

104

strongly disagree to 5 strongly agree. The third section, asking about incorporation of AI in pharmacy

105

education programs. It contained 7 items which used a five -point Likert scale ranging from 1 strongly

106

disagree to 5 strongly agree. Two additional questions collected information about students’ beliefs

107

regarding the time AI will affect pharmacy as a profession and how would they prefer to learn more about

108

AI.

109

Face and content validity were conducted by a group of experts in the field of pharmacy education. The

110

questionnaire was available in both Arabic and English after conducting back-translation to assure the

111

accuracy of the translation conducted the study author DA who are bilingual speaker of both languages.

Statistical analysis

122

The collected data were downloaded, coded and entered using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences

123

(SPSS) version 26.0 for Mac. Demographic and background information were described in terms of

124

frequencies and percentages. Students views on the use of AI in pharmacy education and pharmacy practice

125

(six items) used a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Incorporation

126

of AI in pharmacy education program (seven items) used a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly

127

disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The distribution of the scale was presented in percentages, as well as the

128

median and interquartile range. The internal consistency and reliability of the scales was assessed using

129

Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, with the minimum recommended level being 0.70.

Discussion

196

The current study was conducted to assess pharmacy students' views on AI. Student pharmacists were

197

generally hopeful toward AI. The findings indicate a generally positive attitude toward artificial intelligence

198

(AI) among pharmacy students, with strong support for its development and recognition of its relevance in

199

their field. The statement "To what extent do you support the development of artificial intelligence in your

200

field of specialization?" received the highest Weighted Average (WA) of 3.86, a Median of 4.0, and an

201

Interquartile Range (IQR) of 3.0–5.0, highlighting substantial enthusiasm for advancing AI in pharmacy.

202

Similarly, the statement "I believe that AI will have an impact on my job" scored a Median of 4.0, with a

PeerJ reviewing PDF | (2025:08:124161:1:1:CHECK 14 Nov 2025)

203

narrower IQR of 3.0–4.0 and a WA of 3.33, This finding suggests that students generally acknowledged

204

AI’s potential influence on their professional roles, indicating cautious optimism rather than strong

205

enthusiasm about its impact. Additionally, students expressed moderate agreement on the importance of

206

foundational AI knowledge, underscoring an acknowledgment of its growing significance in pharmacy

207

education and practice. These results suggest that while students are enthusiastic about AI's integration and

208

its career implications, there may still be room to strengthen their understanding of foundational AI

209

concepts to fully prepare them for its practical applications.

210

However, they expressed caution regarding the management of AI in the field and reported limited

211

awareness of the ethical consideration involved.

212

A similar mixed feeling was expressed by Canadian healthcare students, including pharmacists, where they

213

were optimistic towards utilizing AI in their clinical practice, but they were worried that AI will result

214

replacing healthcare professionals. (Teng et al., 2022). A recent commentary suggested a reassuring

215

statement; “AI will not replace you, a person using AI will” and encouraged academic institutions to offer

216

informational and training workshops to their students and staff. (Cain et al., 2023) equipping pharmacy

217

students and academics with the knowledge and skills required to use AI effectively, will prepare them to

218

be better positioned to survive in an AI-driven world rather reducing the risk lose their roles as pharmacists

219

and consequently losing their jobs.

220

Pharmacy students from 12 different countries including Saudi Arabia, conveyed positive views towards

221

incorporation of AI in daily pharmacy practice. However, they expressed lacking confidence in their AI

222

knowledge and showed enthusiasm for integrating AI education as part of their pharmacy degree program.

223

Ethical and legal concerns were also highlighted by pharmacy students. (Allam et al., 2024)other studies

224

raised the same concerns regarding ethical and legal aspects of using AI in pharmacy curricula and

225

In the current study, pharmacy students were supportive of integrating AI into pharmacy program curricula.

230

Their top-rated learning objectives involved understanding and interpreting AI generated results, gaining

231

awareness of ethical consideration of using AI in clinical practice, comprehend how basic technology

232

process the work, expressing how technology functions in a way that others can grasp. Other objectives

233

234

235

the same top-ranked objectives of AI incorporation into pharmacy degree program. For that reason,

236

designing an AI course should consider meeting the highest-rated objectives. (Allam et al., 2024)

237

Similar findings were expressed in a recent multi-country study that highlighted the importance of

238

incorporation of AI-based courses into pharmacy program and continuing medical education. They

239

emphasized the significance of early exposure to AI to advance knowledge and skills required in real-world

240

clinical scenarios. (Allam et al., 2024)

241

To address the identified gaps, pharmacy curricula should integrate focused modules on AI ethics, covering

242

critical areas such as data privacy, algorithmic bias, accountability in AI decision-making, and the potential

243

consequences of AI errors in clinical contexts. Case-based learning can be utilized to provide students with

244

real-world scenarios for applying ethical principles in decision-making. Interdisciplinary collaboration with

245

AI developers and ethicists can further deepen students' understanding of how ethical considerations are

246

embedded into AI systems. Coupled with practical exposure to AI tools and discussions on their ethical

247

implications, these strategies will equip students to critically assess AI-driven recommendations and

248

advocate for their responsible application in pharmacy practice. Embedding ethical training within the

249

curriculum will help students develop the competencies required to manage AI responsibly, bridging the

gap between technical expertise and ethical awareness. These efforts will not only enhance students’

251

252

To ensure practical readiness, the curriculum must extend beyond theoretical knowledge to include hands-

254

255

256

257

258

effectively in real-world pharmacy settings.

Recent research assessed pharmacy students' familiarity with chat-based AI tools, such as ChatGPT®, and

260

their usage patterns. The study found that 88% of students were familiar with these tools, primarily utilizing

261

them for assignments and studying. While 85.3% believed AI enhances academic performance, concerns

262

Similarly, Culp et al. (2023) reported that only 25% of pharmacy students in a U.S. School of Pharmacy

266

268

279

The findings of this study are subject to several limitations. First, the survey was conducted between

280

January and April 2023. Given the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence and the changing perceptions

281

surrounding its use in education, the findings may not fully reflect current attitudes or developments.

282

Second, there was an overrepresentation of female participants and an underrepresentation of males,

283

which may have influenced the generalizability of the results to a more balanced gender demographic.

284

Third, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to establish causal relationships or track changes in

285

perceptions over time. Fourth, the study was conducted at a single pharmacy college in Saudi Arabia,

286

which restricts the generalizability of the findings to similar educational and cultural contexts. Fifth, the

287

relatively small sample size of 133 participants, although adequate for descriptive analysis, may limit the

288

applicability of the findings to the broader population of pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia. The use of a

289

nonprobability convenience sampling method may have introduced selection bias and affected sample

290

diversity, as students more interested in AI or motivated to participate might have been overrepresented.

291

Additionally, reliance on self-reported data introduces the possibility of social desirability bias, where

292

participants may have provided favorable rather than entirely accurate responses.

293

294

The survey’s online format, while efficient, may have influenced participation and introduced variability in

295

responses, particularly among students with limited technological access or language proficiency. Although

PeerJ reviewing PDF | (2025:08:124161:1:1:CHECK 14 Nov 2025)

296

the questionnaire was available in both Arabic and English, students with limited proficiency in either

297

language may have been unintentionally excluded, potentially affecting inclusivity. Moreover, the study

298

did not account for prior exposure to AI technologies or knowledge levels, which could have influenced

299

perceptions. These limitations highlight the need for future studies with larger, more diverse, and multi-

300

institutional samples, incorporating longitudinal and mixed-methods approaches to capture evolving

301

attitudes toward AI integration in pharmacy education and practice.

302

Conclusion

303

The current study was conducted to provide a snapshot of pharmacy students towards the use of AI in

304

pharmacy education and pharmacy practice. Majority of students were supportive of the development

305

of AI in the pharmacy field and valued the importance of having some basic understanding of AI. Ethical

306

and legal consideration of AI used was raised by participants. They were supportive of integrating AI into

307

pharmacy program curricula. Their top-rated learning objectives involved understanding and interpreting

308

AI generated results, gaining awareness of ethical consideration of using AI in clinical practice,

309

comprehend how basic technology process the work, expressing how technology functions in a way that

310

others can grasp.
