R3-Q1 & Q2. Practical assessment detail and illustrative example
Q1: "
The manuscript presents learning outcomes that are measurable and observable, yet it does not provide sufficient detail on how they can be practically assessed. Without clear assessment tools, the applicability of the outcomes in real educational contexts remains limited.
"
Q2:
"Include at least one illustrative example of assessment methods (e.g., portfolio evaluation, project-based assessment, peer and self-assessment, or rubrics) linked to a specific learning outcome, to demonstrate feasibility in practice.
"
Response
:
We appreciate this important observation. While Annex I provides detailed specifications for each learning outcome—including observable behaviors, measurable criteria, and suggested evaluation methods—we recognize that concrete worked examples significantly enhance practical applicability for educators. To address this concern, we have added a new "Implementation notes" subsection in the Results section that presents two complementary assessment examples: the first one provides a complete assessment pathway for a single learning outcome, while the second example demonstrates integrated assessment of multiple outcomes through a community sustainability intervention project, illustrating how educators can assess several learning outcomes holistically through a single complex task while maintaining the gestalt nature of sustainability competencies and avoiding fragmentation. Additionally, we have added signposting text in the Results section (before Table 1) that explicitly directs readers to these worked examples and to the comprehensive assessment specifications in Annex I, making the connection between the learning outcomes framework and practical implementation tools more transparent.
Changes made to manuscript
: A paragraph has been added to the Results > Final learning outcome distribution, and two examples have been included in the new Results/Implementation notes subsection.
R3-Q3. Cultural adaptability and contextualization
"
Although the authors acknowledge cultural constraints, the proposed outcomes are largely framed from a Western European perspective. Considering GreenComp's intended broad application across Europe and beyond, stronger attention to cultural adaptability is necessary. Explicitly highlight these limitations and propose strategies to adapt the outcomes to diverse cultural, educational, and socio-political contexts, including Eastern European and global perspectives.
"
Response
:
This is a crucial point, and we agree that the framework's Western/Southern European origins must be explicitly acknowledged alongside a concrete strategy for cultural adaptation. We have made two significant additions to address this concern. First, we have added a new subsection to the Discussion, "Cultural adaptability and contextualization", which outlines a four-step protocol for adapting the learning outcomes to diverse contexts. This protocol involves (i) local co-design and stakeholder engagement, (ii) translation and localization of concepts (not just words), (iii) integration of locally salient issues and knowledge traditions, and (iv) equity-by-design checks. Second, we have substantially expanded the Discussion > Limitations section to explicitly state the current geographical scope, identify specific Western assumptions embedded in some outcomes (e.g., regarding political agency, democratic participation, human-nature relationships), and note the need for future comparative validations with partners in Eastern/Northern Europe and non-EU contexts.
Changes made to manuscript
: (1) The Discussion > Limitations section has been expanded to elaborate on the cultural scope and outline next steps for validation. (2) A new subsection, "Cultural adaptability and contextualization", has been inserted in the Discussion section after "Challenges and limitations".
R3-Q4. Holistic competencies and constellation-based assessment
"
The manuscript appropriately addresses the inherent tension between 'gestalt competencies' and measurable outcomes. However, it does not sufficiently illustrate how holistic sustainability competencies can be preserved in assessment and credentialing systems. Provide concrete examples of integrated or constellation-based assessment approaches that could help avoid the fragmentation of competencies into disconnected elements.
"
Response
:
This concern is directly addressed through our response to Reviewer 2's Q3, where we provide a detailed worked example of constellation-based assessment demonstrating how multiple learning outcomes can be evaluated holistically through a single complex task using cross-cutting rubric criteria. Additionally, the critical analysis of micro-credentialing risks and safeguards (our response to R2-Q4) explicitly discusses constellation-based credentialing as a strategy to preserve gestalt competence while meeting recognition requirements. Together, these additions provide both concrete examples and conceptual frameworks for avoiding fragmentation.
Changes made to manuscript
: (1) Integrated assessment example as detailed in response to R2-Q3; (2) critical micro-credentialing section as detailed in response to R2-Q4.
R3-Q5. Practical roadmap for educators and policymakers
"
The 'Future Directions' section outlines useful research avenues but remains focused primarily on academic implications. To maximize practical relevance, a clearer roadmap for application in educational practice would be beneficial. Expand this section to include specific guidance for educators and policymakers, such as strategies for teacher training, curriculum integration, and progression pathways across different educational levels.
"
Response
:
This is an excellent recommendation to enhance the practical relevance of our work. We have substantially revised and expanded the "Future Directions" section to provide concrete, actionable guidance across five key implementation domains: (1) educator capacity building through practice-based professional development; (2) curriculum integration strategies emphasizing transdisciplinary infusion; (3) staged progression pathways across educational levels; (4) quality assurance through calibration and moderation; and (5) institutional support requirements including resource allocation and policy alignment. This transforms the section from a primarily research-focused agenda into a practical implementation roadmap for practitioners and policymakers, while maintaining appropriate discussion of needed research.
Changes made to manuscript
: The opening section of Discussion > Future Directions has been replaced with a comprehensive five-point practice roadmap.
