R2-Q1. Expert panel selection: objectivity and geography
"
Expert Panel Selection: The criteria are good, but stating the number of publications or years of experience would add objectivity. The gender balance (8 women, 4 men) is noted, but a brief mention of geographical distribution (beyond 'diverse institutional contexts') would strengthen the claim of European representativeness
."
Response
:
We agree that providing objective thresholds and an explicit geographical distribution of the expert panel enhances the transparency and credibility of our validation process. The Methods section has been revised to include specific eligibility criteria for the experts (≥5 years of relevant practice and/or ≥5 relevant peer-reviewed outputs) and to list the countries where the experts are currently working (Sweden, Greece, Portugal, Spain, Italy, and North Macedonia). Furthermore, we have added a statement to the Discussion's Limitations section to explicitly acknowledge that this distribution skews toward Southern and Western Europe and have committed to broadening this representation in future validation cycles.
Changes made to manuscript
: (1) The first paragraph of Methods > Phase 2: First expert validation round has been expanded to include the objective thresholds, gender balance, sectoral mix, and country distribution. (2) The Discussion > Limitations section has been updated to explicitly note the regional skew.
R2-Q2. Co-design synthesis: transparency
"
Co-design Synthesis: The process of merging 'similar outcomes' and using 'internal Delphi surveys' is described but could be more transparent. A brief example of what made outcomes 'similar' and what criteria were used in the internal ranking would be useful
."
Response
:
We agree that transparency is crucial for the methodological rigor of the consolidation phase. To clarify this process, we have amended the Methods section to include a concrete example of how two draft outcomes were merged because they targeted the same set of GreenComp Knowledge-Skills-Attitudes (KSAs) and had overlapping observable evidence. We have also explicitly stated the three criteria used for the internal ranking—(i) precise alignment to KSA granularity, (ii) clarity of measurability/observability, and (iii) non-duplication—and the tie-breaking protocol, making the synthesis logic both auditable and reproducible.
Changes made to manuscript
: An example of the merging process and the explicit internal ranking criteria have been added to the end of the last paragraph of Methods > Phase 1: Initial co-design process.
R2-Q3. Preserving gestalt in integrated assessment
"
The discussion could be strengthened by providing a concrete example of how two or three different learning outcomes might be assessed together in a single, complex task (e.g., a community project) to demonstrate how the 'gestalt' can be preserved in practice.
"
Response
:
This is an excellent recommendation that addresses the central challenge of operationalizing competencies without losing their holistic nature. To demonstrate how the "gestalt" of sustainability competence can be preserved in practice, we have added an " Integrated assessment example" in the new Results/Implementation notes section. This example details how a single, complex task—a community sustainability intervention project—can be used to assess three distinct learning outcomes from different GreenComp areas (Systems Thinking, Futures Literacy, and Collective Action) simultaneously, using an integrated portfolio and a single analytic rubric with cross-cutting criteria rather than separate checklists.
Changes made to manuscript
: A new example has been added to the Results/Implementation notes section, immediately after the ‘Worked example (single outcome)’, to illustrate an integrated, multi-outcome assessment task.
R2-Q4. Micro-credentials and the risk of trivialization
"
The section on digital badges is somewhat optimistic. It could more forcefully address the significant risk of trivialization—where profound competencies are reduced to a checklist for a badge. A deeper critique of the potential conflict between the micro-credentialing logic and the transformative, holistic aims of ESD would be valuable
."
Response
:
We fully agree with this critical observation and have revised the Discussion to move beyond an optimistic framing of digital badges. A new subsection has been added to critically examine the structural tensions between the logic of micro-credentialing and the transformative, holistic aims of ESD. This section explicitly addresses the risks of "badge inflation," where credentials are issued without rigor, and "checklist drift," where assessment degrades from authentic competence demonstration to mere participation. To counter this, we propose a series of concrete governance and quality assurance safeguards, including rigorous evidence standards tied to authentic performance, the use of constellation-based credentialing for integrated competencies, transparent issuer accountability through detailed metadata, systematic moderation and calibration protocols, and the embedding of badge acquisition within transformative pedagogical approaches rather than as a replacement for them.
Changes made to manuscript
: A new subsection, "Critical perspective: Micro-credentialing risks and necessary safeguards", has been inserted in the Discussion section.
