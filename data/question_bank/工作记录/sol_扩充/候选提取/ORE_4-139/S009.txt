Article: Energy Transition in Northern Netherlands: seeking a balance between top-down and bottom-up initiatives
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.17431.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Benjamin Schmid
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2025-01-17
Report ID: 48327
============================================================

We would like to express our gratitude to the authors for their efforts in conducting this study. Better understanding and improving relationships between emerging local energy initiatives and municipalities is an important area of study to facilitate democratic energy transitions, not only in the Netherlands, but across Europe and globally. The study examines the expectations of various local initiatives, as well as the municipality and an intermediary in the city of Groningen, regarding their cooperation in the local energy transition. Although the study offers interesting insights into these dynamics, we see the need for a comprehensive review before we can recommend final indexing of the manuscript.
Our core issue, echoing in further questions, concerns the main purpose of the article. We are aware that the “short report” format differs from articles in other journals, as there is less space available, and the report character is more pronounced. However, the manuscript is currently written as if it is somewhere between a research article and a policy brief, which makes it ambivalent. Is the purpose of the analysis to identify and formulate specific recommendations for the situation in Groningen, or is the main purpose to provide a basis for more general findings (in the sense of a research article)? While the introduction emphasizes the former, the conclusion begins with a more general statement, indicating an ambition towards the latter purpose. The key purpose should be made clearer and less ambivalent.
If the manuscript is to be read as a research article, we have found that the embedding of the research interest in the established body of literature is underdeveloped, particularly in terms of the lack of a clear research gap relevant to the academic literature.
Furthermore, the application of Sabatier & Mazmanian’s
implementation analysis framework
as theoretical framework in the analysis seems both opaque and not entirely accurate:
The framework is only briefly mentioned in the introduction, while it remains unclear whether and how it was applied as an analytical framework in the analysis. The fact that “open coding was performed…» (p. 4) as the first step in the data analysis suggests that the framework was not used in any major analytical capacity. It is also not mentioned in the discussion or conclusion to make sense of the findings.
It seems questionable to us how appropriate the framework is for the present study (assuming that the authors refer to the study/framework published in 1985 - and not 2005 as written). First, the framework was intended to be applied to policy implementation processes and not necessarily to transitions. Second, the terms “top-down” and “bottom-up” (notably in the title of the manuscript) are not entirely clear and seem to be interpreted differently than suggested in Sabatier & Mazmanian's framework. In the original framework, as we understand it, the difference between top-down and bottom-up refers to the
analytical perspective
(with bottom-up referring specifically to a focus on the local implementation process, including what happens at the local government and administrative level; whereas municipal action is understood as top-down in the manuscript). In contrast, the authors seem to understand the terms “top-down” and “bottom-up” to refer to different
governance approaches
to implementation itself and assume a current imbalance in favour of top-down approaches that should be more balanced in order to accelerate the energy transition. While this is a common assertion in the energy transition literature, it is hardly a direct claim of the policy implementation framework (there is a misleading indirect quote on p. 3 that Sabatier & Mazmanian have made such a claim).
In light of these issues with the framework, we suggest either dropping it altogether or making it clearer how it has been applied analytically and describing more explicitly the national/top-down policies whose implementation is being examined. In any case, the authors should make it clearer what they mean by bottom-up and top-down (processes).
Furthermore, if understood as a research article aiming to make more general statements, it would be desirable in the area of analysis/methodology to learn more about how the situation in Groningen is representative of situation elsewhere - why it is an appropriate case? Also, we wondered, firstly, why only the most active and relevant LEIs were interviewed (wouldn't the non-active LEIs also have been of interest to investigate the research question), and secondly, how the identified differences between the LEIs were reflected analytically (levels of maturity, municipality as initiator or not). How did these differences influence the statements and results on the individual LEIs? At present, the studied LEIs are treated rather homogeneously. Finally, we asked ourselves how representative the interviewees are of the organizations they speak for. While a single interviewee may be sufficient for the LEIs, it remains questionable whether the statements of a single person from the municipality (who apparently does not speak Dutch well, which notably may have complicated the exchange with the LEIs) sufficiently reflect the views and interests of the ‘city’. A critical discussion on these points would greatly improve the analysis (and also the robustness of the policy recommendations), maybe instead of mentioning all the software used.
With regard to the results, we found the direct quotes selected from the interviews to be well chosen and meaningful. We would also like to positively acknowledge that the interview transcripts were published. But we would have hoped for a little more (critical) discussion when interpreting the results/statements from the interviews. This especially concerns not attributing different views between LEIs and the municipality solely to a lack of knowledge/mutual ignorance but considering the fact that these actor types operate with different institutional logics, responsibilities, and with accountabilities to different publics. Linked to this point, we would find a revision of Table 1 desirable: instead of “key activities”, it would be more helpful for the analysis to present the ‘key objectives, as these appear to us to be more relevant for differences in expectations. Consequently, it would also be helpful to add the municipality as another actor and to mention its relevant key objectives / policies.
Many of our points are particularly important if the article is to be read as a research article. But we also see them as helpful if the focus is primarily on formulating policy recommendations.
