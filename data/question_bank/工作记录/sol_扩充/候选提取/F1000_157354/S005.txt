Author Response to Review 2 (v1)
Author: Muna Al Kalbani
Date: 2025-02-10
============================================================

Thank you for your insightful feedback. In response to your first comment regarding the lack of evidence supporting the adaptation of Student Evaluations of Teaching (SETs) from course and programme evaluation to teaching effectiveness assessment, I have revised the manuscript to include additional references. Specifically, I have added a citation to Marsh (1987), which explains the mechanisms by which SETs assess various teaching dimensions, including clarity, organization, instructor enthusiasm, and student interaction. This provides a stronger foundation for the claim and addresses the broader use of SETs.
Whereas, in response to your second comment concerning the citation of Constantinou & Wijnen-Meijer (2022) and the omission of a critical discussion on SET’s potential biases and reliability issues, I have revised the manuscript accordingly. I engaged with the controversies highlighted by the authors, particularly regarding biases and the limitations in measuring teaching quality. This addition enhances the discussion's depth and ensures a more balanced and nuanced perspective.
In response to your third comment about the overgeneralization of SEEQ's global applicability, I have included additional references to support the claim. The revised manuscript now provides a more balanced discussion of SEEQ’s strengths and limitations in diverse educational contexts, reinforcing its broader applicability with stronger, more varied evidence.
In response to your fourth comment about the lack of methodological detail, I have revised the Methods section to describe the diversity of subjects, course sizes, and academic years included in the sample. Additionally, I clarified the randomization process used for course selection. These enhancements improve the context and allow a more robust assessment of generalisability.
Additionally, in response to your fifth comment regarding the inconsistency between SEEQ’s application for programmes and individual courses, I have clarified the discussion to state explicitly that SEEQ is applicable to both contexts. The revised manuscript resolves the contradiction by highlighting the need for larger and more diverse samples to enhance generalisability while maintaining SEEQ’s utility for various evaluation purposes.
Also, in response to your sixth comment, I have removed the reference to interviews in the consent statement since no interview data were collected. The consent description has also been rephrased for clarity and coherence, accurately reflecting the approved consent process by the ethics committee. Finally, in response to your final comment regarding reference inconsistencies, I have thoroughly reviewed the references and corrected duplicate entries and formatting issues. The reference list now adheres to a consistent citation style, enhancing the article's overall professionalism and readability.
