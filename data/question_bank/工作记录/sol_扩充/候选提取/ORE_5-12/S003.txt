Dear Marc-André Simard,
Thank you so much for the time devoted to the manuscript and your insightful comments. They have been a key resource in improving the manuscript and study. Detailed responses below: Overall: Using survey data, this manuscript investigates open science practices (including barriers and challenges) in integrated assessment modeling (IAM) teams. Furthermore, this manuscript proposes an open science protocol to address some key challenges and barriers (such as data weight, lack of time, awareness or knowledge, etc.) in the use of open science within integrated assessment modeling. The protocol is built around validated principles such as FAIR and TRUST. The authors provide sufficient context and information (e.g. packages, checklist, softwares, platforms, etc.) for their model to be adaptable and usable in a wide range of contexts. I do believe that this manuscript would make a great addition to the body of literature on open science, but I believe that there are some things that could be addressed before it is ready to be indexed.  While I understand that the intention of the authors is most likely to keep the introduction/review of literature brief while attempting to cover the massive landscape of OS, it can sometimes feel like some of these issues are tackled in a slightly superficial manner. For instance, a few of the paragraphs could be more fleshed out and expanded into proper sections (e.g. open data, benefits of OS) which could cover some recent empirical research results/literature on these topics.

New section (Methods):  “Methods To identify the open science practices employed by various modeling teams, as well as the main challenges and barriers they face, we designed a survey
47
. The survey was structured into three sections aligned with the traditional IAM modeling steps: (1) model(s) development, calibration, and interconnection; (2) model(s) output analysis and dissemination; and (3) tools used throughout the process. Specifically, the first section focused on the documentation process, including model documentation, input harmonization, and potential multi-model connectivities. The second section examined the management of project outcomes, addressing output standardization, the vetting process, output storage, and results presentation. Finally, the third section investigated the tools used or developed to support open science principles. This survey was sent to all modeling teams from the IAM COMPACT
46
and DIAMOND
56
consortia, gathering a total of 13 responses. IAM COMPACT, a Horizon Europe research project, aims to support the assessment of global climate goals and the design of the next round of Nationally Determined Contributions and policy planning beyond 2030. At its core are diverse multi-model analyses that emphasize the adoption and standardization of open science practices to ensure that model outputs are reliable, accessible, transparent, and reusable. On the other hand, the Horizon Europe DIAMOND project focuses on developing open-source IAMs, which stand to benefit significantly from open science principles by enhancing usability and broadening access across modeling teams. With this broad representation of models, institutions, and project scopes, our survey captures a robust and representative sample of the IAM community. The quantitative and qualitative survey results were analyzed both individually and collectively to identify patterns within specific modeling teams and to highlight common practices shared across the broader community. This dual approach allowed us to capture both the diversity of experiences and the overarching trends in how open science principles are being implemented.”
