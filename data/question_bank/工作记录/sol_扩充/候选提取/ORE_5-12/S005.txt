Summary of the article:
This paper presents an Open Science Protocol specifically tailored for the Integrated Assessment Modeling (IAM)community, based on a survey of IAM teams. The protocol is designed to improve the transparency, accessibility, and reusability of IAM models and outputs by aligning with the FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) and TRUST (Transparency, Responsibility, User focus, Sustainability, Technology) principles. The study highlights current challenges in implementing open science within IAMs—such as inconsistent documentation, limited vetting of outputs, and barriers to sharing data—and offers practical guidance to overcome these. By fostering better communication and usability of models, especially for policymakers, the protocol aims to enhance the credibility and impact of IAM-based climate assessments. A deeper engagement with recent literature and case studies specific to IAMs and these principles would strengthen both the theoretical foundation and practical relevance. Thank you for your comment. Recommendation:
There are a few areas where additional details and refinement could help to strengthen the scientific rigor of your paper. Below are some specific suggestions:
1. Strengthen the Theoretical Framework
It would be beneficial to gain a deeper understanding of how the FAIR and TRUST  principles can be practically applied to IAMs (in detail because in the paper, the 15 FAIR principles are not addressed with sufficient details). This will help better align the theoretical foundation of your protocol with current best practices in Open Science and ensure its relevance to the IAM community. Thank you for your comment. We agree that this aspect could benefit from further detail. While the provided checklist already adheres to both the FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable) and TRUST (Transparency, Responsibility, User focus, Sustainability, and Technology) principles, we recognize the importance of making these connections more explicit for the reader. To address this, we have revised the manuscript to clearly state how the checklist operationalizes these principles. Specifically, we now provide concrete guidance on how both data and metadata should be uploaded, curated, and shared. This includes direct references to the 15 FAIR guiding principles, outlining expectations for persistent identifiers, rich metadata, usage of standard vocabularies, and protocols for accessibility and long-term preservation. Furthermore, the checklist has been designed as a living tool, hosted on GitHub, where it can be refined and expanded as best practices evolve. We have also emphasized that the checklist is intended to support practical implementation across different IAM contexts by offering a structured, step-by-step framework that complements the protocol. Although the journal’s policy limits our ability to include the full checklist as Supplementary Material, we have clearly referenced its location in the manuscript and encourage the community to use and contribute to its ongoing development.
