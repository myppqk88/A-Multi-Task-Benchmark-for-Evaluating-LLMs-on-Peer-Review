Article: Knowledge and skill needs and challenges of innovation ecosystems in the Central-Eastern and South-Eastern European space sector
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.14435.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: June Wang
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2024-02-12
Report ID: 37111
============================================================

The paper presents robust empirical studies aimed at identifying the requisite knowledge and skills for startups in the space industry. The methodology employed to discern these variables is commendably logical, systematically delving deeper at each step. Still, there are noteworthy areas that warrant further consideration:
1. Geographic Distribution of Samples: I observed a predominant representation of respondents from central-eastern and south-eastern European countries, with limited inclusion from western European countries or those traditionally associated with the space industry. While the paper acknowledges this in the title, it remains unclear in the main body whether this distribution is intentional or constrained by accessibility to respondents. This discrepancy could potentially impact the perceived significance of various indicators in "space industry knowledge," such as "relevant national, EU, and international regulations" and "national policy."
2. Initial Baseline Framework: The foundation derived from desktop research on EU projects and the literature is commendable; however, additional elaboration would enhance understanding. Clarification is needed on why certain variables are grouped in specific categories, particularly as some variables lack clarity. For example, the rationale behind placing "entrepreneurship incentives" under "space industry knowledge" requires further justification.
3. Sector-Specific Considerations: While the paper is centred on the space sector, it appears that the study's design, from research methodology to identified indicators, could be applicable to any industrial sector. Is there a bespoke consideration tailored explicitly for the space industry? Even the indicators in "space industry knowledge" seem more general than space-specific, giving the impression that the study is designed for technology startups in general and then applied to the space sector as a case study.
4. Findings Exploration: The paper's findings, especially the substantial disparities among experts, innovation intermediaries, and innovators, are intriguing. However, delving deeper into the underlying reasons behind these gaps would enrich the paper and provide a more comprehensive understanding.
