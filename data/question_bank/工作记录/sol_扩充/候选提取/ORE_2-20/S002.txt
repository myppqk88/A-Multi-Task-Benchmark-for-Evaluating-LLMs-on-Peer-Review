Article: Knowledge and skill needs and challenges of innovation ecosystems in the Central-Eastern and South-Eastern European space sector
DOI: 10.12688/openreseurope.14435.1
Version: 1
Reviewer: Matjaz Vidmar
Status: approved with reservations
Date: 2022-06-28
Report ID: 29275
============================================================

This is a much-needed and clearly well-developed contribution to the field of innovation intermediation in the (European) space industry.
The article is using desk analysis, surveys and interview data to build a picture of the existing provision and skills in innovation intermediation across Europe (especially focusing on central and south-eastern Europe). The findings support existing research pointing to significant differences in needs and available support, with the dominance of soft skills, networking, etc, and a lack of support for R&D. (This has been shown in smaller studies already, like my own work: Vidmar (2019), The Ten Million Euro Question: How Do Innovation Intermediaries Support Smart Specialization? C
roatian Economic Survey
. https://econpapers.repec.org/article/iezsurvey/ces-v21_5f2-2019_5fvidmar.htm)[ref-1]
The main challenge of this paper is its reliance on a very narrow set of exclusively EU/Horizon-funded initiatives for the literature review and desk-research data.
Thus, it neglects recent significant research myself and colleagues have done in innovation intermediation in the space industry (especially in dealing with emerging / non-traditional contexts).
The key references sorely missing here would be:
Vidmar (2020).
Innovation Intermediaries and (Final) Frontiers of High-tech
. Palgrave/Springer. https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-60642-8[ref-2]
as well as:
Venturini and Verbano (2014). A systematic review of the Space technology transfer literature: Research synthesis and emerging gaps.
Space Policy.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spacepol.2014.04.003
[ref-3]
In addition, there is little discussion of the significant role of ESA, as it has been well documented by Mazzucato and Robinson (2016) M
arket Creation and the European Space Agency
. https://esamultimedia.esa.int/docs/business_with_esa/Mazzucato_Robinson_Market_creation_and_ESA.pdf [ref-4]
All of these develop actionable frameworks and taxonomies to bring together the different dimensions of the innovation intermediation roles into more simplified structures, specifically looking at the Space sector, especially Vidmar (2021). Enablers, Equippers, Shapers and Movers:
A typology of innovation intermediaries' i

Some more engagement with this literature, as well as other sources of data on skills and gaps in the European space industry, is strongly recommended (for example, see this report by Space Skills Alliance: https://spaceskills.org/towards-a-space-competencies-taxonomy#summary).[ref-5]
