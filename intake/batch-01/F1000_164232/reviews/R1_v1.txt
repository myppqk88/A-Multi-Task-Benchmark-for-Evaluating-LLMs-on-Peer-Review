This article is a systematic review analyzing research on the relationship between gamification, critical thinking, and academic performance in higher education. Using the PRISMA methodology, the authors searched the Scopus, WoS, and Scielo databases, limiting the search period to the last three years (2022–2024). The final corpus included 124 articles.
The descriptive results reveal a strong predominance of English-language literature (76%) and a clear bias toward quantitative methodology (71%). The majority of studies overwhelmingly focus on university students (92%). In-depth thematic analysis indicates that Self-Determination Theory and Gamified Learning are the most relevant theoretical approaches. The main findings confirm a positive relationship between gamification and learning motivation, which in turn is linked to fostering critical thinking and improving academic performance.
The replicability of the article identification and screening phase is high: the databases, search dates, inclusion/exclusion criteria (e.g., only undergraduate students and articles from 2022–2024) are named, and the 10 exact search equations (Table 1) and the PRISMA flowchart are provided.
Replicability fails in the content analysis and data visualization phase. For the network analysis (Figures 12 and 13) to be reproducible, the authors should:
1. Detail Software Parameters: Specify in the Methods section the exact parameters used to generate the bibliometric and thematic maps (Figures 3, 4, 5, 12, 13). This includes the name of the software (if different from Bibliometrix), the clustering algorithm (e.g., modularity), and the minimum thresholds (e.g., minimum number of occurrences or citations) used to include terms or nodes in the visualization.
2. Data Extraction Protocol (Data Charting): Given that the review was manual, the protocol for data extraction and coding of the thematic findings, which served as the basis for the conceptual networks, should be described in greater detail.
The analysis is suitable for a descriptive study or systematic mapping. The distribution data (language, methodology, year, databases, and population) are clearly presented and graphed.
The network analysis (Discussion) is qualitative in nature, which reduces the rigor of the interpretation of the importance of the themes. To improve robustness:
1. Quantitative Network Validation: The interpretation that certain clusters or nodes are "central" should be supported by centrality metrics. The authors should integrate and report Degree Centrality or Betweenness Centrality values ​​for key nodes (gamification, motivation, critical thinking) that numerically demonstrate their importance as "bridges" or their number of connections within the network. This would transform the visual interpretation into a scientifically quantifiable conclusion.
