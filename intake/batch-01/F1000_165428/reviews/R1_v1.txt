The article is, for the most part, clearly written and logically structured, so I would rate the clarity and accuracy of presentation as
partly
rather than a full yes. The introduction does a good job linking the institutional problem to the research interventions, but it is overly long and at times repeats ideas. Trimming this section and tightening the focus would keep the reader engaged and highlight the central research question more effectively. The literature cited is relevant to the topic and grounded in both national and international work, but some of it is dated. Bringing in more recent studies from 2022–2024—especially on gamification and academic leveling in STEM—would both strengthen the theoretical base and broaden the paper’s global relevance.
Regarding the study design, I consider it appropriate and technically sound, though with minor reservations. The mixed-methods, quasi-experimental approach is well suited to an educational intervention where random assignment is impractical. The use of historical control data alongside a defined at-risk experimental group provides a solid comparative framework. However, the absence of a power calculation or sample size justification is a weakness. Including such a calculation—or at least explaining why the chosen sample size is sufficient—would reassure readers that the study was capable of detecting meaningful effects.
When it comes to the level of methodological detail, I rate it as partly sufficient for full replication. The description of the two interventions is thorough enough for another instructor to reproduce them, and the open sharing of datasets and instruments via Zenodo is excellent. However, the rationale for the choice of statistical tests could be explained more explicitly. While it is mentioned that non-parametric tests were chosen due to non-normality, linking each test choice directly to the research questions and data characteristics would be helpful. It is also unclear whether any data were missing and, if so, how these were handled—this is an important detail for anyone attempting to replicate the analysis.
On the statistical analysis itself, I also answer partly. The selection of non-parametric tests is correct for the data distribution, and the results are clearly reported in terms of p-values. However, the absence of effect sizes means we cannot judge the magnitude of the improvements, which limits the practical interpretability of the results. Additionally, the paper refers to a possible “synergistic effect” when both strategies are combined, but no formal interaction test is presented. Including such a test would make this claim much stronger.
For the question about whether all underlying data are available, my answer is a confident yes. The data and instruments are hosted on Zenodo, have functioning DOIs, and are released under a CC-BY license, which is excellent for transparency and reproducibility.
Finally, regarding whether the conclusions are supported by the results, I would rate this as partly. The conclusion that the leveling course had the most impact is strongly supported by statistically significant results. The gamified kit’s effect is positive, but smaller, and this should be described with a bit more nuance. The claim that these strategies could be applied successfully in other subjects is plausible but not tested in this study, so it should be framed as a recommendation for future research rather than as an established fact.
In short, my main recommendations for improving the paper are:
Streamline the introduction and remove repeated points.
Update the literature with recent (2022–2024) international studies on similar interventions.
Provide a power calculation or clear justification for the sample size.
Clarify how missing or incomplete data were handled.
Report effect sizes for all key comparisons.
Run an interaction test before claiming synergy between strategies.
Reframe the scalability claim as a future research direction.
With these revisions, the manuscript would not only be methodologically stronger but also have greater appeal to an international readership, while still maintaining its strong local relevance.
