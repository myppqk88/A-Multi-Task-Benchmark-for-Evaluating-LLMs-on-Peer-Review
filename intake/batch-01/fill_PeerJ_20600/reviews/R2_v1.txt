Basic reporting
This manuscript describes a survey approach to understand the perspectives of pharmacy students in Saudi Arabia on the introduction of AI into pharmacy practice and education. This is a contemporary question, and the manuscript adds useful data the complement several previous studies. Greater clarity with the comparisons with these studies would be achieved by a better presentation of the results. There is also a need for a stronger linkage of the findings in this manuscript to previous findings in the Results and Discussion sections.
Experimental design
One concern with the study is that the survey was conducted between January and April 2023. With the rate of movement of AI and the ever-changing perceptions and understanding of this area, this data is almost in the category of “old”. They are still worth publishing, but the authors should note this issue in the limitations section.
The definition of the exclusion criteria is inappropriate. These criteria are listed essentially as the opposite of the inclusion criteria. This is not a proper definition of exclusion criteria. These criteria should refer only to the exclusion of subjects (for some reason) within the included group. Please correct this section. The exclusion criteria can probably just be deleted.

Additional comments
The tables were difficult to read. In the revised manuscript, please make Table 1 wider so that the question goes across the page. Also, have the possible answers on a single line, so that the reader can get a better view of the data.
Similarly, for Table 2, write the question going across the page and then have the data below each question. This would be much more readable. The use of the questions from Teng et al. is helpful for comparison. Please add a column in Table 2 that allows a direct comparison of the numerical data with those from Teng et al. for pharmacy students (Figure 3 in that paper). Also, there is no need to repeat the questions and the data in the text. Just make comments that direct the reader to Table 2.
Use a similar format for Table 3 (text across the page) and also make it easier for the reader to compare the results with those in Teng et al. (Figure 4 in that paper). This will greatly increase the value of the data in the manuscript and will make a good contribution to the understanding of the perspectives on AI of pharmacy students globally. Also, avoid restating the results for Table 3 in the text. For Table 4, please use the same across-the-page format for the text.
The statement in the Discussion (Line 246), “reflecting optimism about the potential influence of AI on professional roles,” is not clear. Is it certain that this is the correct interpretation of the data?
The question of student comfort with AI tools was also addressed in a recent article:
Culp et al. Am J Pharm Ed 2024;88:100696.
A question on knowledge of AI tools (Fig. 2B in Culp et al.) gave an initial result of about 25% Strongly Agree+Agree, 50% Neutral, 25% Disagree+Strongly Disagree for students in a US School of Pharmacy.
Comments on how these data compare with the findings in this work would be helpful, perhaps as part of the paragraph on Lines 306-312. This kind of comparison will contribute to and advance the literature on the comfort level of PharmD students with AI tools.

Teng et al. can be brought in here, as well as the cited studies, and a better comparison can be made with the data in the manuscript survey. One further comment is that the sentences on Lines 313-316 require a reference.
