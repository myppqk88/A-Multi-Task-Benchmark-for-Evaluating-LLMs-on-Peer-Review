Reviewer Report
1. Is the work clearly and accurately presented and does it cite the current literature?
Partly.
Add a clear statement of the research gap and contribution at the end of the literature review.
Reduce redundant citations and improve transitions between paragraphs.
Consider synthesizing studies thematically (e.g., hierarchical vs. functional vs. matrix) rather than listing individually.
2. Is the study design appropriate and is the work technically sound?
Mostly yes, but some methodological clarifications are needed.
Include sampling rationale, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and demographic characteristics of respondents.
Report reliability coefficients (Cronbach’s alpha or composite reliability) for each construct.
Strengthen the integration of qualitative and quantitative results in the discussion.
3. Are sufficient details of methods and analysis provided to allow replication by others?
Partly.
Add a table summarizing constructs, items, and measurement sources.
Include a brief subsection on “Data analysis procedures” to distinguish between quantitative (SPSS, regression, correlation) and qualitative (NVivo, thematic analysis) steps.
Discuss ethical clearance more succinctly—keep key approval numbers but reduce repetition.
4. Is the statistical analysis and its interpretation appropriate?
Largely appropriate, but could be strengthened.
Expand the explanation of R² and F-statistics to interpret effect strength.
Present a summary table for reliability, validity, and discriminant validity (if applicable).
Use APA or F1000 statistical reporting standards consistently (e.g.,
r
= .53,
p
< .01).
5. Are the conclusions supported by the results?
Yes, but they could be more analytical and less descriptive.
Add a short subsection titled “Implications for Policy and Practice.”
Discuss limitations (e.g., small sample, cross-sectional design) and suggest areas for future research.
End with a concise summary paragraph reinforcing the main contribution.
6. Is the language and style appropriate?
Edit for brevity and clarity, especially in the literature review and methodology.
Simplify tables—retain essential figures and move detailed statistics to supplementary materials if possible.
Ensure consistent tense (past tense for results, present for discussion).
7. Overall Recommendation
Status:
Approve with major revisions.
The paper addresses a relevant and underexplored issue in higher education management in Uganda. Its theoretical grounding and mixed-methods approach are strong, but the paper needs revision to improve clarity, methodological transparency, and analytical depth.
