       Thank you for this valuable feedback. While the article's focus might appear broad, as
       stated, it draws on a broader doctoral research, which also extensively looks into the history
       of pre-2004 Polish migration to the UK. Therefore, the post-accession migration is not
       studied in a vacuum, but as a part of the complex genealogy. Regarding the comment on
       “particular bad treatment of Poles”, it is important to clarify that the study aims to uncover
       how European integration contributes to their vulnerability, not necessarily to argue that
       Poles are the worst-treated group overall. Regarding participant selection, the methodology
       explicitly states the rationale for focusing on individuals with work experience in low-paid
       sectors: this was a strategic choice to trace a possible correlation between migration
       experience and vulnerability to exploitative practices. While these sectors may indeed
       include a range of jobs, research indicates that construction, agriculture, food, hospitality,
       cleaning, and care-giving are common sectors for exploitative practices. The sample, which
       included participants with master's and doctorate degrees working in these sectors, allowed
       for investigation of the phenomenon of skills downgrading and how even highly qualified
       migrants might accept such roles due to better earning opportunities abroad, despite the
       conditions. Besides, a dedicated part of the broader study zooms in on the characteristics of
       the conveyed exploitative practices, unveiling the prevailing “routine everyday” exploitation
       as opposed to solely severe forms. Therefore, the broader aim was to illuminate the specific
       vulnerabilities and exploitative practices they face despite their EU citizenship and
       “privileged” position relative to other migrant groups. In addition, non-economic factors for
       migration are also acknowledged, with the collected primary evidence pointing to a wide
       range of motivations, varying from self-development and personal growth, to socio-cultural
       and political context in Poland. The comment on the need to limit the scope of the evidence
       and related evidence is retained to enhance the quality of future related research outputs.
