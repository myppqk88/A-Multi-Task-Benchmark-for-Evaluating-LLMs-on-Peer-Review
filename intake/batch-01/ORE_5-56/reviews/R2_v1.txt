       This article titled, ‘Enacting semi-periphery: conditions of post-accession migration from Poland to
       the UK’ explores Polish migration to the UK through the lens of world systems theory. It posits that
       the enlargement of the EU has not translated to inclusion of periphery states in the ‘core’, rather,
       migration patterns re-enforce Poland’s status as a periphery state. The article is clear, well-written
       and well researched and looks to be an excellent contribution to academic literature on inequality
       and migration.

       Starting from the analytical framework: This article would benefit from expansion on its guiding
       theoretical framework. While the premise is indeed convincing on its own, in this paper, it would
       require further elaboration. The EU core and periphery are not well defined and established and a
       number of generalizations are made throughout. For instance, the author mentions unequal
       exchange of goods in passing but does not specifically cite statistics. As this is a novel application
       of this lens, I recommend further data to convince readers of the strength and relevance of this
       lens in the intra-EU context, as opposed to its usual application at the global level.

       Moving on to the case study and discussion: The relevance of bio-politics and biopower remains
       unclear. Inclusion of this as an area of further research in the case study/discussion section of this
       paper is somewhat confusing to the reader and may be best placed in a conclusion. It is unclear
       how this relates to the World Systems Analysis discussed in the previous section and requires
       further elaboration if this were to be kept.

       Beyond the initial discussion, there is very little actually depicting how the immigration of Poles to
       the UK has re-enforced Poland as a periphery state. The data depicts poor working conditions and
       exploitation of migrant workers, however the link to the thesis is not clear. There appear to be
       many implied links and connections made, however it does not yet sufficiently prove the thesis.
       Furthermore, the conclusion introduces new data that would be best placed in the discussion, if it
       is indeed a part of the argument.

       On to the conclusion: The conclusion explicitly states that WSA explains the positioning of Poles in
       the labor market through a look at inequalities. However the ‘red thread’ is not yet clear – there

    are several lines of thinking (WSA, bio-politics, migrant decision making, un-belonging) but they
    are not sufficiently connected. I understand that this likely because the interview content was rich
    and it indeed appears to be! However the thesis is made somewhat disjointed and I’d therefore
    recommend that the author focuses more clearly on one or two of these elements.

    Some small notes:
        ○On the sampling strategy: I note that all migrants interviewed for the purpose of this study
         are workers mainly in the service sectors, with the exception of construction. Was this
         deliberate and if so, why? While not a majority, there are Polish migrants across many
         sectors in the UK with different levels of qualifications. Indeed, this impacts experience and
         I find that the paper would benefit from clarifying why there’s a particular focus on those in
         particular sectors.
        ○Overall: The paper would benefit from a tighter edit.
    Overall this is article is very promising! The data collected appears to be rich and informative and
    may produce several papers as a result. I look forward to its final iteration and congratulate the
    author for this contribution.
