       Kate Golebiowska
       Charles Darwin University, Darwin, Australia

       Thank you for the opportunity to review the manuscript “Enacting Semi-Periphery: Conditions of
       Post-Accession Migration from Poland to the UK.” The paper considers the impact of Poland’s
       accession to the EU on Polish migration to the UK in the context of the EU’s free movement
       regime. Using the World-Systems Analysis and the concepts of biopower and biopolitics, the
       Author proposes that structural pressures have relegated Polish migrants to a peripheral position
       within the EU single market. Below, I am recommending revisions to improve the structure of the
       paper and to better integrate and use theory and evidence to contribute to the debates on
       immigration, power, and the EU labour market. I commend the Author for their efforts to draw
       connections between the macro structures and the experiences of Polish migrants in the UK.

       Introduction. The manuscript opens with general statements such as “The transformations in
       Poland linked to the largest EU enlargement have contributed to a significant increase in the
       cross-European mobility of goods, capital, services and people” and “This has (…) resulted in the
       migration of a sizeable number of Poles to the UK in particular.” Numerical evidence to
       substantiate these statements is needed. Replacing general terms like “significant increase” with
       figures comparing pre- and post-accession migration flows will ground these assertions.
       The second paragraph offers a general statement that “(…) the increasing phenomenon of
       unequal labour practices in the UK affects EU citizens, among others, whereby Polish nationals
       represent the most common group subject to various forms of modern slavery.” This claim
       requires empirical support. The Author may use official statistics or other authoritative data to
       support it.
       The Introduction shifts between the EU labour market and the UK, though the paper’s title
       suggests a focus on the UK. The Author may address this by rewriting the Introduction and
       showing that the EU labour market opportunities and conditions serve as a broad background for
       considering the case of Poles in the UK’s labour market. Additionally, this section will benefit from:
       -Clarifying what is the period covered by “post-accession” migration;
       -Expressly recognising that Polish migrants were present in the UK prior to accession;

       Analytical framework. While the WSA framework is reasonably well laid out in its classical form,

    characteristics of the core, semi-peripheral and peripheral states in the context of the EU labour
    market are missing. When incorporated and supported with concrete examples and references,
    the analytical framework will gain more academic rigour. For example, on p. 3. the Author
    mentions ‘high unemployment rates in Poland’ that fuelled their intra-EU mobility for employment,
    but the reader is left wondering what these rates were and when, and why the scale of vacancies
    in the UK was a motivating factor to move. What was the unemployment rate in the UK in the
    period under review and in the industries where Poles found employment? Another point relates
    to the notion of productive power. If it is intended as an analytical framework, it should be
    included in the abstract.
    Sampling strategy and criteria. This section will benefit from further elaboration. Please explain
    the rationale for choosing the specific industries of employment of the participants. If hospitality
    and agriculture are cited as the most common sectors for Polish migrants, supporting this claim
    with labour market data will strengthen its validity. Additionally, clarifying when the interviews
    were conducted (year) will help readers understand the participants’ length of stay in the UK.

    Polish migrant as targets of power. This section blurs the boundaries between literature review,
    analytical framework and results and is difficult to follow. Concepts like ‘biopower’ and ‘biopolitics’
    are introduced here but belong in the analytical framework. I recommend reorganising the paper
    so that all concepts are introduced and defined in the ‘Analytical framework’ section. This will
    improve the clarity and logical flow of the paper.
    The Author introduces Marx’s concept of ‘class’ (p. 7) here, further confusing the reader. I am not
    convinced that the Author is well-versed in the Marxian concept of ‘class’; it is hardly explained in
    one passage on p. 7 and referenced on p.8. If the Author chooses to retain it, a fuller explanation
    and integration with the WSA framework (especially Wallerstein’s categories) is needed.
    Otherwise, the concept can be omitted, and efforts should be directed toward developing a more
    focused, in-depth analysis (pp. 7-8).
    The closing paragraph on. p.8 is a moment where concepts and data should intersect
    meaningfully, but the interpretation is superficial and underdeveloped. For example, biopower
    and biopolitics are briefly mentioned there but not sufficiently applied to the empirical material.
    Expanding this section to show how these frameworks help explain participants’ experiences will
    enhance analytical depth. The citation of Massey et al. (1993) can be enriched by drawing on
    Okólski and Salt (2014), which is already cited elsewhere and would offer more contextual nuance.

    Conclusion: This section is again confusing. It includes elements that should be moved to the data
    section, such as new examples (see P31) and the accompanying considerations. The last
    paragraph introduces a new topic: the Brexit-related emotional effects, which, in my view, fall
    outside the paper’s scope and can removed. This will create space to develop a more profound
    analysis using the conceptual framework.

    The Conclusion will benefit from a style review, too. The opening sentence is too long (it makes up
    the entire opening paragraph), and the word “focusing” is repeated in the first line. The second
    paragraph needs specific examples and a more detailed discussion earlier to make sense.
    Specifically, the claim about how the core-periphery system produces “population inequalities”
    requires demonstration earlier in the analysis.

    I hope the Author receives this feedback in good faith and considers the suggested revisions to
    develop a sharper paper. Best wishes.
