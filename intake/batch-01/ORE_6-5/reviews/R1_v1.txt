       This article offers a clear and policy-relevant narrative review of the emerging literature on the
       EU’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). The proposed “trilemma” framework provides
       a useful heuristic for organizing ongoing debates around competitiveness, climate justice, and
       international policy coordination. That said, I have several suggestions that I believe would
       strengthen the conceptual precision and analytical completeness of the article, particularly given
       its aim to provide a comprehensive review of CBAM design options.

       1. Clarifying the attribution of policy objectives

       One point that would benefit from further clarification concerns the attribution of policy
       objectives. Throughout the article, the three elements of the “trilemma” are presented as
       objectives of the CBAM itself. Conceptually, however, it may be more precise to distinguish
       between:

       CBAM as a policy instrument, whose primary and legally explicit objective is to address carbon
       leakage and maintain a level playing field; and

       Broader EU climate and trade objectives, such as advancing climate justice or fostering a de facto
       climate club, which shape the political context and design choices surrounding CBAM.

       Reframing the trilemma as reflecting EU policy objectives in the implementation of CBAM, rather
       than intrinsic objectives of CBAM as such, would improve conceptual clarity without undermining
       the core analytical structure of the paper. This distinction could be reflected in the title, abstract,
       and selected passages in Sections 2 and 3.

    2. Broadening the framing of the “climate justice” objective

    The discussion of climate justice is thoughtful and well grounded in normative literature,
    particularly through the isolationist versus integrationist distinction. At the same time, the framing
    may appear somewhat narrow relative to the policy concerns of many low- and lower-middle-
    income trading partners.

    Beyond distributive justice considerations in a strict normative sense, concerns about trade
    continuity, political acceptability, and development trajectories play a central role in shaping third-
    country responses to CBAM. The analysis could be strengthened by explicitly acknowledging that
    what is framed as a “justice” objective also encompasses broader political economy considerations
    related to sustaining economic participation in EU-centered value chains.

    3. Engaging with credit-based or offset-linked design proposals

    To further enhance the comprehensiveness of the review, I encourage the author to engage with
    emerging proposals that conceptualize credit-based approaches as an alternative way of
    navigating the CBAM trilemma. In particular, recent literature has explored the possibility of
    allowing exporters from developing countries to retire high-integrity carbon credits (for example,
    under Article 6 mechanisms) in order to qualify these payments as a “carbon price effectively paid”
    for CBAM purposes.

    Such approaches differ analytically from the three design paths discussed in Section 4, as they
    potentially combine carbon price equivalence with climate finance generation, while avoiding
    explicit exemptions or reliance on external aid channels. Even if the author ultimately finds these
    proposals normatively or practically limited, briefly situating them within the comparative
    landscape would strengthen the article’s claim to provide a comprehensive review of current
    CBAM design debates.

    4. Minor suggestions

    The comparative discussion in Section 4 could benefit from more explicit articulation of the
    conditions under which one design approach might dominate another (e.g., assumptions about
    carbon leakage risks or fiscal capacity).

    A short concluding paragraph reflecting on how the trilemma might evolve as CBAM coverage
    expands or as more countries adopt domestic carbon pricing could further enhance the forward-
    looking relevance of the paper.

    As a minor technical point, I noticed that one reference is cited as “(Ziyue, 2025)”. In this case,
    “Wang” appears to be the author’s family name, with “Ziyue” being the given name. It may
    therefore be more appropriate to cite this work as “(Wang, 2025)” for consistency with standard
    referencing conventions.
