       Thank you very much for your valuable suggestions, which have helped me greatly in
       working on a second version of the article. Please find below a point-by-point response to
       your comments. Please note that, as some of the issues raised in reports 1 and 2 are similar,
       some of the responses below are repeated in my responses to report 1 and vice versa. This
       is unavoidable as I am not allowed to provide a general response to the two reports
       received so far; I can only respond to them individually.

       Point 1. In the new version, I have clarified the policy objectives of the CBAM as you
       suggested. Throughout the new version, I refer to EU policy goals in the implementation of
       the CBAM rather than CBAM objectives. I have also clarified that the explicit objective of the
       CBAM regulation is carbon price equivalence, and that the other goals mainly derive from
       the EU and its Member States' other international commitments.

       Point 2. I have expanded the concept of climate justice. In the new version, I begin by
       distinguishing between isolationist and integrationist methodologies in relation to climate
       justice. I then argue that a way to make both methodologies less vague and more
       operational would be to state that the EU's CBAM is inequitable if it poses a significant
       obstacle to low- and lower-middle-income countries trading with the EU, and if the
       development of these countries depends on this trade. I emphasised how this reflects the
       concrete concerns of trading partners in the Global South. I also addressed the issue of the
       international political acceptability of the CBAM.

       Point 3. I have added and discussed the credit-based approach as a fourth method of
       navigating the EU's CBAM. This is based on Sandler & Schrag (2025). This makes the
       literature review proposed by the article more comprehensive and policy-guiding. Thank
