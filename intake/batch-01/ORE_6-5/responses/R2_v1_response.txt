      Thank you very much for your report. It was extremely helpful in guiding me through the
      process of creating a second version of the article. In the new version, I have addressed all
      of the issues you raised. Specifically, I have made the changes outlined below. Please note
      that, as some of the issues raised in reports 1 and 2 are similar, some of the responses
      below are repeated in my responses to report 2 and vice versa. This is unavoidable as I am
      not allowed to provide a general response to the two reports received so far; I can only
      respond to them individually.

      Point 1. Throughout the new version, I refer to EU policy goals in the implementation of the
      CBAM rather than to CBAM objectives. I also clarify that the explicit objective of the CBAM
      regulation is carbon price equivalence, with the other goals mainly deriving from the EU and
      its Member States' other international commitments.

      Point 2. I have added and discussed the credit-based approach as a fourth method of
      navigating the EU's CBAM. This is based on Sandler & Schrag (2025). This makes the
      literature review proposed by the article more comprehensive and policy-guiding. Thank
      you so much for this.

      Point 3. I have expanded the concept of climate justice. However, I have not abandoned the
      more abstract formulation. I have decided to retain both formulations. I started from the
      distinction between isolationist and integrationist methodologies in relation to climate
      justice. I then argued that a way to make both methodologies less vague and more
      operational would be to state that the EU's CBAM is inequitable if it poses a significant
      obstacle to low- and lower-middle-income countries trading with the EU and if legitimate
      expectations of development in these countries depend on this trade. I emphasised how
      this reflects the concrete concerns of trading partners in the Global South. I also addressed
      the issue of the international political acceptability of the CBAM. Taking all this into account,
      I have chosen to retain the term 'climate justice goal' to emphasise the connection between
      the empirical and normative aspects of the article.
