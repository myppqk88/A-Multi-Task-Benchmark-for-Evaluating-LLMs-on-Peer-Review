REVISED Amendments from Version 1
The second version of the article introduces five key changes compared to the previous version. Firstly, it provides a more
precise characterisation of the EU’s policy goals that give rise to the CBAM policy trilemma. It clarifies that the EU’s primary
objective in introducing the CBAM is to establish carbon price equivalence across borders. Meanwhile, extending the scope
of the de facto climate club and implementing a collective climate mitigation effort based on equity considerations derive
from broader international commitments of the EU and its Member States. Secondly, the new version expands the range of
possible approaches to navigating the EU’s CBAM policy trilemma to include the credit-based approach recently proposed
in the literature. This approach involves allowing foreign operators to offset their CBAM liabilities with certified carbon
credits. Thirdly, the new version suggests a way of operationalising the climate justice goal in terms of trade continuity. In
doing so, it acknowledges the practical concerns raised by the Global South with respect to the EU’s CBAM. Fourthly, it takes
into account developments in policymaking and academic literature since the first version was published. Finally, it briefly
discusses potential future developments in the EU’s CBAM policy trilemma.
