Comment
: Abstract: The abstract provides a broad overview of the field but does not clearly articulate the novel contributions of this study. It should explicitly state how the bibliometric analysis advances current knowledge beyond existing reviews in the field. The claim about "important research gaps" is vague. Specify which gaps exist and how this study addresses them with new findings. Instead of just stating that ML is growing in risk management, emphasize the emerging trends and their practical implications. Phrases like "growing interest in ML for risk management" or "cross-cutting nature of ML applications" should be replaced with quantifiable trends, e.g., "Between 2018 and 2023, ML-related publications in risk management increased by X%."

: The abstract was improved, specifying gaps, emerging trends, and including quantifiable data as recommended.

Comment
: Methods
Bibliometric methodology lacks justification. The authors state they used the PRISMA 2020 framework but do not justify why this specific bibliometric method was chosen over others (e.g., systematic mapping studies). Also. With the sentence ´ “Logistic regression" is a classic statistical method that has been widely used in various fields, but with the advancement of more complex machine learning techniques, its use in risk management may have decreased, cite Chandrasekar V et al. [2025 (Ref-1)] https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhazmat.2024.137071 to make bibliography up to date. Explain why the inclusion/exclusion criteria were structured this way and how they ensure robustness in capturing relevant literature. The search strategy in Scopus and Web of Science is mentioned, but no actual search queries or filters are provided. Authors should provide a supplementary table listing keywords, Boolean operators, and filters used. Bias assessment is superficial. The discussion on selection bias, publication bias, and potential missing studies should be expanded. The authors mention automated classifiers, but how were these validated? Overreliance on bibliometrics without qualitative insights. While bibliometric analysis is useful, the paper lacks in-depth discussions on real-world applications, challenges, and limitations of ML in risk management. A hybrid methodology combining bibliometrics with expert interviews or case studies would have strengthened this work.
Response
: Methodology was improved, adding a limitation on the lack of qualitative methods.
