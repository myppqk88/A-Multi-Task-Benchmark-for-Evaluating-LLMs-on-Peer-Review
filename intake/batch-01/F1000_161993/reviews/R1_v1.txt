The paper provides a valuable bibliometric analysis of machine learning applications in risk management, highlighting key trends, emerging topics, and leading contributors, reinforcing the growing importance of AI-driven approaches in risk assessment. However, the study requires
major revisions
to enhance
methodological transparency, critical discussion of real-world challenges, regulatory considerations, and actionable insights
, ensuring its findings contribute meaningfully to both academia and industry. My comments are given below-
Abstract:
The abstract provides a broad overview of the field but does not clearly articulate the novel contributions of this study. It should explicitly state how the bibliometric analysis advances current knowledge beyond existing reviews in the field. The claim about "important research gaps" is vague. Specify which gaps exist and how this study addresses them with new findings. Instead of just stating that ML is growing in risk management, emphasize the emerging trends and their practical implications. Phrases like "growing interest in ML for risk management" or "cross-cutting nature of ML applications" should be replaced with quantifiable trends, e.g., "Between 2018 and 2023, ML-related publications in risk management increased by X%."
Introduction
Need for a stronger problem statement
- The introduction does not effectively frame the specific research challenges in applying ML to risk management. A well-structured introduction should highlight why existing ML applications in risk management face limitations. Define specific obstacles such as interpretability, bias, regulatory concerns, and data limitations. Show how this study uniquely contributes to bridging these gaps
Literature review lacks depth-
While some ML applications in risk management are mentioned (e.g., supply chain, healthcare, finance), there is no critical evaluation of previous works. The authors should incorporate a comparative analysis of existing systematic reviews on this topic to clarify what is missing and what this study adds.
Ambiguous transition from traditional to AI-driven risk management
- The text should clarify how ML surpasses conventional risk assessment models, providing examples of cases where ML outperforms traditional methods.
Methods
Bibliometric methodology lacks justification. The authors state they used the PRISMA 2020 framework but do not justify why this specific bibliometric method was chosen over others (e.g., systematic mapping studies). Also. With the sentence ´ “Logistic regression" is a classic statistical method that has been widely used in various fields, but with the advancement of more complex machine learning techniques, its use in risk management may have decreased, cite Chandrasekar V et al. [2025 (Ref-1)]
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhazmat.2024.137071
to make bibliography up to date. Explain why the inclusion/exclusion criteria were structured this way and how they ensure robustness in capturing relevant literature. The search strategy in Scopus and Web of Science is mentioned, but no actual search queries or filters are provided. Authors should provide a supplementary table listing keywords, Boolean operators, and filters used. Bias assessment is superficial. The discussion on selection bias, publication bias, and potential missing studies should be expanded. The authors mention automated classifiers, but how were these validated? Overreliance on bibliometrics without qualitative insights. While bibliometric analysis is useful, the paper lacks in-depth discussions on real-world applications, challenges, and limitations of ML in risk management. A hybrid methodology combining bibliometrics with expert interviews or case studies would have strengthened this work.
Results & Discussion
Limited discussion of emerging methodologies-
The study highlights deep learning and feature selection but fails to discuss newer paradigms, such as: Explainable AI (XAI) in risk management. Self-supervised learning and federated learning for improving ML robustness. Causal inference models for better decision-making in risk contexts.
Overemphasis on publication trends without actionable insights
- The results section predominantly focuses on publication growth trends but lacks insights into what these trends mean for the field. Instead of just listing “most productive countries” or “most cited papers,” authors should: Identify key breakthroughs in ML for risk management over time. Highlight specific challenges (e.g., explainability, fairness, regulatory concerns) in high-impact ML applications. Cite Singh AV et al. [2024 (Ref-2)]
https://doi.org/10.3389/ftox.2024.1461587
with the sentence ´deep learning’ enables a deeper representation of data, which has led to significant advances in risk analysis and prediction in various fields such as medicine´
Country-specific trends should be contextualized
- The paper states that China, South Korea, and the U.S. lead ML research in risk management, but does not analyze why. A deeper dive into policy, funding, and institutional strengths in these countries would add significant value.
Superficial discussion of thematic clusters
- The clustering analysis is presented without critical evaluation. Instead of just listing keyword groups, the authors should: Explain why certain themes emerged together. Provide real-world examples of these clusters (e.g., case studies where ML has successfully improved risk assessment).
Weak connection between results and real-world impact
- The discussion does not sufficiently address how the identified trends translate into practical risk management applications. For example, how does the rise of deep learning address current gaps in financial or medical risk assessment?
Lack of discussion on ethical and regulatory challenges-
Regulatory barriers to ML adoption in risk management (e.g., GDPR, explainability requirements in finance) should be included. Issues related to bias, fairness, and model interpretability in risk-sensitive areas (e.g., healthcare, finance) should be discussed in greater depth.
Missing discussion on data limitations
- The paper does not consider the challenges of data availability, quality, and bias in ML-based risk assessments. Many ML models suffer from biased training data, leading to inaccurate predictions—this crucial issue is overlooked. With the sentence ´It is also important to explore how the machine learning approaches used for Covid -19 can be applied to other infectious diseases, and how they can be adapted to address future health crises´, cite Barghash RF et al. [2024 (Ref-3)]
https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules29235564
a recent report to support the statement.
Superficial treatment of research gaps-
The identified gaps (e.g., lack of studies in certain domains) are stated but not critically examined. The paper should propose concrete future research directions, such as: Developing hybrid models that combine ML with traditional statistical risk models. Improving interpretability techniques in ML risk models. Addressing geographical disparities in ML adoption for risk management.
