Title:
Machine Learning Applications in Risk Management: Trends and Research Agenda
Authors:
Valencia-Arias A, Jimenez Garcia JA, Agudelo-Ceballos E et al.
Journal:
F1000Research (2025, 14:233)
DOI:
https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.161993.1
1. Summary of the Article
This study presents a
bibliometric analysis
on the application of
machine learning (ML) in risk management
, focusing on research trends, influential authors, top contributing countries, thematic clusters, and emerging keywords. The authors utilized the
PRISMA-2020
framework and retrieved data from
Scopus and Web of Science
.
Key Findings:
Growing Interest:
There has been a substantial increase in research publications on ML in risk management, particularly from 2020 to 2023, indicating the rising importance of this field.
Geographical Distribution:
The
United States, China, and South Korea
are leading contributors to research output.
Thematic Evolution:
The field has evolved from early topics like
stacking
to emerging applications in
urban trees
,
Covid-19 risk management
, and
XGBoost models
.
Methodological Advances:
Core ML techniques such as
Random Forest, SVM, Deep Learning, Feature Selection
, and
Big Data
have gained prominence.
Emerging Topics:
Keywords such as
prediction, postpartum depression, security, and big data
indicate new frontiers in ML-based risk management.
The authors also present a
research agenda
, highlighting areas for further exploration, including
cross-disciplinary ML integration, ethical considerations, and domain-specific risk modeling
.
2. General Assessment
This article is
a well-structured bibliometric study
with a clear focus on the application of ML in risk management. The methodology follows established bibliometric practices, and the discussion provides
insightful interpretations
of trends. However,
some areas require improvement
to enhance scientific rigor, clarity, and completeness.

3.2 Methodology
Strengths:
✅
PRISMA-2020 Framework:
The study adheres to PRISMA guidelines for systematic reviews, ensuring methodological rigor.
✅
Database Selection:
Scopus & Web of Science are reputable sources for bibliometric research.
✅
Search Strategy:
The search formulas are appropriate, targeting
risk management and ML-related keywords
.
Areas for Improvement:
❌
Data Extraction & Preprocessing:
The paper does not discuss potential
data biases
(e.g., missing articles due to indexing limitations).
The
data cleaning process
(e.g., removal of duplicates, handling of missing metadata) should be explicitly stated.
❌
Quantitative Indicators:
The authors use
basic bibliometric measures (e.g., publication count, citation count)
.
Advanced
scientometric techniques
like
h-index trends, impact factor analysis, and co-citation mapping
would strengthen the results.
Suggested Enhancements:
✔
Clarify Data Cleaning Steps
: Describe how duplicate records, irrelevant studies, and indexing errors were managed.
✔
Use Additional Bibliometric Indicators
: Include
h-index trends, impact factor, and author impact score
for deeper insights.
✔
Consider Topic Modeling
: Using
Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)
for automated topic extraction would provide an alternative view of thematic evolution.
3.3 Results & Discussion
Strengths:
✅ The findings are well-organized into
publication trends, author contributions, thematic clusters, and keyword evolution
.
✅ The figures (Figures 2–9) provide clear visualizations of key insights.
✅ The discussion effectively
connects bibliometric results with practical applications
in risk management.
Areas for Improvement:
❌
Comparative Analysis Missing:
The study does not compare findings with
existing bibliometric reviews on ML in risk management
.
A
benchmarking analysis
against similar studies could add more context.
❌
Potential Bias in Geographical Analysis:
The study states that China, South Korea, and the U.S. dominate the field but
does not discuss potential biases
, such as language-based indexing preferences (English vs. non-English sources).
Suggested Enhancements:
✔
Include a Literature Comparison Table:
Compare this study’s findings with prior bibliometric studies.
✔
Discuss Possible Indexing Biases:
Consider how language limitations may influence the geographical distribution of publications.
3.4 Research Agenda & Future Directions
Strengths:
✅ The research agenda is
comprehensive and well-structured
.
✅ The paper identifies
four key research gaps
:
Lack of domain-specific ML adaptation
Geographical disparities in ML applications
Need for interdisciplinary risk models
Integration of ML with traditional risk management frameworks
Areas for Improvement:
❌
Insufficient Ethical Considerations:
There is
no discussion of ethical implications
, such as ML bias, fairness, and explainability in risk management.
❌
Regulatory & Industry Adoption Challenges Not Addressed:
The study does not explore
real-world adoption barriers
, such as
data privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA)
and
ML interpretability issues in high-stakes domains (e.g., finance, healthcare)
.
Suggested Enhancements:
✔
Include a Section on ML Ethics:
Discuss potential
biases, transparency challenges, and fairness considerations
.
✔
Explore Regulatory & Adoption Challenges:
Address
legal frameworks, industry regulations, and practical barriers to ML deployment
in risk management.
