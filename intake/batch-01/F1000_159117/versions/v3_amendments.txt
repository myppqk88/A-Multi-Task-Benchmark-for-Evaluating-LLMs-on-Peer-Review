Revised Amendments from Version 2
Compared to the previously published version, this revised article incorporates substantial improvements in both structure and academic rigor, in direct response to the reviewers' recommendations. A key enhancement is the inclusion of a dedicated theoretical framework section, which thoroughly examines the principal conceptual and theoretical approaches within gender studies in Latin America, such as feminist, intersectional, and critical perspectives on neoliberalism. This addition contextualizes the bibliometric findings more effectively and strengthens the overall analytical depth of the study.
The methodology section, although already robust and aligned with PRISMA-2020 guidelines, has been clarified further by acknowledging the limitation of using only the term “Latin America” in the search strategy. This recognition underscores potential biases in country-level representation and enhances the transparency of the research design.
The conclusions section has also been revised to include a more explicit discussion of the study’s limitations and to incorporate a clearer articulation of the theoretical and practical implications. These are now presented in separate subsections. The theoretical implications explore how the findings contribute to the consolidation and critique of dominant frameworks in gender research, while the practical implications highlight potential applications in public policy, educational programming, and regional research agendas.
Finally, the section on research gaps has been refined by removing the superficial paragraph that followed the table and replacing it with a more nuanced discussion organized into three dimensions: thematic, geographic, and interdisciplinary gaps. This approach offers a clearer roadmap for future studies.
