Comment:
It is recommended to include a broader theoretical background section that contextualizes the study within the Latin American literature on university entrepreneurial behavior. This would highlight the gaps addressed by the research and strengthen its theoretical contribution.
Response:
Thank you very much for your comment. In response to the reviewer’s recommendation, an expanded section on theoretical background has been incorporated to contextualize the study within the Latin American literature on University Entrepreneurial Behavior (UEB).
This section synthesizes the main theories and quantitative findings developed in the region over the past 15 years, highlighting the predominance of the Theory of Planned Behavior (Ajzen, 1991) and the incorporation of competency-based approaches (Fernández-Pérez et al., 2019). It also analyzes cross-national comparative studies that integrate psychological, cognitive, and contextual variables in countries such as Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Peru, and Ecuador.
Furthermore, it identifies existing gaps in the regional literature, such as the scarcity of longitudinal studies, the limited integration among psychological, cognitive, and contextual factors, the unequal geographical coverage, and the weak articulation between micro-level (individual cognition) and macro-level (institutional context) analyses.
The section argues for the theoretical contribution of the study by proposing a model that integrates these factors within a causal and comparative framework for Latin America, thereby strengthening the understanding of university entrepreneurial behavior from a regional and updated perspective.
Comment:
The sample size and selection should be explained in greater detail, including the justification for the number of participants per country, the voluntary sampling criterion, and potential limitations of representativeness. Including a statistical power analysis or a theoretical reference would strengthen methodological validity
.
Response:
We appreciate the reviewer’s comment regarding the sample size and selection. The study employed a voluntary, purposive sampling strategy, coordinated with accredited universities in five Latin American countries, to ensure diversity among participants in terms of gender, academic level, and institutional background.
The achieved sample sizes per country (Mexico = 168, Peru = 100, Ecuador = 147, Paraguay = 84, Colombia = 41; total N = 541) exceed the minimum thresholds recommended for cross-cultural comparative analyses (Field, 2013). To verify empirical adequacy, a post hoc power analysis was conducted for an omnibus comparison equivalent to the Kruskal–Wallis test (five groups, α = .05, medium effect f = 0.25 ≈ η² = 0.06; Cohen, 1988). The resulting statistical power was 0.97, indicating that the total sample was more than sufficient to detect effects of medium magnitude. Consistently, the descriptive results in Appendix A show low standard errors (SE ≤ 0.14) and small to moderate effect sizes (ω² and ε²), supporting the precision and stability of the estimates.
Although voluntary sampling limits strict representativeness, diversity was maximized by recruiting from multiple ACBSP-accredited universities in each country. This approach aligns with methodological standards in cross-national research on entrepreneurial education (Fayolle & Gailly, 2015; López et al., 2021).
