       The paper tackles a compelling and timely topic by focussing on a theoretic specification of how
       parties may employ crowdsourcing to enhance intra-party but also beyond-party democracy. The
       paper is well structured and well informed.
       My understanding is that the paper aims to target a more general audience in party research and
       therefore I suggest a connection with Harmel and Janda (1994) in terms of their party goal of
       democracy maximation and the multispeed-membership model by Scarrow (2015). This would
       enable the scholarly community to learn more about the specific membership types that might
       contribute to party activities via crowdsourcing.
       Regarding the demographic nature of participants of crowdsourcing, the paper refers to Albrecht
       (2006). A lot has happened ever since, especially the founding of the parties mentioned in the
       paper (Podemos, M5S) but also the advancements in the digital sphere have evolved immensely.
       Therefore, I suggest including the work of Vromen and colleagues (2022) which provides more
       recent information.

    German movement Aufstehen. Aufstehen is a political organization, not a party according to the
    German Party Law §2. Therefore, this statement requires revision for accuracy. Aufstehen marked
    Sahra Wagenknecht’s political break from Die Linke (The Left Party). It was subsequently
    succeeded by the founding of BSW (Bündnis Sahra Wagenknecht), which began as a political
    association before being formally registered as a political party and later contesting elections
    (sources below).

    The paper points to different chances and challenges of crowdsourcing for parties. Reading the
    challenge section, I found myself reflecting on whether crowdsourcing might be more effectively
    applied to certain types of issues — perhaps more suited to valence issues than position issues.
    This consideration seems particularly relevant in the context of the example discussed in the
    paper (migration policy). I would therefore gently suggest integrating insights from this broader
    literature into the paper’s argument, which could help offer a more nuanced and comprehensive
    perspective on the topic.
    Overall, the paper is very inspiring. Despite the few requests that in my opinion would strengthen
    the relevance of this article in different areas of party research, I find no compelling reason to
    recommend against publication — the article is well-argued, timely, and worthy of consideration.
