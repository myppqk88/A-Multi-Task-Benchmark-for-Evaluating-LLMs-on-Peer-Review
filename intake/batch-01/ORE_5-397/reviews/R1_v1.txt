       Michal Malý
       Charles University, Prague, Czech Republic

       The manuscript tackles a timely, highly relevant topic and presents its argument clearly,
       coherently, and in a well-structured manner. I found the conceptual discussion
       persuasive—especially the positioning of digital crowdsourcing as a comparatively “lightweight”
       participatory tool when contrasted with more demanding deliberative procedures and all-in-one
       party platforms, and the proposed typology is analytically useful and easy to operationalise across
       different empirical contexts.
       I only have 3 short comments to consider:
            1. In the section “Digital crowdsourcing in public institutions and political parties,” where the
               author briefly lists illustrative examples, I would recommend adding Swarmwise (Falkvinge
               2013) as an additional reference, because it explicitly frames the organisational logic of
               early Pirate parties through crowdsourcing/swarm-like participation and therefore fits well
               alongside the other examples the author provides.
            2. While the manuscript notes that parties adopt these tools in pursuit of party goals, I would
               encourage the author to anchor this point more explicitly in the party change literature,
               particularly Harmel and Janda (1994), to better specify how strategic incentives, adaptation
               pressures, and organisational objectives intersect with (and sometimes override)
               democratic-participatory rationales.
            3. I would suggest adding one explicit, instrumentally oriented motivation that is often central
               in practice but is only implicit here: parties may deploy crowdsourcing not only to generate
               input or signal openness, but also to harvest and consolidate participant contacts
               (supporter lists, mailing lists, mobilization channels), which can then be leveraged for
               continuous communication about party activities, campaigns, and organizational matters;
               acknowledging this mechanism would (in my opinion) further sharpen the paper’s
               discussion of strategic uses and potential co-optation of participatory design.
       The manuscript is well argued, conceptually clear, and addresses a timely and important topic;
       once the above points are taken into account, I see no reason not to recommend the article for
       acceptance.
