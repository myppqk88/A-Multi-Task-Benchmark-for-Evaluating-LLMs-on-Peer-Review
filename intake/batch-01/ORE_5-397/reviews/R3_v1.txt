       This is a thoughtful article which makes an innovative intervention in a growing area of
       scholarship about political parties: new forms of intra-party democracy and digital media.
       Specifically the focus is on practices of crowd-sourcing, as bottom-up channels by means of which
       poltical parties may be able to tap into people's knowledge and preferences, thus overcoming
       some of the distance from the citizenry which currently results in very low approval rates. What is
       proposed is a theoretical discussion of how digital crowdsourcing may provide a way to move out
       some of the present impasses, also seen in recent political phenomena, that while integrating
       digtal media and deliberative practices are caught in self-defeating contradictions. I find the
       reconstruction of the debate clear and knowledgeable, and most importantly the typology of

    different digital crowd-sourcing practices very useful for analytical and orientational purposes.
    Similarly useful is the delineation of different challenges which party organisers would need to
    take into account in order to make the most of digital crowd-sourcing. I think that give the nature
    of the article as an exploratory contributon, there is no need for empirical support, besides the
    few examples that are already cited and that a more robust empirical analysis may be best suited
    to ensuing contributions within this research agenda. Otherwise, the article is very well structured,
    convincingly argued, and well written, and it seems to me to be ready for publication as it stands.
    One possible criticism would be that the conclusion as it is very short and one may have done
    without it, perhaps expanding a bit more the discussion part and turning it into a discussion and
    conclusion section. But more substantially I do not see the need for any significant revision as the
    article stands already well on its feet and makes a useful contribution to debates.
