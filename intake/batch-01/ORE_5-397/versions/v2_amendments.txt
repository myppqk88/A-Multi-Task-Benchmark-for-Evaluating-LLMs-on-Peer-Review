REVISED Amendments from Version 1
This revised version incorporates the reviewers’ suggestions through several changes.
First, the paper now more clearly situates its argument within the literature on party change. References to Harmel and
Janda’s framework have been integrated at multiple points in the manuscript, including the beginning of the theoretical
section and the conclusion. The conclusion has also been expanded to provide a broader reflection on how parties’ primary
goals (vote-, office-, policy-, or intra-party democracy–seeking) and external shocks may influence the adoption of participatory innovations such as digital crowdsourcing.
Second, the manuscript now engages more directly with Susan Scarrow’s work on party organization and membership,
particularly the “multi-speed membership” model. This perspective has been introduced in the introduction and further
developed in the theoretical section to explain how digital crowdsourcing may lower barriers to participation and involve
different categories of supporters beyond formal party members.
Third, the section discussing the participatory dynamics and risks of digital crowdsourcing has been updated. In particular,
recent research by Vromen, Halpin, and Vaughan (2022) has been incorporated to provide a more nuanced discussion of
the demographic composition of online participants and the potential implications for representativeness and participatory
inequality.
Fourth, some examples have been added. The discussion of crowdsourcing practices now includes additional cases from
Pirate parties (including the Swedish Pirate Party and the UK Policy Beta platform). A factual correction has also been made
regarding the German political movement Aufstehen, including a clarifying note on its organizational status.
Finally, the section on the practical implementation of crowdsourcing has been expanded to discuss the distinction between
valence and position issues, suggesting that crowdsourcing processes may be more feasible when initially applied to
valence issues. The discussion of potential manipulation from above has also been further developed by mentioning the
party use of users’ data.
Any further responses from the reviewers can be found at the end of the article
