       Summary:
       The paper presents findings from a study conducted in Lebanon as part of the EU Horizon-funded
       project ADMIGOV, focusing on Syrian refugees' experiences in the country. The study employs a
       mixed-method approach, combining qualitative and quantitative data collection methods to shed
       light on the challenges faced by Syrian refugees living in informal settlements. While the paper
       offers valuable insights into the difficulties encountered by Syrian refugees in Lebanon and
       discusses the shortcomings of existing aid interventions, it falls short in exploring new
       intersections in the lived experiences of Syrian refugees and lacks an intersectional approach.

       Strengths:
           1. Solid Methodology: The paper demonstrates a robust methodological framework,
              incorporating a mixed-method approach that combines qualitative and quantitative data
              collection methods. The extensive fieldwork and immersion in the community contribute to
              the credibility of the study's findings.
           2. Relevance: The topic of Syrian refugees' experiences in Lebanon is timely and relevant,
              given the ongoing crisis in Syria and the challenges faced by displaced populations in the
              region. The paper addresses an important issue and offers valuable insights for
              policymakers, practitioners, and researchers working in the field of forced migration and
              humanitarian responses.
           3. Practical Implications: The findings of the study have practical implications for
              humanitarian actors and policymakers, highlighting the inadequacies of existing aid
              interventions and the need for more effective governance mechanisms to address the
              challenges faced by Syrian refugees in Lebanon.
       Weaknesses:
           1. Lack of Exploration of New Intersections: While the paper provides a comprehensive
              overview of the challenges faced by Syrian refugees in Lebanon, it does not explore any new
              intersections or phenomena in their lived experiences. The findings seem to reiterate

           existing knowledge without offering any novel insights into the complexities of Syrian
           refugees' experiences in Lebanon.
        2. Absence of Intersectional Approach: The paper lacks an intersectional approach, as the
           findings are presented in a rather generalized manner, and the population of Syrian
           refugees is homogenized. There is a missed opportunity to explore the intersecting factors
           that shape the experiences of different subgroups within the Syrian refugee population,
           such as gender, age, socioeconomic status, and educational background.
        3. Qualitative Findings Need Further Development: While the qualitative findings provide
           valuable insights into the challenges faced by Syrian refugees in Lebanon, they could be
           further developed to offer more nuanced and 'new' approaches to the research question.
           The paper primarily focuses on highlighting challenges matter-of-factly without delving into
           the underlying dynamics and complexities of the refugee experience.
    Recommendations:
        1. Develop New Intersections: The authors should endeavor to explore new intersections in
           the lived experiences of Syrian refugees in Lebanon, moving beyond existing knowledge to
           uncover novel insights into the complexities of displacement and forced migration. This
           could involve considering factors such as gender, age, socioeconomic status, and
           educational background and examining how these intersecting identities shape refugees'
           experiences and outcomes.
        2. Adopt an Intersectional Approach: The paper would benefit from adopting an
           intersectional approach to analyze the findings, recognizing the diversity within the Syrian
           refugee population and exploring how intersecting factors influence their experiences and
           vulnerabilities. This would provide a more nuanced understanding of the challenges faced
           by different subgroups within the refugee population.
        3. Use Qualitative Findings to Develop Nuanced Approaches: The authors should utilize the
           qualitative findings to develop more nuanced and 'new' approaches to the research
           question, moving beyond merely highlighting challenges to uncovering the underlying
           dynamics and complexities of the refugee experience. This could involve conducting further
           qualitative analysis to explore themes and patterns in greater depth and developing
           recommendations based on these insights.
    Overall, while the paper presents a solid analysis of the challenges faced by Syrian refugees in
    Lebanon, there is room for improvement in terms of exploring new intersections, adopting an
    intersectional approach, and using qualitative findings to develop more nuanced approaches to
    the research question. With these revisions, the paper has the potential to make a significant
    contribution to the field of forced migration and humanitarian responses.
