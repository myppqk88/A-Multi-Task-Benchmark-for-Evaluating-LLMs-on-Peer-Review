       Author Response 22 May 2024
       Maria Gabriella Trovato

       Dear, Thank you for allowing us to adjust our paper. Below is the list of issues you asked us
       to address and our responses.
           1. While acknowledging the existence of prior research on displaced Syrians in Lebanon, such
              as the works of Fakhoury, Janmyr, and Romola, the authors could have further elaborated
              on how their current findings either corroborate or challenge the conclusions of these
              scholars. This comparative analysis would have enriched the discourse.
        Throughout the paper, our data analysis has been compared with the results of other
       scholars' work, even though there is not a dedicated section of the paper on this
       comparative work.
           1. The authors might have delved deeper into the data derived from their quantitative and
              qualitative interviews to expound upon the stark disparities in aspirations and priorities
              between displaced Syrians in Bekaa and Saida. By shedding light on the underlying
              determinants of these differences, the paper could have offered insights into how the
              socio-economic circumstances and living conditions of refugees collectively shape their
              hopes, expectations, and, consequently, their decisions regarding relocation.
       We answered this suggestion by reworking the section of the paper on the respondents'
       aspirations and priorities. Moreover, we added a paragraph in the discussion and
       conclusion. (Please see the part … Location appears to also affect the aspirations of
       displaced Syrians, with those living in the city dreaming of leaving and going abroad, while
       Syrians from Bekaa have the goals of improving their standard of living and meeting their
       basic needs.)
           1. While acknowledging that the influx of Syrians has altered the ethnic and sectarian
              composition of Lebanese cities and towns, exacerbating social tensions and fostering
              racial prejudices, the research paper, in its current form, falls short in adequately
              addressing how displaced Syrians experience these tensions. Additionally, there is room for
              a more comprehensive exploration of how the degree and nature of negative attitudes
              within the local population have evolved in tandem with Lebanon's multifaceted crises and
              how these experiences influence the dire conditions faced by the displaced population.
              Given that the authors mention their interviews aimed to gauge the strength of Syrians'
              ties to local communities, readers would benefit from an analysis of how intercommunal
              relations between the local Lebanese community and displaced Syrians evolve over time
       To answer this remark, we added a part at the beginning of the discussion and conclusion
       and reworked the introduction to clarify this point from the beginning of the article.
           1. Numerous studies conducted in Turkey on the conditions of Syrian asylum seekers
              document similar challenges to those experienced by Syrians in Lebanon. Incorporating
              insights from this extensive body of research in Turkey, juxtaposed with the Lebanese
              context, could provide valuable comparative analysis, highlighting the ways in which
              Lebanon's unique political, legal, and social conditions exert a distinct influence on the
              Syrian population within its borders.
       We are aware of the importance of comparing migrants' conditions in different countries.
       However, our paper's scope was different. Nonetheless, we are in touch with our Horizon
       research partner in Turkey, and we have discussed writing a paper comparing the data from

          1. In light of the prevailing structural constraints, notably the absence of a robust domestic
             legal framework designed to safeguard the rights and well-being of Syrians residing in
             Lebanon, it becomes increasingly questionable whether the ostensibly modest policy
             recommendations put forth by the researchers can yield any discernible and positive
             impact on the dire circumstances faced by the Syrian population. An astute examination of
             the limitations inherent in these short-term and relatively small-scale policy proposals
             could have imparted a greater degree of nuance and sophistication to the authors'
             discussion. By addressing this crucial concern, the paper could have embraced a more
             comprehensive perspective, acknowledging the formidable hurdles posed by the
             overarching legal and institutional deficiencies within Lebanon. In doing so, it would have
             offered readers a more well-rounded understanding of the complexities inherent in
             effecting change in the lives of displaced Syrians within such a challenging and
             constrained environment.
      We are aware of the modesty of the proposed policy recommendations compared to the
      complexity of the governance of the phenomena. Therefore, we have developed new
      recommendations based on the reworked discussion and conclusions.
