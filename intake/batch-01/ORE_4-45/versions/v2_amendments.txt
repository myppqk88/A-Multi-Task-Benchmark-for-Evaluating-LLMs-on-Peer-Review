Amendments from Version 1

In response to reviewers feedback, we have made several
significant revisions to the manuscript to enhance its theoretical
grounding, relevance, and clarity. These changes include:
- Clarification of the theory of change: We expanded the
introduction to provide a clearer explanation of the “theory of
change” proposed in the manuscript. We linked this theory to
alternative sociotechnical imaginaries, addressing the need
for participatory governance models that integrate narratives,
infrastructures, and collective expectations. This revision helps
situate the paper’s arguments within broader debates on data
governance.
- Integration of key literature: To strengthen the contextualization
of the paper, we incorporated several key references, including
foundational works by Ostrom (1990), Morozov (2015),
Fuchs (2021), and Calzada (2021). These references support
our discussions on data commons, digital sovereignty, and
community-driven governance, providing a more comprehensive
academic framework.
- Clarification of the DECODE-Decidim project’s contemporary
relevance: We revised the discussion section to clarify how the
DECODE-Decidim project remains relevant to contemporary
issues such as AI disruption and data sovereignty. This added
context emphasizes the continued importance of decentralized
and commons-based governance in addressing modern
challenges like algorithmic decision-making and surveillance.
- Reframing the manifesto as a technopolitical artefact: We
reframed the Digital Democracy and Data Commons Manifesto
as a technopolitical artefact, highlighting its role as a living
document that challenges technocentric, AI-driven governance
models. This revision underscores the manifesto’s potential
impact on policy, infrastructure, and participatory technology
design.
- Enhancing the abstract: We revised the abstract to provide
clearer details on the outcomes of the collaborative writing sprint
and the evolution of the manifesto, making the paper’s objectives
and results more explicit.
Any further responses from the reviewers can be found at
the end of the article
