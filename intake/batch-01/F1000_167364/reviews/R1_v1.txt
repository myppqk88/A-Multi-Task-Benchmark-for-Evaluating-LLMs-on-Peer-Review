This is a well-written study that makes a valuable contribution to understanding the integration of digital technologies in intercultural teaching, offering a solid basis for further research in the field.
However, the following aspects merit consideration to reinforce the study.
The inclusion criterion IC1, “Scientific articles written in English or Spanish,” may have influenced the results for RQ1, as the concentration of research in the Americas and Europe could be partly explained by the acceptance of Spanish-language publications. The finding that Mexico leads scientific production with 15% (n=4), followed by the United States, Spain, and Russia with 11% each (n=3), should therefore be acknowledged as potentially shaped by language-based inclusion.
The inclusion of Ciftci and Savas (2018) also appears problematic, since the study emphasizes the benefits of technologies in intercultural teaching but does not specify the use of particular digital tools, raising questions about consistency with the criteria.
For RQ3, it remains unclear whether the reported areas of digital technology integration (communication skills, cultural attitudes, and technological adoption) were deliberately targeted in the interventions or whether they emerged as benefits observed during implementation. A similar lack of clarity is noted in RQ4 with respect to intercultural competences: it is not evident whether these were explicitly addressed in the design of interventions or whether they developed incidentally during the research process. Finally, the potential influence of specific digital tools on the development of particular competences is not sufficiently addressed, and further insights in this direction would strengthen the study.
