Dear Reviewer,
Warm greetings. We sincerely thank you for the time dedicated to reviewing our manuscript entitled
“Integration of digital technologies in the teaching of intercultural competences: systematic literature mapping”
, and for your valuable comments, which have allowed us to strengthen the quality and rigor of the study.
Below, we provide detailed responses to each of the comments received:
Comment 1:
The inclusion criterion IC1, “Scientific articles written in English or Spanish,” may have influenced the concentration of research in the Americas and Europe, thus affecting the findings for RQ1.
Response:
We agree that the adopted language criterion (IC1) may have partially influenced the results of RQ1. We acknowledge that the inclusion of publications in Spanish may have favored the representation of studies from Latin American contexts, especially from Mexico and South America. This limitation has been added as a critical observation in the discussion section, in order to make transparent the possible linguistic-geographical bias in the analyzed sample.
Incorporated explanatory text in the discussion section (at the end of RQ1):
Additionally, it is important to consider that the inclusion of studies exclusively in English or Spanish may have limited the geographical representativeness of the sample, favoring research originating from Latin America and Spanish-speaking countries. This linguistic bias may have influenced the predominance of Mexico and Spain in the results; therefore, future reviews could expand the language criterion to include other relevant languages, such as French, Chinese, or Arabic, to better reflect geocultural diversity.
Comment 2:
The inclusion of the study by Ciftci and Savas (2018) appears problematic, as it does not specify particular digital tools.
Response:
After reexamining the study by Ciftci and Savas (2018), we agree that, although it addresses intercultural teaching in digitally mediated contexts, it does not specify particular digital technologies, which raises questions about its alignment with criterion IC5. Therefore, we have marked this case as an exception in the results section and have revised its treatment to clearly distinguish it from studies that do report specific digital tools.
Text added in the article (in RQ2):
It is worth noting that the study by Ciftci and Savas (2018) constitutes an exception, as it recognizes the benefits of using technologies in intercultural contexts but does not describe specific digital tools implemented in the intervention. This limits its comparability with other studies in the corpus and should be considered a methodological limitation in the interpretation of the results.
Comment 3:
In RQ3 and RQ4, it is unclear whether the competences and areas were explicit objectives of the interventions or emerged as incidental outcomes.
Response:
We acknowledge that not all studies in the corpus clearly differentiated between competences explicitly targeted in the pedagogical design and those that emerged during the implementation process. This distinction has been added to the corresponding sections, along with a methodological note in the data extraction section, indicating how both types of findings were recorded.
Text added in the article, in the Methods section (end of 2.4):
It is important to note that during data extraction, both competences and focus areas explicitly stated in the objectives or methodological designs of the studies, as well as those that emerged as observed outcomes or later reflections, were recorded. This distinction was maintained in the analysis matrices, although not all studies clearly differentiated between these levels.
Text added at the end of RQ3:
It is important to mention that in several studies, the intercultural competences and areas of digital integration were identified as emerging benefits during the implementation of the interventions rather than as predefined pedagogical goals. This methodological ambiguity may affect the interpretation of the results and highlights the need for future studies with more explicit designs and structured pre-post evaluations.
Comment 4:
The potential influence of specific digital tools on the development of particular competences is not sufficiently addressed.
Response:
We highly value this observation, which has allowed us to enrich the analysis. We have incorporated an additional reflection in the discussion section, addressing the need for deeper investigation into the correspondence between types of digital tools and specific intercultural competences—particularly in teaching contexts mediated by emerging technologies such as immersive environments or social networks.
Text added in the article, in the Discussion section (after RQ4):
Although technological categories and frequently addressed intercultural competences were identified, there remains a gap in the literature regarding the specific correlation between particular digital tools and the competences developed. Except for some cases—such as the use of COIL to promote intercultural communication—studies rarely establish a direct relationship between the tool used and the competence achieved. This methodological limitation suggests the need for future research using experimental designs that can assess the differential impact of specific technologies (such as virtual reality, gamification, or social networks) on the development of competences such as empathy, global collaboration, or intercultural critical thinking.
Once again, we sincerely thank you for your suggestions, which have significantly enriched the manuscript and helped us to reinforce its conceptual and methodological soundness. We remain attentive to any further comments you may wish to share.
