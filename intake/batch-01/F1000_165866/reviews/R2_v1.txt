Review Report
Title:
Research Trends on Motivation and Job Satisfaction: A Scientometric Analysis
An informative scientometric summary of research trends on job satisfaction and motivation throughout the past ten years (2014–2023) is given in this paper. The authors properly analyzed publication trends, citation patterns, keyword occurrences, and authorship networks using data taken from the Scopus database and scientometric tools, including VOSviewer and Biblioshiny. The methodological strategy is suitable and fits in nicely with the goals of the study.
The present paper provides an incisive scientometric analysis of research trends on motivation and job satisfaction over the last 10 years (2014–2023). Using data from the Scopus database and scientometric tools including VOSviewer and Biblioshiny, the authors appropriately examined publication trends, citation patterns, keyword occurrences, and authorship networks.
The methodological approach is appropriate and aligns closely with the study's objectives.
However, to strengthen the manuscript, the authors could:
Extend the discussion section to analyze the consequences of collaboration patterns by author and country.
To more clearly depict the bibliometric linkages, use visual aids like co-authorship or keyword co-occurrence maps.
Give more details on the search approach, such as the inclusion/exclusion criteria and the kinds of documents that were examined (such as conference papers, reviews, and articles).
Discuss limitations—such as database dependence (Scopus only, not Web of Sciences) and potential bias due to language restrictions—to enhance scientific rigor.
Provide suggestions for future HRM study avenues based on the tendencies that have been noticed.
Overall, the study makes a significant contribution to the body of existing literature and is cohesive and well-structured. It could be deemed scientifically sound and ready for indexing, if possible, after the aforementioned changes.
