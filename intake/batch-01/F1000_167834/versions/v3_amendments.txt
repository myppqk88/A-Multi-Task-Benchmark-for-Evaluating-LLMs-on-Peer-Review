Revised Amendments from Version 2
The authors thank the reviewers and editors for their insightful feedback, which has significantly improved the quality and clarity of this article. In response, major revisions were made to strengthen interpretation, methodological transparency, and policy relevance.
The Results and Discussion sections have been expanded to move beyond statistical reporting by explaining how demographic factors—such as gender, age, education level, position, and teaching experience—actively shape academic staff performance. The revised analysis now connects these variables to teaching quality, research engagement, and leadership participation, supported by recent literature (2018–2025).
The discussion on gender disparities has been deepened to address institutional and cultural influences, including promotion practices, mentorship opportunities, and family-care responsibilities that affect female academics’ advancement.
Under Limitations and Future Research, we now acknowledge that the cross-sectional design limits causal inference and recommend longitudinal or causal modeling approaches to explore cause–effect relationships.
The Recommendations section has been rewritten to include actionable strategies such as gender-responsive mentorship programs, study-leave and research funding, transparent promotion systems, and inclusive leadership pathways.
Finally, the Conclusion was refined to emphasize that demographic characteristics are not merely descriptive but functionally shape performance outcomes. The revised version integrates statistical, institutional, and policy perspectives, offering practical insights for improving equity, leadership development, and performance management in Uganda’s private universities.
The authors hope that these improvements make the article more informative, evidence-driven, and useful to researchers, policymakers, and higher education practitioners.
