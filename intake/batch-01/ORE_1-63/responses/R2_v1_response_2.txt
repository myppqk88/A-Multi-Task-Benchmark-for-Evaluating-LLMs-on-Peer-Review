  Author Response 05 Oct 2021
  ANASTASIA ZABANIOTOU

  Reply to comments of REVIEWER #3 Dr Elizabeth Pollitzer, Director, Portia Ltd, UK; founder, Gender
  Summit.

  Comment 1: There are a number of potentially promising aspects raised in the paper, namely devising
  and implementing gender equality policies in engineering education contexts and in countries and
  institutions with cultural and possibly legal barriers to gender equality, and the experience of the RMEI
  network functioning as a CoP to advance good practice for gender equality in engineering. Focusing on
  these topics would be very useful to anyone planning a proposal for Horizon Europe.
  Reply 1: Thank you for appreciating my effort and taking the time and effort to review my paper.

  Comment 2: However, these aspects get lost in the current text, which includes a description of the
  TARGET project and extensive discussion of topics only partly relevant to the three questions listed in the
  Methods issues, e.g., discussion of SDGs.
  Reply 2: The reviewer’s comments have been taken into consideration and the paper has
  transformed to a METHOD paper, that builds upon a use case that is the case of the RMEI network
  where the SFL was implemented and validated, following the suggestion of ORE’s
  editors. According to ORE guidelines “Method articles in some areas of research, e.g. education, social
  sciences or economics, may benefit from a different structure, in which case a more flexible format is
  possible as long as the authors ensure that they describe their methods and sources in sufficient detail
  for others to be able to repeat the research’. For this reason, the title has changed to: Learning
  outcomes of leader and leadership development: a method for a soft and female leadership (SFL)
  empirical framework for driving the gender equality change in Mediterranean engineering
  education [version 3]

  Comment 3: The SFL is extensively discussed as a framework but only very briefly how it was actually
  practised by the CoP for the development of gender equality policy for the institutions involved and how
  the toolbox was developed and tested.
  Reply 3: In this revised METHOD paper, in the conclusion part, I added a USE CASE section where it
  is explained how the SFL is practiced and validated. The SFL was validated at the network level. It is
  expecting that by implementing the SFL framework members of the RMEI CoP can integrate
  gender equality knowledge via mindful awareness, widen their experience, deepen their
  understanding, and build a gender-sensitive mindset. With practice, a journey of looking inward
  can begin for the participants, with the help of science, art, and culture, via workshops to inquire,

  challenge, and furthermore proceed with gender equality change at their institution. It is expecting
  that change agents and new leaders for gender equality will be developed to inspire other higher

  Comment 4: I would caution about the description of the leadership style as a combination of 'soft
  leadership' and 'female leadership'. Whilst women leaders have been shown often to adopt different
  leadership styles to men, there is no such a thing as "female" leadership.
  Reply 4: Thank you for the comment. At the RMEI, we decided to call the new leadership SFL
  because it is a combination of a female leader and soft competence building. This is a new term we
  applied in our method. The specific techniques that are used in the current study are empirical,
  supported also by the literature, and the gender equality knowledge built by the EU HORIZON2020
  TARGET project. The author replicated the soft leadership framework developed by prof Rao M.S
  but also added to the field by examining aspects of the engineering education domain and the
  Mediterranean community of practice (CoP) for gender equality.

  Comment 5: I would also caution about the potential misunderstanding of the acronym «GEP" which is
  widely used to stand for Gender Equality Plans rather than as in the paper for gender equality policy.
  Reply 5: Thank you, it is corrected as suggested.
