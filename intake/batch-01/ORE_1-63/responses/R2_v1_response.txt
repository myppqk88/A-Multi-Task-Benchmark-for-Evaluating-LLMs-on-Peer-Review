       Reply to comments by REVIEWER #1 Jerneja Penca, Euro-Mediterranean University, Piran,
       Slovenia
       Comment 1 I am not very familiar with the scope and aim of the journal to which I was invited
       to review. Thus, I may not be pitching my response at the right level, as I opted to provide a
       review of the article in light of the questions posed to me and my knowledge of the area I am
       reviewing.
        Reply 1: We are sorry you are not very familiar with ORE. For your information, Open
       Research Europe's scope covers any scholarly work in a social sciences discipline resulting
       from studies that have Horizon 2020 funding, regardless of the perceived interest and the
       extent of novelty. The peer review focuses on whether the paper has academic merit and
       whether adequate detail has been provided to enable others to reproduce and use the
       method. Nevertheless, I would like to thank you for making the effort to provide your
       review that helped us to upgrade the manuscript as a METHOD paper. https://open-
       research-europe.ec.europa.eu/for-authors/article-guidelines/social-sciences/method-
       articles#main

       Comment 2: The article engages with some of the relevant literature to support the claims
       made, but it does not make it clear how does it build on that literature, what gaps it fills and how
       does it contribute to existing knowledge. I wonder if a comprehensive survey of existing literature
       regarding gender and leadership types has even been made.
       Reply 2: Following the ORE editors, this paper was transformed into a METHOD paper
       because this type of article was the best fit for our paper. Therefore, the paper as it is
       transformed to a METHOD paper builds upon a use case that is the case of the RMEI

      research, e.g. education, social sciences or economics, may benefit from a different structure, in

      Comment 3: I also wonder about the extent to which expertise from the disciplines of sociology,
      gender studies or other social science backgrounds have been involved in the research of this
      interdisciplinary theme.
      Reply 3: As is stated in the conclusion section, we countered the lack of knowledge on social
      change theories; therefore, we used our intuition, creativity, interdisciplinarity approaches,
      and our embedded experience from scientific and academic work, engineering problem-
      solving methodologies and R&I approaches. All these are complemented with tailored and
      customized activities, under a reflexive prism that the TARGET project taught us. That’s why
      the SFL and leaders’ development framework for gender equality advancement in
      engineering, that we developed at RMEI, is grounded on empirical evidence. It derives as an
      outcome from an engineering context across Mediterranean countries.

      Comment 4: It is not clear what kind of empirical data supports the piece. The authors are clear
      in stating their commitment (as a group) to promote gender equality in engineering schools and
      learning for sustainability, and to fight the under-representation of women in leadership and
      senior positions at the engineering education systems. But we miss the information on what
      study/research was conducted and to what aim. Rather than being supported by a study, the
      article comes closer to a manifesto. The content may be convincing, and I (as probably many
      other readers) sympathize with the goal of gender equality in managerial positions and the
      education machinery, support values proposed and agree with multiple other claims made (e.g.
      that gender equality strategies are context-sensitive or that leadership has typically had a
      masculine construction.). However, the article would need to be significantly more precise in
      various points to qualify as a research article.
      Reply 4: I fully understand your claims because as you reviewed it as a research paper.
      Now, that I am resubmitting it as a METHOD paper, I hope you are satisfied with the
      information provided to support a METHOD paper at ORE. Discussion is now enriched with a
      section ‘USE CASE’ where the implementation of the SFL is explained.

      Comment 5: As a side note, for analytical purposes, I would suggest writing in slightly longer
      paragraphs where a certain thought is developed, rather than in one sentence paragraphs
      (Discussion, Conclusion section)
      Reply 5: Following your suggestion, I tried as much as possible, to shorten sentences and
      paragraphs (Results, Conclusion) and somehow the discussion part.
