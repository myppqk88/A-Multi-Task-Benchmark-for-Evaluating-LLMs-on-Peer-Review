       There are a number of potentially promising aspects raised in the paper, namely devising and
       implementing gender equality policies in engineering education contexts and in countries and
       institutions with cultural and possibly legal barriers to gender equality, and the experience of the
       RMEI network functioning as a CoP to advance good practice for gender equality in engineering.
       Focusing on these topics would be very useful to anyone planing a proposal for Horizon Europe.

       However, these aspects get lost in the current text, which includes description of the TARGET
       project and extensive discussion of topics only partly relevant to the three questions listed in the
       Methods issues, e.g., discussion of SDGs. The SFL is extensively discussed as a framework but only
       very briefly how it was actually practiced by the CoP for development of gender equality policy for
       the institutions involved and how the toolbox was developed and tested. I would caution about
       the description of the leadership style as a combination of 'soft leadership' and 'female
       leadership'. Whilst women leaders have been shown often to adopt different leadership styles to
       men, there is no such a thing as "female" leadership. I would also caution about the potential
       misunderstanding of the acronym "GEP" which is widely used to stand for Gender Equality Plans
       rather than as in the paper for gender equality policy.
