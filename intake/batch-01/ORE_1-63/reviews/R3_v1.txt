       Built upon an interrogation of the dominant gender inequality in higher education institutions,
       this paper addresses the concept of leader and female leadership in the 21st century as a pathway
       to establish a new promising and creative mindset. To come to terms with such a sublime aim, the
       paper presents a logical analytical process that falls into five main parts:

        1. The introduction including definitions of the key concepts of soft skills and leadership that
           Figure 1clearly illustrates. Then, it underpins the aim and the methods of the study. In line
           with the scientists’ assumption about the limits of the concepts of leadership, the author
           argues that leadership is a social construct that embodies new relationships combining soft
           skills with women’s leadership ability.

          Leadership integrates context-based characteristics and comprises soft skills foreshadowing
          a change in the cultural frame of mind rather than focusing on mere reassignment and/ or
          redistribution of tasks.

          Given that the aim is to affect people’s behaviour(s), manners and ways of modernized
          thinking to establish women's leadership, the author’s title highlights a complex framework
          of soft female leadership to boost gender equality in engineering education, as well as in
          other fields.

        2. This study positions itself in the TARGET project that supports GEPs within the network of
           RMEI that inspires the author’s vision of gender equality via leadership and its diverse
           performances in various contexts with varied characteristics as mentioned in figure 1.
           Therefore, the author fairly explains that leadership transcends the conventional set of
           criteria to focus more profoundly on the mental and affective processes that instigate a new
           set of behaviour and ethics as an alternative to the existing ones.

        3. A thorough investigation of SFL endorsed by a multidimensional gender equality learning
           process to build up individual and collective behaviour(s) with a far-reaching impact that
           blurs the conventional borderlines of gender.

          At this level, I applaud the coherent analysis along with the smooth shift from the
          conceptualized soft skills leadership to ‘transformative learning’ within the educational and
          societal contexts. The paper has provided thorough implications of the SL in terms of space
          (Mediterranean institutions, 28 engineering schools, and societies), time (present and
          future) and power. When the author proceeds to develop SL ‘’new narratives’, readers are
          systematically provided with Figure 2.

        4. The core of the paper contains 5 pages conducting readers to track the six steps of the SFL
           framework. ‘’SFL framework new deliveries’’ attempts to reveal the interconnectedness
           between approaches, narratives, and a selected toolbox entailing the change of the mindset
           and endorsing flexibility towards the achievement of SDGs.

          Based on the author’s knowledge of the socio-cultural and psychological burden of gender
          inequality in engineering, she insightfully and minutely develops six steps of soft female
          leadership comprising leadership in research and innovation that inter-connect diverse
          fields in academia and industry.

        5. The discussion section and the conclusions present the author’s combination of the
           theoretical, thematic and structural dimensions of gender equality from the perspective of
           soft female leadership that falls into the column of behavioural theory of leadership; figures
           3 and 4 explicitly display.
    The paper presents an inspiring contribution to academic research prompting context-based

    female leadership. In challenge of the male-dominated institutions, the author concludes that
    each model of leadership should be tailored according to the diagnosed problem with gender
    equality. Given the thought-provoking arguments, this paper could originate future
    interdisciplinary research towards the establishment of soft female leadership in institutions and
    society equally.

    What I also appreciate is the gender-sensitive language which is strategically avoided. For
    example, the term female, as a noun, normally implies a set of biological characteristics of
    femaleness. Yet, in this paper, the term ‘female’ is used as an adjective, as in ‘female leadership,’ to
    imply the author's awareness of the rhetoric of gender.

    Accordingly, I confirm that I have read the submitted paper, and I confirm that it responds to
    academic scientific standards. I, therefore, approve that this paper is ready to be indexed.
