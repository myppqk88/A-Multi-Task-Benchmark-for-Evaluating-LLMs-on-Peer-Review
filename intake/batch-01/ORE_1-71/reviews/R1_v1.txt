       Dear authors,

       Thank you for giving me the opportunity to review your manuscript entitled “Measuring the
       impact of publicly funded open innovation programs: the case of Data Market Services
       Accelerator.” This research topic is highly relevant due to the strong increase in publicly funded
       open innovation initiatives. Thus, it is of utmost importance to generate standard measurement
       criteria to assess their effectiveness and efficiency. In addition, I appreciate the idea of employing
       the Data Market Services Accelerator as a case study to test the implementation of the metrics.

       However, whereas the research idea is great, there exist room for improvements in the research
       execution. I outline my concerns below:

       Standardized measurement scale:
       The authors` “intention in this article is to identify a flexible and generalizable framework” for
       measuring the impact of publicly funded open innovation initiatives (p. 7). Yet the results as
       exhibited in Tables 4 through 7 solely highlight criteria relevant for the DMS. Based on the claims
       in the introduction (and also the lengthy elaborations on why open data requires standardization),
       I was surprised to not see a list of criteria applicable to various open innovation programs as the
       result. In fact, the results part contains the findings from the measures for the DMS.
       Consequently, there exist two ways forwards: Either the authors reduce their claims in the
       introduction and transparently depict that the manuscript focuses on reporting the effectiveness
       and efficiency of the DMS or the authors tone down the DMS related work but instead present the
       general toolkit as their main results.

       Relevance and coherence of arguments:
       The manuscript includes (especially in the introduction but also in other parts) lengthy
       elaborations on topics only peripherally related to measuring open innovation programs. In turn,
       at other parts the authors jump from one thought to another without providing logical
       connections. For example, the sub-chapter “Open innovation programmes for startups and SMEs”

    does not include any example of open innovation programmes (Kindly note that this only
    represents one of multiple appearances of this problem). Instead, it elaborates in general what
    open innovation is and how it can be seen in the context of AI. To overcome this issue, I would
    suggest the authors to rework the whole manuscript and cut all paragraphs that do not directly
    relate to the main theme. This especially applies to the introduction which is considerably long and
    contains several paragraphs which are not necessary from a scientific point of view but instead are
    written in similar fashion with consulting reports which must contain certain words (AI,
    standardization, Big Data, …) and phrases. On this note, I would also like to remind the authors
    that good scientific practice implies that we include all information sources as citation. In fact, the
    first two pages of the manuscript contain only four citations. There exist numerous paragraphs
    with statements including important contents that do not include references. To make this more
    comprehensible for readers please include citations for every idea that was not discovered by
    yourself. As an approximate guideline I would like to give you the following advice: Every sentence
    containing a statement (not based on your research) that is more specific than “the sky is blue”
    should include a citation

    Accelerator, incubator and open innovation:
    “Support programmes for startups and SMEs have typically taken the form of accelerators and
    incubators. More recently, open innovation programmes have emerged at the intersection of
    these two paradigms” (p. 2). Whereas the introduction states that this research focuses on the
    intersection and interplay of accelerators and incubators, the methods and results sections do
    only focus on accelerator. Again, please ensure coherency throughout the manuscript. On this
    note, I also doubt that the developed measures can apply to all open innovation programs as
    open innovation does not only take place within startups and SMEs but also includes stake holders
    coming from large industry, universities and other research institutions. Consequently, I would
    suggest toning down the focus on open innovation and instead rely on a more nuanced approach
    towards entrepreneurship.

    Methods:
    The survey sample consists of six respondents for the first cohort and 20 respondents for the
    second cohort. Nevertheless, the authors argue as if the sample size would be sufficiently large by
    employing percentage numbers and inducing from the survey results that the DMS worked
    successful. Being a quantitative researcher myself, I strongly doubt the expressiveness of these
    two surveys. Consequently, I would advise to refrain from using percentages but instead including
    the actual numbers (e.g. 2 companies instead of 33.3% of our respondents) to transparently depict
    the small sample size. Moreover, please refrain, especially in the discussion section, from making
    strong claims based on the survey and workshop results.

    Discussion:
    The discussion section does not include limitations and suggestion for future research. Please add
    them. Moreover, readers might want to know the theoretical and scientific implications of your
    work. In addition, the discussion section should not present or discuss new results. Therefore, I
    would suggest moving those parts of the “Increasing engagement” sub-chapter highlighting new
    results (e.g. results from the first workshop) to the results section. Last, the authors argue in the
    beginning of the discussion that their research shows differences in regard to geographic origins
    and industry sectors. However, the results section does not split the findings into geographical
    regions or industry. Please only include elaborations on findings in the discussion section that
    were also shown in the results section.

    Summing up, I agree with the importance and the potential of the research topic. Yet I believe the
    manuscript requires substantial improvement work before it can be considered for indexing.
    Therefore, my suggestion would be a major revision.
