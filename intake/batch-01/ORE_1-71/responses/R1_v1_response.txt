       Maria Priestley

       Dear Dr. Pruschak,

       We would like to thank you for the detailed feedback which you provided on our manuscript
       last year. Over the past few months, we have conducted a major revision of the paper in
       response to your suggestions.

       A full description of the changes can be found in a separate document linked here.

       The main changes can be summarised as follows:
           ○Our claims about the generalisability of the framework and the strength of claims
            regarding our results have been reduced. We have also highlighted that our

                  manuscript focuses on measuring the performance of only one programme – the
                  DMS Accelerator.

              ○   More references have been added to support our claims, particularly in the
                  Introduction section.

              ○   We have provided more examples of open innovation, and have justified our decision
                  to describe DMS and other similar initiatives as examples of open innovation, rather
                  than relying on traditional notions of acceleration and incubation. We have also
                  clarified that the scope of our paper focuses on initiatives that serve startups and
                  SMEs, which are only some of the various possible stakeholders that can be targeted
                  by open innovation initiatives.

              ○   We have decided to retain information about the characteristics of data-centric
                  programmes (including words such as “AI”, “standardisation” and “big data”), as these
                  concepts are an essential part of the specialised technical training and advisory
                  services offered by data-centric open innovation programmes. By including these
                  terms in the article, we are able to engage with audiences outside academia who
                  work in data-oriented initiatives.

              ○   We have obtained additional data, which has increased our survey sample size from
                  20 to 47 respondents across the three DMS cohorts. We have also reported the new
                  results using actual numbers (rather than percentages) for transparency.

              ○   We have now added a section on the “Limitations and future work”.
          Once again, thank you for taking the time to consider our submission. Your insights are
          greatly appreciated. We hope that you will find the updated article more rigorous and
          worthy of indexing.
