      I have added something on this topic to the introduction, namely the following passage:

      "In the academic literature, one finds a distinction between three processes of
      autocratisation, namely “democratic regression/backsliding (when the core features of
      liberal democracy are eroded but no transition to autocratic regime occurs), democratic
      breakdown (where democracy collapses), or authoritarian deepening (when we observe the
      deepening of authoritarian characteristics in already autocratic regimes)“ (Tomini, Gibril &
      Bochev 2023, 120). Waldner and Lust (2018) review theories that explain the causes of the
      rise of authoritarianism. They distinguish between “six theory families” explaining
      autocratisation (109): There are approaches that stress the role of political actors, political
      culture, political institutions, political economy (income levels, income distribution,
      macroeconomic development, class relations, etc.), conflicts among groups of citizens, and
      international actors. Waldner and Lust argue for a model that synthesises such
      explanations. The six theory families include economic, political, and cultural factors. If a
      synthesis is feasible, then the implication is that there can be economic, political, and
      cultural factors that make the rise or strengthening of authoritarianism more likely. In a
      comparable manner, the political theorist Franz L. Neumann (2017) argues that there are
      economic, political, and socio-cultural forms of alienation that drive the rise or
      intensification of authoritarianism if they combine with massive fears among the population
      (destructive psychological alienation) that turns into persecutory anxiety organised by
      authoritarian parties, leaders, institutions, or social movements (see also Fuchs 2017)."

      “However, there can also be what Amlinger and Nachtwey (2025) term “democratic fascism”
      where democracy is used to advance fascism, which does not mean that fascism is
      democratic, but that fascism uses the structures, concepts, rhetoric, and language of
      democracy to present itself as democratic and democrats as undemocratic in order to
      successively undermine democracy from within. Lührmann and Lindberg (2019) identified
      217 instances of autocratisation in a total of 109 countries in the years from 1900 until 2017.
      They speak of three waves of autocratisation (1926-1942, 1961-1977, since 1994). The third
      wave corresponds to what Amlinger and Nachtwey describe as democratic fascism,
      fascism’s hijacking of democracy: “Democratic erosion became the modal tactic during the
      third wave of autocratization. Here, incumbents legally access power and then gradually,
      but substantially, undermine democratic norms without abolishing key democratic
      institutions. Such processes account for 70% in the third reversal wave with prominent
      cases of such gradual deterioration“ (Lührmann and Lindberg 2019, 1105).The implication of
      such developments is that public and commons projects need a deliberate commitment to
      democracy”

      Author:
      That’s a good point, I added a new paragraph on this topic:
      “An action-based humanist approach faces the question if autonomous AI-based system
      such as self-driving cars, autonomous weapon systems, cleaning robots, generative AI
      systems such as ChatGPT, or Internet bots are actors or not. From a humanist perspective,
      the answer is that so-called autonomous technologies do not operate independently from
      humans but depend on them. They depend on human engineering, programming, repair,
      energy produced by humans, etc. In addition, they do not have the same features as
      humans have, which is why robots and AI cannot replace humans. “Robots cannot truly
      replace humans because they lack praxis and poiesis, which means they do not understand
      what it means to be social, what it means to love, what it means to be sad, what morality is,
      what desires and creativity are etc. They lack fundamental human capacities. Robots do not
      have feelings. They do not have intentions. They do not have morals. They do not have
      desires. They do not have a will. They do not understand and experience love, sadness and
      happiness. Computers cannot deal with ambiguity and dialectics. They operate based on
      algorithms, while humans are non-algorithmic” (Fuchs 2022, 146-147). The philosopher
      Hubert Dreyfus (1986, xiv) argues in this context: “Human emotional life remains unique, to
      be sure, but what is more important is our ability to recognize, to synthesize, to intuit. There
      are good reasons to believe that those abilities as well are rooted in processes altogether
      different from the calculative reason of computer programs”.”
