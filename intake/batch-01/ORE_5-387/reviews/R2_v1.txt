    Review of
    “What is and how do we achieve a resilient digital democracy?”
    By Christian Fuchs

    Overall, I have read the contribution with interest, and consider it worthy of indexing, but
    considering the comments below in a revision would further strengthen the manuscript.

    The arguments provided add nicely to the recent discussion on digital democracy/ies.
    However, the literature appears to be unnecessarily restricted to political science references, while
    significant contributions have also been recently published in neighboring fields such as
    computational social science. It would be valuable to mention some of those activities as well. The
    same applies to participatory resilience and various new participatory/co-creation approaches to
    democracy. (This is just a suggestion though, not a strict requirement for the acceptance of this
    paper.)
    In the following, I offer some more specific comments.
    Page 6: It would be interesting to explore “democracy as a feature and property of resilience” vs.
    “resilience as a feature of democracy”. I actually think the two are closely related: decentralized
    bottom-up elements and collective intelligence-based approaches are features of democracies,
    which are also significant for resilience. The same applies to diversity/pluralism and to a fair
    competition of ideas (“the best approach should win”).

    It would be further interesting to learn what is known about the transition of democracies to
    autocratic systems? What are the drivers of this process? Identifying them would allow one to
    defend against this process more effectively. I think that democracies are not only degrading.
    They are under attack, for example, by hybrid war and, potentially, by surveillance capitalism as
    well. They are also being questioned or challenged by various hierarchical institutions and
    platform monopolies.
    Page 10: The second paragraph is mentioning the role of social media platforms, which may
    support authoritarianism. In fact, this very much depends on the network structure, which has
    become more centralized over time. Recent publications, for example, have stressed the role of
    network structure for the diversity of opinions in a system.
    Page 11 mentions the role of private platform ownership. It may be mentioned more explicitly that
    these are “hijacking” attention by means of big nudging/micro-targeting, by auctions of human
    attention.
    The limitations of fact-checking should be mentioned explicitly. It is slow, while microtargeting is
    automated. Moreover, it cannot cope with manipulation based on converging technologies.
    Page 13: “there can also be democratic forms of fascism”: I think this formulation is highly
    misleading. The sentence probably wanted to articulate that some current forms of democracies
    have been hollowed out and their cores have been replaced by elements of fascism.
    Democratic ideals: Be more concrete in what you mean: constitutional values, human rights?
    You mention “collective problem-solving” in the last paragraph. It may be worth mentioning the
    role that “collective intelligence” can play in thriving democracies, if suitable participatory
    frameworks are offered for this.

    Minor:
    Page 4: I would suggest to replace “the delaminates” by “that delimits”
    “As an act of resistance, resilient action is always a political and normative form of action.”: I do not
    share this view. It appears too special/restrictive to me. You may find more general definitions in

    the literature on “participatory resilience”.
    “But neither resistance nor resilience or automatically…” should probably be replaced by “But
    neither resistance nor resilience are automatically…”
    Page 5: “… systems are actors and systems, not humans,…” might be better replaced by “…
    systems are actors and systems. These systems, not humans,…”

    “the present author takes an actor- and action-based human-oriented approach…”: What about
    the widespread role of AI in today’s society? It should probably be considered as well (e.g. in
    connection with mis- and disinformation, opinion manipulation).
    “we can distinguish between environmental, economic, political, and cultural crisis”:
    “technological” is missing, plus the close interrelationship of all of these areas in the information
    society, so we are actually faced with “techno-socio-economic-environmental systems”
    I would suggest to replace “equity and diversity” by “fairness and diversity”.
    Page 7: In Table 1, I would replace “economic organisations” by “economic institutions”
    Why do you start columns in your various Tables typically with capital letters – despite in column
    3?
    Table 2: Shouldn’t “terrorism and organized crime” be added and “hybrid warfare” as well?
    What about processes that may slowly undermine the values and principles of democracies?
    What about migration-related risks or health risks (such as pandemics)?
    Page 9: “can be disrupted by environmental, technological economic…”: What about digital
    systems and processes?
    As much as a “non-commerical”, “data minimization” and “green computing” approach are
    desirable: are these approaches realistic, as long as surveillance capitalism and hybrid wars reign?
    How can we regain agency?
    Table 3: “Decentralised data storage” could be better replaced by the more general term
    “Decentralisation” (everywhere in the manuscript)
    The 3rd and 5th row (Economy and Culture) have identical entries in the 3rd column (probably by
    accident?)
    “digital giants” would typically be referred to as “big tech” (should be replaced everywhere
    throughout the entire manuscript, if considered fitting)
    “Political disruption means that digital democracy platforms are censored”: This can be
    misinterpreted, as the headline suggests you are talking about “resilient digital democracy”, and
    censored systems would likely not be considered democratic systems
    Page 10: “number of legal rules” may be replaced by “number of legal acts”
    Page 11: “Between November 2023 and November.”: sentence is incomplete.
    Page 12: Besides Wikipedia, OpenStreetMap is another well-known and impressive example of
    non-commercial participatory platforms.
    Page 13: “decentralized data storage” would be better replaced by the more general term
    “decentralisation”
