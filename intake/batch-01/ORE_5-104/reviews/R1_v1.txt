       This manuscript provides a timely and well-structured evaluation of recent EU regulatory
       initiatives-specifically the Green Deal Industrial Plan (GDIP), Net Zero Industry Act (NZIA), Critical
       Raw Materials Act (CRMA), and the 2023 Battery Regulation-through the lens of stakeholder
       perceptions gathered via interactive workshops. The authors effectively synthesize policy
       objectives with empirical stakeholder feedback, offering insight into the coherence, feasibility, and
       implementation challenges of EU sustainability regulations. The article is particularly valuable in
       highlighting key areas of regulatory overlap, the role of skills development, and the need for social
       acceptance and streamlined permitting. While it avoids complex statistical modeling, its use of
       structured interactive tools (Mentimeter) enables the collection of relevant stakeholder data,
       making it highly suitable for an audience in sustainability policy, clean tech governance, or
       regulatory impact assessment. To enhance the manuscript's impact, the authors might consider:
            1. Including direct anonymized quotes for richer qualitative insight.
           2. Adding light statistical comparison (even descriptive trends across sessions).
           3. Situating EU policy efforts more explicitly within the global critical materials supply chain,
              especially regarding China's dominance.
       Overall, the work is a meaningful contribution to ongoing discussions around industrial policy,
       clean energy transitions, and regulatory design in the EU context.
