Comment 1.
Why was the secondary school level specifically examined in the study? Why weren't elementary schools or other levels examined? Because such a problem also exists in other levels. The article needs to explain in more detail why secondary schools were chosen.
Answer.
We appreciate this observation. The focus on secondary education is theoretically and pedagogically justified, as this stage is characterized by the consolidation of abstract, formal, and hypothetical-deductive reasoning, which are essential for the deep integration of mathematical competence and critical thinking. International frameworks (OECD, UNESCO) explicitly position secondary education as the key stage for developing higher-order thinking, analytical reasoning, and problem-solving skills. Therefore, the choice does not disregard other educational levels but reflects the cognitive, curricular, and evaluative density specific to secondary education.
Text included in the second paragraph of the introduction
This systematic review focuses on secondary education because this stage represents a critical period for the consolidation of abstract, formal, and hypothetical-deductive reasoning, which are foundational for both mathematical competence and critical thinking development (Inhelder & Piaget, 2013; OECD, 2019). International curricular frameworks emphasize secondary education as the stage where higher-order thinking, problem solving, and analytical reasoning are explicitly articulated as learning outcomes (UNESCO, 2021; OECD, 2022). Furthermore, secondary school mathematics involves increased conceptual complexity, formalization, and transfer demands, making it a particularly relevant context to examine the integration of mathematical competence and critical thinking.

Comment 2.
Why were studies conducted specifically between 2019 and 2025 considered in this research? Is there a particular reason for choosing 2019 as the starting year? It is believed that explaining the rationale for this choice in the article would be appropriate.
Answer.
The 2019–2025 timeframe was selected based on three criteria:
The recent consolidation of critical thinking as a transversal competence in international educational policies (OECD, UNESCO).
The expansion of active and digital methodologies following the COVID-19 pandemic.
The need to capture contemporary and contextually relevant evidence on the integration of both constructs in current educational settings.
Text included in the last paragraph of section 2.1 Information sources and search period
The time frame 2019–2025 was selected to capture the most recent and relevant evidence following the global curricular shift toward competency-based education and critical thinking development (OECD, 2019; UNESCO, 2021). This period also reflects the acceleration of digital transformation and active methodologies in education, particularly in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, which significantly reshaped pedagogical practices worldwide.

Comment 4.
The need for such a study should be explained in greater detail in the introduction section of the article. At the same time, it remains unclear whether critical thinking influences mathematical competence or whether mathematical competence influences critical thinking. What kind of relationship exists between these two constructs? The answers to these questions should be discussed more comprehensively in the introduction section.
Answer.
The introduction has been expanded to clarify that the relationship between critical thinking and mathematical competence is bidirectional, reciprocal, and dynamic, grounded in constructivist and sociocultural frameworks. It is argued that critical thinking enhances mathematical understanding, while structured mathematical activity fosters the development of critical thinking.
Text included in the introduction section
The relationship between mathematical competence and critical thinking is not unidirectional but reciprocal and recursive. Mathematical reasoning provides structured contexts for analysis, argumentation, and abstraction, while critical thinking enhances conceptual understanding, problem interpretation, and metacognitive regulation in mathematics (OECD, 2019; Pratiwi et al., 2025; Pereira-González et al., 2024; Rahmatika et al., 2024). Recent empirical studies confirm that students who engage in mathematical tasks requiring justification, modeling, and decision-making simultaneously develop higher levels of critical evaluation, cognitive flexibility, and reflective thinking (Li & Oon, 2024; Er, 2024; Pramasdyahsari et al., 2023). This interdependence positions both constructs as mutually reinforcing rather than hierarchically ordered, suggesting that their integration is pedagogically synergistic rather than sequential.
