       We thank the reviewer for the valuable comments.
       Comment: Methods: Providing a few more details re: EcoStack would help contextualize the
       research and ground the paper better
       Answer: We added information about the global object of the EcoStack project: “The aim of
       this research project is to develop ecologically, economically and socially sustainable crop
       production strategies via stacking of functional biodiversity. A specific objective was also to
       study farmer perspectives regarding a shift in focus from implementing single farm
       management practices to ‘stacking’ different practices. While benefits of agroecological
       practices are widely known, little information is available on combining those practices.”

      Comment: Discussion: “There was a striking contrast in the perception of agroecology
      between the participants and the research teams, with differing terms of reference between
      science and practice.” (pg 8) – more explanation/detail here would be helpful
      Answer: We provide now some more explanation: “There was a striking contrast in the
      perception of agroecology between the participants and the research teams. Indeed, the
      terms used by both to which the practices refer do not come from the same frame of
      reference between science on the one hand and the administrative context that financially

      Comment: Table 6: did reasons for adopting agroecological practices differ considerably
      across country? I’m specifically curious about “subsidies” and whether this was identified
      across all sites, or whether it was concentrated in a few countries. In general, it would be
      interesting to see more discussion disaggregated by country
      Answer: We explain a bit more the limitations: “Another limitation is that, due to the
      multiple workshops in the various countries, we were dependent on the data provided by
      project colleagues, sometimes with total and global results without the possibility of always
      being able to disaggregate the data (by country or participant profiles)”. With data from a
      questionnaire, it would have been easy to disaggregate it. Unfortunately, the format of the
      workshops, organized by different people, makes this impossible because the data was not
      all transmitted in the same way. We were only able to analyze the data in a limited way,
      which explains why it is difficult to see differences between countries and between types of
      participants. Furthermore, the orders of magnitude (number of responses per country or
      per profile) are not always equivalent.
