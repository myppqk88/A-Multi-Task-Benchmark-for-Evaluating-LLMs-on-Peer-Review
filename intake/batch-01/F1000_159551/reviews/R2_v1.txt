This manuscript offers a valuable bibliometric and systematic review of mobile payment adoption literature through a PRISMA-guided approach, identifying key trends, theories (e.g., UTAUT, TAM), and future research directions. The structure is clear and the topic is timely given the rapid expansion of digital finance.
However, several critical improvements are needed to strengthen academic rigor, enhance clarity, and update the manuscript’s scholarly relevance.
Major Comments and Suggestions
1.
Update Literature Coverage
Issue
: Although the scope spans from 2004 to 2023, many of the included references and patterns emphasize publications from 2016–2021. Yet new developments—like biometric authentication and Web3 applications—have emerged strongly in 2024–2025.
Recommendation
:
Update the literature search to
extend through late 2024
, capturing key recent studies (e.g., advances in contactless payments, super-apps in emerging markets).
Ensure the
final selection reflects the most current studies
, possibly adjusting inclusion criteria to i) recent top-tier journal papers or ii) novel technologies in mobile finance.
2.
Clarify Methodology and PRISMA Flow
Issue
: The PRISMA flow is mentioned but specific numbers (e.g., initial hits, duplicates removed, exclusions) are unclear.
Recommendation
:
Present a
PRISMA flowchart
in the main text or supplementary materials with explicit counts for each filtering stage.
Provide criteria for scoring and removing duplicates or irrelevant articles to increase transparency.
3.
Refine Coding and Thematic Analysis
Issue
: While TAM/UTAUT usage is highlighted, thematic coding methodology (category definitions, inter-rater reliability) is not clearly described.
Recommendation
:
Explain the
coding process
: how themes/categories were defined, who conducted the coding, and whether reliability was assessed (e.g., Cohen’s kappa).
Discuss how qualitative interpretation mapped keywords to theoretical models like trust, social influence, or effort expectancy.
4.
Enhance Visualizations and Narrative Flow
Issue
: Figures such as keyword evolution (Fig. 7) and thematic agenda (Fig. 8) contain many items with unclear visual differentiation.
Recommendation
:
Improve figure clarity through better
color schemes
, simplified legends, and highlighted trends.
In the text, explicitly
interpret each figure
, guiding the reader through the most salient keywords and emerging themes rather than just referencing them.
5.
Balance Research Gaps and Practical Relevance
Issue
: Table 3 and the agenda are comprehensive but lack prioritization and alignment with practice.
Recommendation
:
Distinguish
high-priority gaps
(e.g., adoption in the context of financial inclusion, data privacy implications, regulatory frameworks) vs. exploratory ones.
Connect these gaps to
real-world implications
—for policymakers, mobile platform designers, or regulators in digital finance.
6.
Strengthen Limitations and Future Work
Issue
: The limitations of the bibliometric method are briefly noted, but there is no discussion of possible biases (e.g., English-language only, citation lag).
Recommendation
:
Expand limitations to cover language bias, bibliometric time-lag skew, and potential database (Scopus/WoS) coverage issues.
Suggest follow-up studies combining
qualitative interviews
, thematic deep-dives, or emerging market case studies to triangulate findings.
Minor Suggestions
Provide
descriptive table captions
, especially where acronyms appear (e.g., clarify TAM, UTAUT).
Check consistency in referencing formatting and ensure DOI inclusion throughout.
Consider adding an
acronym glossary
for ease of reading.
Note any funding or open data sources for replication transparency.
Concluding Remarks
This manuscript addresses an important and evolving research area. With major revisions focused on updating literature, clarifying methods, refining narrative clarity, and strengthening the connection to practice, it could meaningfully advance research in mobile payment adoption which is acceptable with major revisions.
