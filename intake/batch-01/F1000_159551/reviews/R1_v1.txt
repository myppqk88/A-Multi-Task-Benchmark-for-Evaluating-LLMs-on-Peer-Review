This article explores the integration of mobile payments into the realm of research, with the objective of elucidating the technological, socio-economic, and cultural factors that influence user experience and satisfaction, thereby attracting a greater number of users. A systematic literature review based on the PRISMA 2020 framework was conducted to select 63 documents from Scopus and WoS. This review explored research trends in mobile payment adoption. The most frequently utilized models for this analysis are the extended Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the extended Unified Theory of Acceptance of Technology (UTAUT). The most salient variables in this study are social influence, perceived security, perceived risk, trust, and perceived benefits. Consequently, it is recommended that future research agendas prioritize subjects such as biometric authentication, payment flexibility, and contactless payments. From a business perspective, there is an emphasis on the development of more intuitive user interfaces.
This article provides a valuable roadmap for future research, particularly in identifying underexplored variables. The topic of this research is highly relevant and timely, particularly in light of the rapid advancements in digital finance and payment systems. The article is well-structured and clearly presented, contributing to its overall readability and coherence. The methodology employed is appropriate and provides meaningful insights for both theoretical development and practical application in the field.
Nevertheless, with minor revisions,  there are several areas that require further clarification and refinement in order to enhance the clarity, accuracy, and academic rigor of the paper. Below are some key points identified in the article that could benefit from improvement:
1) Inclusion of Database Search Timeframe
The article does not specify the timeframe during which the database searches were conducted in Scopus and Web of Science (WoS). This information is crucial, as the number of publications indexed in these databases evolves continuously, potentially influencing the comprehensiveness and accuracy of the literature analysis. To ensure consistency and reproducibility, it is recommended that future studies clearly report the exact dates of database searches and conduct them simultaneously in both Scopus and WoS to minimize discrepancies and maintain methodological rigor.
2) Inclusion of References as Scientific Evidence
Several statements presented in the introduction section appear to reflect opinions or conclusions that are not supported by appropriate scholarly references. It is strongly recommended to ensure that all such assertions be accompanied by relevant citations from credible academic sources. Incorporating references will strengthen the validity and credibility of the claims made, grounding them in existing scientific literature and enhancing the overall academic rigor of the paper.
3) Clarity Regarding the Use of Analytical Tools
This article does mention the use of Microsoft Excel and VOSViewer; however, the nature of the analysis and the specific analytical methods used remain unclear. For example, in Figure 7, what analytical approach was used, and what tools were utilized? Furthermore, Figure 8 suggests that the visualization is the result of burst analysis using the Scitespace tool. This statement is speculative, as the source material does not explicitly address this point. Clear explanations and elaborations are essential. To enhance methodological transparency and reproducibility, this study should provide a clear mapping between the tools used and the specific analyses conducted.
4) Clarify the PRISMA Flow Diagram
The stages in the PRISMA method have already been explained in the section on inclusion and exclusion criteria. However, it is still unclear to me what the criteria are for “report not retrievable = 305” and also for “record excluded = 167.” It would be better to present the criteria in the PRISMA flow diagram.
5) Discussion on Limited Practical Case Studies
The number of practical case studies employed to illustrate theoretical insights is limited. It is imperative to provide concrete examples of successful mobile payment systems in diverse contexts.
