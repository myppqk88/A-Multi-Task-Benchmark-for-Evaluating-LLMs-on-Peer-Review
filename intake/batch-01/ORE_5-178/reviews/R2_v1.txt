    for the EICAA project (EICAA-CF). EICAA stands for the Entrepreneurial and Intrapreneurial
    Competences Assessment Alliance. It is an Erasmus+ project that aims to bridge the gap between
    tools that assess individuals' entrepreneurial competencies and the adoption of tailored training
    actions.
    To achieve this objective, it was decided to use EntreComp as a reference, as it is promoted by the
    European Commission’s Joint Research Centre. However, since EntreComp was developed a
    decade ago, it requires revision. To this end, a two-part investigation was conducted. In the first
    part, a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) on entrepreneurial competencies was undertaken. In
    the second part, a prospective analysis using the Delphi method was employed to build on the
    previously obtained results. The results of the first part include relevant references from the
    literature, which are quantified in tables included in this study. These tables help confirm some
    findings and address questions or supplement others by the subsequent Delphi process.
    In conclusion, the modified competency framework is presented. This framework is an evolution
    of EntreComp, with some key aspects highlighted at the end of the article. In the ‘Ideas and
    Opportunities’ area of competencies, the concept of design was included. In the “Resources” area,
    the notion of digital competencies was introduced. Finally, in ‘Into Action’, the concept of process
    management was incorporated.

    To the authors: Thank you for submitting this paper. The approach of reviewing the literature and
    combining this with expert input in the context of a Delphi study is adequate and balanced. As far
    as I saw, the deductive coding to the EntreComp Framework was comprehensible. Still, when
    looking at some of the assignments to categories, one may ask whether a more inductive
    approach would have yielded additional insights about potential alternative categorizations. I
    understand that you avoided such an inductive approach as you wanted to respect the original
    categories of the EntreComp Framework. I still suggest mentioning this choice (even more)
    explicitly and making it a topic in a limitations paragraphs of the paper. After all, the literature
    discusses several alternative category systems (e.g., the categories for self competences, social
    competences, and domain competences). Apart form this limitation, your research suggests new
    aspects (design competence, digital competence, and process competence) that are without doubt
    of eminent importance - these are indeed intersting findings.
    Overall, the paper makes a relevant and solid contribution.
