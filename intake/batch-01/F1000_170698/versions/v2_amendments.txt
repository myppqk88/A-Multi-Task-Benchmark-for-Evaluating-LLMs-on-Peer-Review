Revised Amendments from Version 1
The previously submitted version was deeply and rigorously revised. As suggested by the second reviewer, the title was changed to “Project-based learning in STEAM subjects for socioformation: a systematic review of contributions, prevalence, and challenges”. The revised version incorporates substantial methodological, theoretical, analytical, and technical improvements in response to reviewers’ feedback.
First, the methodology was strengthened. Search criteria were refined using systematic Boolean combinations (AND, OR, NOT), and the publication time span was narrowed to 2015–2026 to enhance relevance. The sample size increased from 20 to 32 studies following an expanded search strategy. The PRISMA flow diagram was corrected, inter-rater reliability (IRR) was added, and risk of bias was rigorously assessed using the ROBIS protocol, indicating low overall risk.
Second, the theoretical framework was expanded. The manuscript moved beyond an exclusively constructivist foundation by integrating socioformation as a complementary and contemporary pedagogical model. The revised version critically contrasts traditional project-based learning (PBL) with socioformative approaches, particularly in the context of Artificial Intelligence (AI), sustainability, and territorial problem-solving. The shortcomings of traditional PjBL, especially its focus on products potentially automatable by AI, were analyzed.
Third, the results and discussion were deepened. Prevalence percentages were recalculated based on 32 studies and clearly explained. Greater attention was given to geographical distribution, education levels, and duration of implementation. The analysis now evaluates whether PBL initiatives promote socio-critical thinking, ethical engagement, and sustainable social development.
Finally, the title, abstract (IMRaD format), research questions, language, citation consistency, and typographical errors were revised to improve clarity, originality, and academic rigor, while the conclusion was refined to avoid overgeneralization.
