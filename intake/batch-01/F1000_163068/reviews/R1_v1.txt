I would like to thank the authors for this submission. The proposal of a Realist Review for Staff Student Partnership should add significant structure and rigour to theory and practice in this area. I particularly welcome the sharing of the protocol through this platform and at this stage to add transparency and the potential for future replicability. As such, I recommend this study protocol is Indexed and look forward to reading the outcomes of the research once it is published. In terms of feedback for the authors, below are some comments and recommendations that I strongly encourage them to consider and revise their protocol as it is currently published accordingly.
Background:
The background section generally sets the broad context around SSP well and draws on all of the relevant literature one would expect. It might benefit from some more practical examples of what a SSP interventions or activities might look like.
I recommend including more information and broader context about Irish HE for an international journal, it only focuses on Ireland in the context of SSP not generally It would be useful to have some evidence to support the claim that many Irish institutions have adopted SSPs E.g. referencing some studies such as ‘Brennan 2012, Reflections on establishing a student–staff partnership in Irish university-based teacher education’, ‘Munro et al 2022 Learning Together in, and about, Student-Staff Partnership in Higher Education’.
Study objectives and location:
The aims of the project are worthwhile and relevant, but there were some potential issues with their framing as research questions:
Q1, there are many variables here which might not overlap, namely that the enablers for students may be different than for staff
Q1, how is ‘meaningful’ being measured?
Q1, given the adoption of a broad definition of SSP (which is well rationalised) it might be challenging to place boundaries around ‘what counts’ for inclusion
Q2, “mindsets” and “modes” are very different things and raise questions of the ability to meaningfully answer this this research question
Q2, Assumes a uniformity of purpose of SSP, namely ‘improve the university experience for all’ which might not be relevant for all initiatives
Much of the concerns I have about the research questions are fairly well resolved by the methodology, but further clarity of purpose here will help the authors with reporting and for potential readers of the research.
Methods:
The rationale for a realist inquiry is well explained and promises an interesting addition to the literature that gets more meaningfully under the skin of partnership.
CMOC is well explained in general terms but examples linked to HE are somewhat glossed over. As building a rationale for this method is key to distinguish this work amongst a range of other reviews in the field, this could benefit from expansion and identifying more of what this adds to knowledge beyond other review types. A potential area to address is that the examples provided for Realist Reviews seem to focus on more narrowly defined areas, whereas SSP is a principle underpinning many diverse practices.
When building a rationale for this approach (section: “The use of realist review in higher education”) the authors discuss existing systematic reviews and theoretical pieces but argue a broader study is needed about context. But in doing so they draw upon Realist methodological literature. This feels somewhat circular and tautological in reasoning, namely that a realist methodology justifies itself. Perhaps spending more time unpacking the limitations of the existing SSP reviews might strengthen this. Particularly, as the Mercer-Mapstone et al review for example does address relevant contextual aspects such as positive/negative outcomes and synthesises conclusions around making spaces and engagement practices. The sample search strategy in this protocol seems comprehensive and likely to yield a wealth of relevant publications. The effectiveness of this is a clear distinction between this and prior reviews which is worth emphasising further to defend the original contribution of this protocol.
I think the protocol would be enhanced with more information about the advisory groups. For the local reference group, further unpacking what types of SSP are undertaken by participants would allow scrutiny of the breadth or specificity of focus. For the Expert Panel, an indication of the members would be helpful too, particularly for F1000 given this journal’s emphasis on transparency in processes and removal of anonymity. But I will leave this to the discretion of the authors.
General comments:
CMO 'configuration' and CMO ‘framework’ are both used, I suggest choosing one for consistency
There are two headings ‘Methods’, please rectify
For the search strategy, it is worth noting that a number of journals that specialise in SSP (i.e.
International Journal of Students as Partners, Student Engagement in Higher Education, Journal of Education, Innovation, Partnership and Change
) are not associated with traditional publishers and can be inconsistently found on academic databases. I feel this is a limitation of structured reviewing generally rather than this design, but as I am very eager to see the comprehensive results of this review I hope some accommodation is made for this
