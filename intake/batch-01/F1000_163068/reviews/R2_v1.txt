The protocol “Exploring the impact of student-staff partnerships in Higher Education: A Realist Review” describes a literature study proposal aimed at better understanding how student-staff partnership improves the university experiences for all and which challenges are faced and how they are dealt with. The topic of this proposal is interesting and suitable for a realist review study; however, I also see room for improvement, which I will describe in my suggestions for revisions below.
In the Background section, the authors place considerable emphasis on the local context in Ireland, including an elaborated paragraph about this context upfront in the second paragraph of the protocol. Given that the rest of the protocol is globally relevant, it’s better not to emphasize the context excessively and mention it only in the Methods section.
Additionally, the Background would benefit from widening the literature review to include knowledge described in papers that use synonyms of SSP to describe similar initiatives (e.g., co-creation, participatory design). In the Background, you neglected this issue, which you did recognize in defining the search terms for the literature search. Better acknowledge it from the beginning. Related to this issue, it appears that you have overlooked relevant literature on challenges and their solutions (Bovill et al., 2016). There is a recent overview of this, from the perspectives of learners, teachers and educational institutions (Könings et al., 2021). It is incorrect to indicate this as a gap in the literature, so this passage needs revision in your text.
The aim of the study is interesting and important, but in the aim as well as the research question I struggle with the fact that you consider the effects to be applicable for all. In the “Framework of Stakeholder Involvement in Co-Creation” we distinguish between effect for the participating stakeholders in the partnership/co-creation and those who do not directly participate but hopefully benefit from the changes developed in the partnership initiatives. The examples of benefits that you describe are all mainly relevant to the direct participants, as they are related to the improved psychosocial learning environment, and motivation and metacognition. Only improved quality of educational design is likely to affect all. By considering all effects to be equally influencing all, you would overlook the important factor of being direct or indirect affected by SSP.
The second research question does not logically follow from the introduction, and I wonder what mindsets and modes of engagement are about. Maybe you can keep this more general, i.e. ‘personal characteristics and SSP approaches?
The argument for conducting a realist review is convincing and clear. There is some repetition in the sections on ‘Realist inquiry’ and ‘Realist review’.
The proposed method is well described.
Under ‘Advisory groups’, you stated that preliminary theories will be developed from the “scoping review”. Please clarify which scoping review.
Table 1 provides a good list of synonyms. What are your arguments to include design-based research as a synonym? Often, these initiatives do not really include learners in the way that SSP aims for.
In reviewing the studies and extracting the data, have you considered how to distinguish between true partnerships and pseudo-partnerships (Baradell & Bell, 2020)? When conducting a recent scoping review on the impact of SSP on learner well-being, we encountered the challenges of assessing whether learners were genuinely involved in the design and implementation of the initiative. I would recommend developing criteria to follow in the selection process.
Related to my earlier comment about effects “for all”, in the CMO – Outcomes you’ll have to differentiate between consequences for direct participants and those affected (which can be missing or even contradictory). I hope my suggestions will be helpful in strengthening the protocol. Good luck with the study. I look forward to seeing the results.
