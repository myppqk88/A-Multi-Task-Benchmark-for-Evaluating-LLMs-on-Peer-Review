       Dear Ursula, we would like to thank you for your kind comments and remarks, which were
       very helpful in strengthening the paper. Below we respond to the detailed suggestions that
       you raised:
       In response to your suggestion, with which we agree, the reference to the concepts of
       entrepreneurial universities has been removed from the text, since, as the reviewer
       suggests, these are concepts that are not developed in this manuscript, and would also limit
       the application of unicanvas to universities with this orientation. Precisely, one of the
       advantages of this tool is its versatility, which allows it to be applied to any university with
       any orientation, and to design tailored business models to achieve any value proposition.
       However, we have referred in the introduction to the quintuple helix model, where
       universities face their three traditional missions (teaching, research, and transfer) together
       with the contribution to the social and natural environment to contextualise the new ways
       of creating value for these institutions, the importance of developing business model
       innovation and to highlight that all these challenges require visual and dynamic tools such
       as the canvas. Pages 2 and 3 We have changed the sentence by extending the transfer
       process not only to the market but to the needs and demands of society as suggested by
       the reviewer. The consideration of the quintuple helix helps us to broaden the missions of
       the university. Pages 2 and 3. The articles suggested by the reviewer to reflect on the role of
       students in universities have been taken into account. In this sense, when analysing the
       "beneficiaries" block, we have reflected on the differences that Unicanvas proposes for
       private universities versus public universities, the former being market-oriented, and
       considering the student as the client, and the latter not necessarily, with the student being a
       resource rather than a client. After reviewing the literature, we have concluded that both
       public and private universities must be student-oriented, which in both cases is a key
       beneficiary. Page 7. We agree with the reviewer, and as pointed out in points 1 and 3, the
       versatility of the unicanvas tool, which can be applied to any university, has been
       highlighted in the paper. In this sense, we refer in the paper only to the marketisation of
       HEIs in the sense of broadening the beneficiaries of HEIs and to which universities can bring
