         Author Response 24 Oct 2023
         Marjan Mohammadzadeh

             ○  In a "Responsible Research approach" it is essential to incorporate participatory
                processes throughout all stages of research, and this is especially relevant with
                vulnerable populations. Considering what is reflected in the section
                “Experiences with participant recruitment resources and strategies”, it does not
                seem that the different source groups have been involved in the investigation
                from the outset, being part of the design of the recruitment strategies
                themselves. It does not seem appropriate for the authors to claim that it was
                obvious that some strategies would not work, because if they did, they should
                not have been incorporated. These issues should be part of Discussion, as they
                may be part of the reasons for the poor success of several of the strategies. Nor
                does it seem prudent to make judgments such as “This kind of feedback raised
                our concerns because we consider these statements as stereotyping refugee
                adolescents, and therewith as unethical and unprofessional, especially when
                expressed from those who are in direct contact with the refugee community”,
                given that no research has been carried out on these issues.
          Not all the data collection strategies used in this study were considered "participatory."
         The participatory approach was just one part of our data collection strategy, but it turned
         out to be very effective. We didn't initially plan to involve as many different sources as we
         eventually did. Given the vulnerability of the population, adding more sources could
         increase the risk, especially considering we already used over five different sources for data
         collection. We weren't sure about the effectiveness of our methods until we tried them. The
         observations and experiences we shared in this paper, such as noting that "major
         organizations were reluctant to get involved in research projects," were the results of our
         own experiences and observations, not preconceived ideas or prior knowledge. The
         feedback we received regarding some refugee centers was based on our real experiences
         and interactions. Other researchers may have different experiences and perspectives on
         these matters. In conclusion, we view this article as a reflection of our honest experiences
         during the data collection process rather than a theoretical discussion of data collection
         methods. Our hope is that this article can assist researchers in the same field in navigating
         the process more efficiently.
