               ○ The key finding was that trust was a critical factor in recruiting their target
                 population, whom they labeled as vulnerable. However, the authors do not
                 explore an extensive trust literature with vulnerable populations (citations
                 added). Nor do they fully explain the vulnerable nature of the adolescent
                 population. "The power of trustworthy people" in research is an important
                 discussion point that should be explored more in the context of future clinical
                 research approaches.
          Additional points and highlights related to the requested modifications have been included
          in the conclusion section and we have included additional literature addressing trust.
               ○ There are several ethical considerations involving coercion of this population: 1.
                 Utilizing peer pressure and 2. The value of gift cards to this population. These
                 are reasonable approaches, but considering this manuscript is specifically about
                 vulnerable populations, some explanation of these how these things were
                 managed is appropriate.
           It's worth noting that throughout our process, we did not encounter any instances of peer
          "pressure," Peer pressure would be significantly distant from the goals of our project.
          Rather, what we observed was peer support and trust, as you astutely pointed out in your
          previous comment. Our participants appeared happier and more at ease working together,
          as explicitly discussed in the paper under "Experiences with participant recruitment
          resources and strategies," part 4, paragraph 2. The” value of gift cards” is based on similar
          studies conducted in Germany and endorsed by the ethical committee. Nonetheless,
          various considerations regarding this matter are discussed in paragraph 10 of the
          discussion section.
