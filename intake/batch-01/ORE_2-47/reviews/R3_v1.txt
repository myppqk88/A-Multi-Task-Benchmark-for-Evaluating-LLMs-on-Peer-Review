      Maura Dowling
      School of Nursing and Midwifery, National University of Ireland, Galway, Galway, Ireland

      The discussion paper by Mohammadzadeh and colleagues presents a very interesting overview of
      the challenges in recruiting vulnerable populations for a qualitative study in a pandemic. The
      various strategies employed by the research team to recruit refugee adolescents in Germany for
      their study are discussed and obstacles are highlighted. In addition, the challenges to recruitment
      in a time of restrictions during the COVID-19 pandemic are presented.

      I have some minor comments for consideration that will further strengthen this paper.
          ○  The authors should reframe the discussion on the challenges of the first two approaches
             (major government and private organisations) in the context of gatekeepers. The discussion
             could be strengthened by reference to insights provided by Crowhurst and Kenney-MacFoy
             (2013) and the findings of Snowden and Young (2017) on gatekeeping as a continuum from
             unconscious to conscious.

          ○   Some attention to research reflexivity would also strengthen the discussion on accessing
              participants via gatekeepers and any possible power dynamics. The paper by Turhan and
              Bernard (2020) offers guidance on approaching reflexive accounts.
          ○   The participatory data collection strategy was found to be the most successful recruitment
              strategy. It would be interesting to mention any details of including some refugee
              adolescents on the research team when designing the study (public and patient
              involvement [PP1]). You may have done this or it may not have been possible due to the
              pandemic, but it is not clear from the account provided.
          ○   Rephrasing of the following sentences is needed where the meaning is not entirely clear: a)
              The last sentence in the section ‘ethical considerations’; ‘ …according to the informed
              consent forms…’ ; b) Discussion, paragraph 5, the first sentence; what does ‘more and less
              predictable’ mean?

          ○   Some minor typos noted as follows: a) Introduction, paragraph 3, sentence beginning with
              ‘In other words…in one or more characteristic [sic]’; b) Introduction, paragraph 3, sentence
              beginning with ‘However, scientific studies …instead of the whole populations [sic]’
