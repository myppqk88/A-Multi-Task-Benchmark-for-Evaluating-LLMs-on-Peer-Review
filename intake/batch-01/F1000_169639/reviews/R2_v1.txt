Predicting Consumer Adoption of Luxury Products via Instagram Marketing: A Machine Learning Approach
The purpose of this paper, "Predicting Consumer Adoption of Luxury Products via Instagram Marketing: A Machine Learning Approach," is to investigate the factors that influence the purchase of luxury products on Instagram. The study employs a mixed-methods approach to collect data from 205 participants in India. The study contributes on the idea that consumer adoption was not significantly influenced by superficial engagement metrics. Further, psychological and brand-related factors, including personalised recommendations, brand trust, and brand prestige, were acknowledged as robust and significant predictors of purchase intention. The study also highlights the nature of the content which is crucial for brand trust. High accuracy was demonstrated by machine learning models (Support Vector Machine and Decision Tree) in predicting the likelihood of an individual adopting a product based on psychological, social, and demographic factors. The sentiment analysis of qualitative feedback revealed that the majority of consumers had favourable sentiments towards Instagram luxury marketing; however, there were some concerns regarding the authenticity of influencers. The paper concludes that luxury marketing on Instagram should shift from a focus on sheer visibility to the cultivation of emotional connections, trust, and the provision of personalised, high-quality content.
Comment: Revision
This study conducts an investigation into the factors influencing luxury product adoption on Instagram, utilising a mixed-methods approach that integrates traditional statistical analysis with machine learning models.  The examination of Instagram marketing for luxury goods in the emerging market of India highlights a significant gap in existing literature and is highly pertinent to the field. Advanced analytical techniques involve the integration of machine learning models. The discovery that "surface-level" engagement metrics, such as time spent and story ads, lack significance, whereas psychological factors like trust, prestige, and personalisation are strong predictors, represents a crucial and actionable contribution.  This challenges traditional marketing metrics.
Nonetheless, the study is considerably constrained by a significant limitation: The research is hindered by an absence of a unified theoretical framework, resulting in fragmented hypothesis testing. The theories are relevant; however, they are not cohesively integrated. It is essential to address these fundamental issues for the paper to achieve the standards of a high-quality indexing.
The sample size (n=205) is insufficient for a machine learning approach and is exclusively from India, which complicates the applicability of the findings to other cases.
The lack of clarity in the definition and examples of "luxury products" may indicate that individuals had in mind a variety of distinct categories of products.
The study examines six distinct hypotheses utilising four varied analytical methods.  A comprehensive conceptual model illustrating the interrelations among the constructs of engagement, trust, purchase intention, and adoption is lacking.  The theoretical and empirical distinctions between H2 (Purchase Intention) and H4 (Adoption) remain ambiguous.  This results in the paper appearing as a collection of distinct mini-studies instead of a unified work.
The results are presented clearly with helpful visualizations. However, the interpretation often oversteps the data by implying causality. The conclusions do tie the elements together but are framed with more certainty than the methodological design permits.
Omitted Variable Bias: Unmeasured factors, such as general consumer materialism, brand affinity, and cultural trends, are likely to affect both the independent variables, including perceived brand trust, and the dependent variable, which is adoption intention.
Discussion and Limitations: A specific subsection should be incorporated into the discussion and limitations sections to explicitly address the issue of endogeneity, clarifying that the results demonstrate predictive power rather than causal relationships.
Future Research: The authors ought to propose methodologies for subsequent studies to determine causality, including longitudinal designs, field experiments, or the application of instrumental variables.
Overall, I recommend a revision for the paper.
