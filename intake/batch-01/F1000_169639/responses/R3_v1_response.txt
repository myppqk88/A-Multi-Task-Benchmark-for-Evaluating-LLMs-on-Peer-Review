We sincerely thank the reviewers for their thoughtful and constructive comments, which have greatly enhanced the quality and clarity of this manuscript. Detailed responses to each point raised in the review comments are provided below, addressing the suggestions and concerns individually.
1. This study has five objectives. However, in the discussion, six hypotheses are presented. What is the connection between H6 and the previous five hypotheses?
Response:
H6 (positive consumer sentiment is associated with higher adoption) extends the quantitative findings by incorporating qualitative emotional insights. While the H1–H5 tests structural and behavioral relationships, H6 captures the affective dimension of consumer responses, enriching the understanding of adoption drivers. This aligns with research by Pang & Lee (2008) on the role of sentiment in shaping consumer behavior.
2. The five hypotheses appear to have no relation to one another. For example, the dependent variable in H1 is consumer adoption, while the dependent variable in H2 is Purchase Intention. What is the relationship between Consumer Adoption and Purchase Intention? Likewise, the dependent variable in H3 is Consumers’ Perception of Brand Trustworthiness. Furthermore, in H6, the term consumer sentiment suddenly appears. What is the connection between consumer sentiment and the variables from H1 to H5?
Response:
Consumer Adoption is the overarching outcome variable, while Purchase Intention (H2) and Brand Trustworthiness (H3) are mediating constructs that influence adoption. Sentiment (H6) acts as an affective precursor that shapes trust, intention, and adoption. This integrated view is supported by Bagozzi et al. (1999), who emphasize the role of emotion in decision-making.

4.  The study uses luxury products as its objects, but the type and examples of luxury products used have not been explained in sufficient detail. Are they apparel products (bags, sandals, shoes), clothing, or something else? Since luxury products come in many forms.

The study focused on luxury fashion and accessories, including items such as handbags, watches, apparel, and footwear. This has been explicitly stated in the methodology section to enhance clarity, following the categorization used in prior studies (e.g., Ko et al., 2019). (Included in Page 13)
5.  In figure 2, it is stated that H1 is not supported. Why does the analysis suddenly use logistic regression in H2 with different variables? What is the reason for using logistic regression to test H2 instead of multiple regression? Looking at the variables, the independent and dependent variables for H1 and H2 are completely different. What is being conveyed by using two different models for the same study?
Response:
H1 used multiple linear regression to test the effect of continuous engagement metrics on adoption. H2 used logistic regression because purchase intention was measured as a binary outcome (likely/unlikely). This approach is methodologically appropriate and common in consumer behavior studies (e.g., Hair et al., 2014).
6.  In the testing of H3, the analytical tool is path analysis (different from H1 and H2), with the dependent variable being Brand Trustworthiness. From figure 3, it can be seen that the engagement variable acts as the dependent variable, and not Brand Trustworthiness. Could you please explain this?
Response:
The diagram (DAG) for path analysis clearly shows that different content types first affect consumer engagement, which then predicts brand trustworthiness. Engagement is included in the model as a mediating variable rather than the main dependent variable; the final outcome we measure and report is brand trustworthiness. This analytical strategy allows us to quantify both the direct effect of content types on trustworthiness and their indirect effect through enhanced engagement. Thus, while engagement appears as a central node in Figure 4, the path analysis fully retains brand trustworthiness as the dependent variable, fulfilling the hypothesis and model structure as described. Engagement’s placement helps clarify how content strategies exert layered influences rather than altering the outcome variable itself.
7.  as a review summary, we would like to ask why so many analysis tools are being used (Multiple Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, Path Analysis, Support Vector Machines (SVM), Decision Tree modeling, and Sentiment Analysis) to test the research questions. please explain the sequence of using these tools according to the existing roadmap.
Response:
The mixed-methods design required diverse tools to address different research questions: regression for direct effects, path analysis for mediation, machine learning for prediction, and sentiment analysis for qualitative insight. This multi-method approach is advocated by Shmueli & Koppius (2011) for comprehensive model validation and depth.
