We sincerely thank the reviewers for their thoughtful and constructive comments, which have greatly enhanced the quality and clarity of this manuscript. Detailed responses to each point raised in the review comments are provided below, addressing the suggestions and concerns individually.
1. What are the implications of using a sample size of 205 for Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) analysis, and how might this impact the reliability of the findings?
Response:
A sample size of 205 is adequate for path analysis, which was used in this study, as it falls within the commonly accepted range for SEM (Kline, 2015). However, for more complex models with many latent variables, a larger sample would be preferable. Our model was parsimonious, and all paths were statistically significant, indicating sufficient power for the tested relationships.
2. How does the sample size of 205 affect the generalizability of the study's findings to the broader population of luxury brand consumers?
Response:
While the sample size of 205 is reasonable for exploratory and model-building research, it may limit generalizability across diverse cultural or demographic contexts. The study focused on Indian Instagram users, and future replications with larger, cross-cultural samples are recommended to enhance external validity.
3. Were any statistical techniques, such as bootstrapping or parceling, employed to mitigate potential issues related to sample size?
Response:
Bootstrapping was not employed in this study, but the use of machine learning models like SVM and decision trees, which are robust to smaller samples, helped complement the statistical analyses. Future studies will incorporate resampling methods to further validate the stability of the estimates.
4. How does the choice of test-train data split impact the performance and generalizability of the machine learning models used in the study?
Response:
The models were evaluated using standard train-test splits (e.g., 70-30 or 80-20) to ensure performance metrics were not overfitted. This approach helps in assessing model generalizability to unseen data, as recommended in predictive analytics literature (Hastie et al., 2009).
5. Can you provide more details on why specific statistical techniques and machine learning models, such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), were chosen for this study?
Response:
SVM was chosen for its effectiveness in handling high-dimensional data and non-linear relationships, which are common in behavioral datasets (Cortes & Vapnik, 1995). Logistic regression was used for interpretability of psychological predictors, while decision trees offered transparent demographic segmentation.
6. What are the most significant findings of the study, particularly with regards to the influence of brand trust, prestige, uniqueness, and personalized recommendations on purchase intention?
Response:
Personalized recommendations and brand trust were the strongest predictors of purchase intention, followed by brand prestige. Brand uniqueness, while positive, was not significant, suggesting that differentiation alone may not drive adoption without emotional or relational reinforcement.
7. What are the practical implications of the study's findings for luxury brand marketers, and how can they leverage Instagram to build brand trust and prestige?
Response:
Marketers should prioritize personalized content, foster trust through authentic storytelling and influencer transparency, and leverage interactive formats (e.g., polls, Q&As) to build emotional connections. Psychographic segmentation using ML models can also enhance targeting efficiency.
8. What are the implications of using an SVM model for predicting adoption likelihood, and how does this contribute to our understanding of luxury brand marketing on Instagram?
Response:
The SVM model’s high accuracy (87%) demonstrates the value of psychological and social constructs in predicting adoption. This supports the use of AI-driven tools in luxury marketing for precise consumer profiling and campaign optimization.
9. How do the study's findings contribute to our understanding of luxury brand marketing on Instagram, and what are the practical implications for marketers seeking to optimize their Instagram strategies?
Response:
The study highlights that emotional and symbolic factor—more than surface-level engagement—drive luxury adoption. Marketers should focus on trust-building content, personalization, and emotional resonance rather than mere exposure metrics.
10. What avenues for future research are suggested by the study's findings, and how might these inform the development of more effective luxury brand marketing strategies on Instagram?
Response:
Future studies could explore longitudinal designs, cross-platform comparisons (e.g., Instagram vs. TikTok), integration of neuro-marketing tools, and advanced NLP for sentiment analysis to capture nuanced consumer emotions.
