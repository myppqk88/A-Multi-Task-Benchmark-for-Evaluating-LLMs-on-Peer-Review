       The article discusses stylized facts related to ESM/EFSF bond issuances. The applied methodology
       is quite simple but in my view the paper reaches its goal in terms of sharing basic statistical
       features of the bond issuers that has had a great importance in dealing with sovereign funding
       crises in the 2010s.

       I would point out some minor and a couple of major recommendations in connection with the
       manuscript.

       Major:
          1. Inflated order book could be a result of 'market-testing', when participants on the auctions
             submit more bids with different pricings. They usually do this to ensure they will reach the
             amount of bonds they intend but they are unsure of other participants' bids. Honestly, I am
             not sure how EFSF/ESM auctions work, but even if it is not an issue (because of the feature
             of the auctions) authors should note this.

          2. I would recommend further analysis on the story behind Figure 1. It looks like yield
             premium over German bund increased around 2016-7 and it may be interesting what
             determined this spread. But it is rather a suggestion for further research than an actual
             criticism.
       Minor:
          1. I have found footnote 2 about benchmark and benchmark issuance but I found still
             confusing in the text. For the premia over German bonds, Bund-spread or spread over Bund
             benchmark would be clearer.

           2. It was not very meaningful to test the null hypothesis of the smaller estimate being actually
              smaller than the bigger one. Equality of these would make more sense from a perspective
              of formal testing, though I believe that it would not really change the outcome.

           3. I think Table 1 was misplaced, it would be better in the following section.
