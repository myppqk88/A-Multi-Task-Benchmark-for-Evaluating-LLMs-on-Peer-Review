Reviewer Comment 1: The abstract must be rewritten, giving greater focus on the outcome of the research.

The abstract has been completely rewritten in order both to feature our main findings and conclusions more prominently and clearly enunciate the spatial patterns of virtual activity, the established zone of influence, the temporal peak at 4 PM, and the methodological contributions of the study.
Reviewer Comment 2: The introduction part merely covers the background on research and does not include any discussion on comparable studies; furthermore, the research problem and targets are hardly clearly defined and need restructuring in an effort to make the arguments more systematic.
Response: We thank this useful commentary. We have substantially rewritten the introduction in response to these concerns:
The following new paragraph has been added, which reviews antecedent associated research and clearly outlines the research void which our study aims at compensating.
The overall research question and the three particular study targets have been clearly established.
The section has been reorganized to enhance its logical coherence, transitioning from a broad contextual overview to the particular gap in knowledge, our suggested methodology, and concluding with the case study.
Reviewer Comment 3: Clearly define the variables and parameters utilized in the study. Data analysis procedures should be provided so that the study can be replicated.
We both agree that this explanation is necessary in order to allow for reproducibility. Therefore, we have added a new subsection titled "Data Analysis Procedures and Variables" at the end of the Methods section. This inclusion succinctly lists the main variables analyzed (Timestamp, Geographic Coordinates, User ID) and provides a step-by-step, detailed description of the data analysis approach, from data cleaning to spatial network analysis, finishing in the final calculation of the interpolation. We also specify the important parameter (mean distance) utilized in the triangulation method.
Reviewer Comment 4: Quantitative symbology is needed on many maps, yet there is no clear need on Figures. Some interpretation is required in order to validate and interpret the results.
We are grateful to the reviewer for this perceptive remark. Although the figures themselves were not altered, we did significantly expand the description of every map in the Findings section:
For Figure 3, we explain that point size in Map E is also proportional to user activity levels. In Figure 4 (Map M), we present a comprehensive analysis, which interprets how the four impact categories are encapsulated by a normalized score obtained by our quantitative interpolation model, thereby directly associating the "Very High Impact" area with the city's culture corridor.
