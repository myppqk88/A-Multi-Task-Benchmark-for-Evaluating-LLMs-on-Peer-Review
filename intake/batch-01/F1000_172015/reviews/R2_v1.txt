Introduction
The article addresses an important and timely question in mathematics education: domain-specific patterns of achievement in TIMSS 2023, comparing South African Grade 5 learners with Singaporean Grade 4 learners. The topic is relevant and well grounded providing an opportunity for policy and practice impact in South Africa and the wider Sub-Saharan region.
The research purpose and alignment with TIMSS conceptual frameworks are quite clear. Descriptive statistical analysis and accurate identification of domain-specific weaknesses is evident.  There is observed an effective use of TIMSS as an international benchmark to highlight systemic performance gaps. The integration of literature from both high-performing (Singapore) and lower-performing (South Africa) systems is provided in a coherent narrative that links content and cognitive deficits to curriculum and instructional factors.
However several conceptual and methodological limitations  which currently reduce the manuscript’s rigor. have been identified. Revisions are required, particularly in theoretical framing, TIMSS- specific methodology, and the depth of interpretation and contextualization. Some minor corrections have also been identified for authors attention for proof reading
The  following observations have been made and related. action points  suggested for consideration
A): Presentation
The article  is generally well structured, articulate, and contextually grounded. The introduction to the article provides a clear rationale for focusing on South African primary mathematics achievement using TIMSS 2023 and situates the work within persistent national under performance. The performance gap (- 250 points) between South African Grade 5 and Singaporean Grade 4 learners is clearly stated and framed as a systemic issue rather than a purely learner-level deficit. The need to move beyond overall scores to content and cognitive domain analysis is well argued and aligned with TIMSS reporting structures. In addition, the contribution of the study, especially the focus on Grade 5 as an under-researched phase relative to Grade 9, is well articulated
The following issues were noted in the presentation
The rationale  of benchmarking South African Grade 5 learners against Singaporean Grade 4 learners though  methodologically common in TIMSS is not well explained. The clarification of why Singapore Grade 4 is used as a comparator is necessary . Secondly ,the article introduces and sustains a misaligned reference to Early Childhood Education (ECE). This introduction at one point frames the work as focusing on
Early Childhood Education,
which is conceptually inaccurate for TIMSS Grade 5. Grade 5 sits in the primary/intermediate phase, not ECE.. In addition, the conceptual rationale for the  theoretical framing of content and cognitive domains did not recognize frameworks such as: TIMSS assessment framework, Kilpatrick et al.’s strands of mathematical proficiency and  theories of cognitive demand and learning progression,. Finally, there is need to clarify  the research gap.  It is stated that Grade 5 and domain-specific analyses are under-researched, but this gap is not supported by specific citations and a brief synthesis of existing work..
As a result, the author is encouraged  to Action as follows
Clearly justify the benchmarking of South Africa Grade 5 against Singapore Grade 4.
Remove or revise all references to ECE; and  use more accurate terms
Strengthen theoretical framing by linking content and cognitive domains to recognized frameworks (e.g., mathematical proficiency, cognitive demand, learning trajectories).
Clarify the research gap with citations that show the relative neglect of Grade 5 and domain-specific analyses.
Streamline the introduction by reducing redundant statements and improving paragraph transitions
B: Study Design and Data Sources.
The use of quantitative secondary analysis of TIMSS 2023 data is appropriate and well aligned with the research questions. The sample sizes and the national representativeness of the TIMSS samples justifies the generalizability of the findings. The recognition of  TIMSS as a well-established international assessment enables valid cross-national comparisons. The description of the test design is broadly accurate and shows familiarity with large-scale assessment methodology.
The following issues were observed
The  description of TIMSS technical procedures in the article is incomplete.  Although TIMSS design is mentioned, the article does not explicitly state how plausible values (PVs) were handled in the analysis. These details will help to confirm that TIMSS technical standards were followed.
The
analysis does not explain whether or how contextual variables collected during the study (SES, language of instruction, teacher qualifications, opportunity-to-learn indicators) were used. Given TIMSS’s strong emphasis on context, this is a missed opportunity for explanatory depth.
For the psychometric and reliability considerations, there is need for reference to internal consistency, While TIMSS provides validated items, secondary analyses typically reference psychometric adequacy or refer explicitly to IEA technical documentation. Inclusion of some justification of Grade-Level Comparison is missing
.
A rationale for South Africa testing at Grade 5 (rather than Grade 4) for the primary cycle should be explicitly noted to strengthen the conceptual legitimacy of the comparison with Singapore Grade 4.
The following action points are suggested
Ex
plicitly describe
the use of plausible values (number of PVs, which domains, how handled in analysis).
Detail the weighting strategy
, including sampling weights and replicate weights, and explain how the two-stage sampling structure was accounted for.
Indicate whether contextual variables
(SES, language, school resources, teacher characteristics) were included in any extended analysis; if not, explain this limitation.
Reference psychometric evidence
(e.g., IEA technical reports) to reassure readers about reliability and validity of domain scores.
Clarify the grade-level choice
for South Africa and how it affects comparability with Singapore Grade 4.
C: Methodology and Statistical Analysis The categorization of content domains (Number, Measurement & Geometry, Data) and cognitive domains (Knowing, Applying, Reasoning) is appropriate and consistent with TIMSS. The use of weighted estimates, mean differences, and effect sizes (Cohen’s d) is methodologically  suitable for characterizing performance gaps..
The following issues were observed:
To avoid biased estimates, domain-level comparisons in TIMSS require appropriate PV handling and replication methods. It is not clear whether the analysis uses domain-specific plausible values correctly
For the variance estimation and significance testing, it is does not specify whether standard errors, confidence intervals, or p-values were computed. Descriptive statistics without uncertainty estimates weaken inferential robustness. The author had limited use of advanced modelling. The analysis is largely descriptive (means and effect sizes)..Given the richness of TIMSS data, multivariate methods (e.g., regression, multilevel models, mediation with contextual variables, DIF analyses) could substantially deepen the insights. While Cohen’s D  is reported, the interpretation of effect sizes in context is limited. There is little discussion of what constitutes a small/medium/large effect in educational terms.,
Suggested action points
Specify analytical tools and procedures used to handle PVs and weights (software, settings, treatment of domains).
Report standard errors and/or confidence intervals for key estimates and clarify whether hypothesis tests were performed.
Where feasible, incorporate multivariate or multilevel analyses to examine how contextual variables relate to domain-specific performance.
Interpret effect sizes in educationally meaningful terms, not just numerically.
D: Interpretation of Findings, Discussion, and Conclusion
The discussion is comprehensive and analytically rich. Content and cognitive domain findings and linking them to curriculum, pedagogy, and systemic factors is integrated well. Content-level weaknesses  and cognitive weaknesses are clearly interpreted and consistent with previous research .Policy and practice implications including pillars of reform around foundational knowledge, geometry/spatial reasoning, and leveraging application skills to cultivate reasoning is well structured. The conclusion offers a strong synthesis, links to broader goals such as SDG 4, and emphasizes curriculum-pedagogy-assessment coherence through Curriculum Alignment Theory
Issues observed included  the need for greater engagement with learning progression theory, to deepen the explanatory power.
Some  claims require evidence or qualification statements such as “procedural accuracy over conceptual exploration” or “curriculum spreads content thinly” need either citation or more cautious phrasing.
The claim that learners are “stronger in applying” requires quantification and statistical validation .
ECE References  in this section should also be expunged and corrected .
To maintain academic balance, the discussion on using  Singapore as a model should acknowledge potential constraints such as high academic pressure, streaming, selective teacher recruitment, and cultural expectations.
Some weaknesses (e.g., geometry and knowing) are repeated in multiple sections affecting  structure   In Pillar(3) three  repetition  is noted, The discussion is not using empirical evidence and relies of vague quantifiers. The structure of the discussion could be sharpened with clearer subheadings and more concise phrasing.
Some
recommendations
(e.g., reducing class sizes) may be politically or financially challenging; indicating alternative or incremental strategies would strengthen the practical relevance.
The conclusion would benefit from a brief statement of study limitations (e.g., reliance on secondary data, limited use of contextual variables, cross-sectional design) and directions for future research.
Action points suggested
Deepen theoretical engagement by explicitly drawing on mathematical proficiency and learning progression frameworks.
Support key claims with evidence (citations or data) and quantify statements about “relative strength” in applying.
Remove or reframe ECE references, using appropriate phase terminology.
Provide a more nuanced view of Singapore, noting contextual constraints and non-transferable features.
Consolidate repeated material on geometry/knowing weaknesses and improve subheadings and paragraph transitions.
Comment on feasibility of policy recommendations and suggest realistic pathways or phases of implementation.
Add a short section on study limitations and future research directions
replace vague quantifiers with measurable empirical evidence
Summary of Key Action Points for Revision
Conceptual and Theoretical
Clarify the benchmarking rationale (Singapore Grade 4 vs South Africa Grade 5).
Correct ECE misalignment and use appropriate phase terminology.
Strengthen theoretical framing using TIMSS, mathematical proficiency, and learning progression frameworks.
Provide a more balanced portrayal of Singapore, acknowledging contextual constraints.
Methodological and Statistical
Explicitly describe handling of plausible values, sampling weights, and replicate weights.
Report standard errors, confidence intervals, and significance tests for key comparisons.
Consider multivariate or multilevel analyses using contextual variables
Interpret effect sizes substantively, not just numerically.
Context and Interpretation
Integrate contextual questionnaire data (SES, language, resources, teacher factors) where possible.
Link performance patterns explicitly to systemic factors (teacher knowledge, curriculum pacing, language of learning and teaching)
Ensure claims are evidence-based and appropriately referenced.
Structure and Presentation  >>minor corrections
Streamline the introduction and discussion, removing redundancy and improving coherence.
Strengthen section headings and transitions for easier navigation.
Include tables or figures with domain-specific means, SEs, and effect sizes
Add a concise section on limitations and future research.
