Thank you very much for your thoughtful and encouraging review. We are grateful for your recognition of the relevance of our work and the constructive suggestions you provided. In response to your comments, we have made several additions and clarifications in the manuscript:
We reinforced in the discussion that the absence of a control group limits our ability to attribute attitudinal changes exclusively to the intervention.
We revised our interpretation of OSF account creation, framing it as an introductory step rather than a true behavioural shift.
We added a remark in the limitations section emphasizing that relying solely on OSF may have underestimated the extent of open science engagement across disciplines. We made it clear that future research should track behavioural changes through a broader set of platforms and metrics.
We now highlight the value of follow-up with qualitative methods, such as interviews or focus group discussions, to better understand barriers to behavioural change.
We acknowledged that the questionnaire, although piloted, was not formally validated, and we recommend future validation with psychometric expertise.
We addressed the limitation of self-selection bias and the context-specific nature of the cohort, suggesting the need for broader, multi-institutional studies.
Finally, we now emphasize in the conclusion that educational efforts alone are insufficient for sustained behavioural change, and must be paired with institutional and structural incentives.
Thank you again for your careful review and for helping us improve the manuscript.
