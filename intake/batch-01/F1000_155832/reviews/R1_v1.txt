This article examines a pre-post study evaluating an educational intervention’s impact on graduate students’ understanding of open science and responsible research practices. While the intervention had limited effect on actual implementation (within the period studied), it did lead to an increase in intention to adopt open science practices.
I think the authors do a good job of identifying the limitations of the study. I also agree with Sarahanne that the Registered Report format would have been preferable to be able to resolve some of the larger design concerns before data collection.
Nevertheless, I will focus on things the authors can change within the write-up itself, now that the study has been completed.
1. I think the description of the increase in OSF accounts is misleading -- we later find out that students has to make an OSF account as part of the course itself, so I don't actually think this makes sense as an outcome measure. Instead, perhaps it would make sense to frame it as "despite the vast majority of students instructed to make an OSF account during the course doing so, [results about outputs on OSF]". So, the course helped them with one barrier to sharing outputs on the OSF (creating an account), but this wasn't enough to push them to share outputs.
2. There is currently little rationale for the time points chosen for follow-up. It's ok if the rationale is logistical, but then this should be made clear.
3. It would be helpful to have some detail about how the course was developed and by whom.
4. The course is a great undertaking! The authors may consider sharing the course with FORRT (https://forrt.org/) so that it can be disseminated widely and further refined in future versions.
5. The sentence "This platform was chosen because the participants had no prior knowledge of any OS tools" on page 6 is misleading -- there's no way of knowing that participants had no prior knowledge of OS tools, and there were a few accounts already, so at least some did have knowledge of the OSF. One rationale for the OSF is that it is a "one stop shop" for many different practices, rather than needing to learn multiple tools in order to engage in open science.
6. The distinction between traditional and non-traditional seems a bit forced and the rationale for this split and definitions / conceptualisations could be clearer.
7. There isn't a rationale provided for why some of the scales go from "does not contribute" to "contributes to good practices" -- isn't it possible that participants think these things contribute to BAD practices?
8. Figure 7 caption is wrong -- same as Figure 5
9. "However, creating an OSF account was one of the tasks to be carried out by students in the course, demonstrating that the best incentive to adopt new practices is to make them normative. This supports the idea that the role in changing policies of institutions and funders are essential for inducing a broad adherence to OS practices and RRPs, which was previously showed." -- I'm not sure about this, doesn't this just show that if you ask students to do something as part of the course they'll do it?
10. "This finding suggests that educational interventions should be implemented as a routine." -- I'm not sure this conclusion follows.
11. It would be good to introduce something about models of behaviour change (e.g. the COM-B model) when talking about behavioural change in the discussion. Something that might be interesting (although take with a pinch of salt as it is my own paper) is Silverstein et al. (2024) where we show that capability, opportunity, and motivation to engage in open research practices all affect engagement in open research practices. We found automatic motivation to be the strongest -- that is, having open research as part of your research routine and habits.
