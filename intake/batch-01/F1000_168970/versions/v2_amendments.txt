Revised Amendments from Version 1
This Version 2 of “Modernizing Commercial Agency Regulations in Saudi Arabia: Legal Reforms and Comparative Insights” reflects substantial conceptual, structural, and stylistic revisions in direct response to peer reviewers’ feedback. The title, author list, and affiliations remain unchanged.
1. Conceptual Enhancements:
A strengthened theoretical framework now links the reforms to debates on legal transplants, institutional adaptation, and law-and-development. A new “Conceptual Framing” segment concludes the Introduction, and the Methodology and Sources section clarifies the analytical corpus (2018–2024). The new Comparative Framework emphasizes contextual adaptation, Shariah compatibility, and Vision 2030 alignment, highlighting Saudi Arabia’s indigenous reform design.
2. Doctrinal and Comparative Expansion:
The doctrinal analysis now systematically maps old and new statutory provisions, supported by an Old vs. New Regime Table showing differences in registration, termination, dispute resolution, and transparency. The comparative discussion has been deepened to explain how calibrated reforms enhance predictability and institutional coherence.
3. Empirical and Shariah Integration:
Empirical claims on FDI, arbitration, and business indicators are now supported by time-series data (2018–2023) from official sources. Section 6.1 adds examples of judicial reconciliation between statutory text and Shariah doctrines (gharar, fasakh, akl al-māl bil-bāṭil).
4. Structural and Policy Refinements:
Repetition was reduced, sentences simplified, and Vision 2030 context centralized in the Introduction. The Policy Recommendations section was reorganized into three clusters: judicial capacity, regulatory transparency, and regional harmonization. References were verified and reformatted consistently.
Collectively, these revisions strengthen the article’s theoretical foundation, doctrinal precision, and overall analytical coherence.
