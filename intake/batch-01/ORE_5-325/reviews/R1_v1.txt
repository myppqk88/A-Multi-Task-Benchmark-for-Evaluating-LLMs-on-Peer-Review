       Dear Valerio,

       We found your paper genuinely interesting to read. The topic—exploring how Cuban returnees
       transition from low-status work abroad to entrepreneurial pursuits back home—is both timely and
       important. Your ethnographic material is rich and engaging, offering real insight into lives shaped
       by migration, economic crisis, and return. The narratives of Joel, Roberto, Jorge, and others bring
       these experiences vividly to life. However, the manuscript needs substantial work before it can be
       considered as an academic research article.

       While the stories you tell are compelling, the paper currently reads more like a collection of
       ethnographic narratives than a structured academic article. This isn't to diminish the value of what
       you've captured—quite the opposite. The material is too good not to be given proper analytical
       treatment and theoretical framing. Below, we outline the main areas for improvement in this
       paper.

       The manuscript lacks the conventional structure readers expect from an academic article. You
       have an Introduction and Methods section, then several narrative subsections, and finally a
       lengthy Conclusion—but critically, there is no dedicated Results section and no standalone
       Discussion section. This makes it difficult to distinguish where you're presenting findings, where
       you're interpreting them, and where you're connecting them to broader scholarly conversations.

       Your Introduction currently reads as study background—it tells the story of how you came to this
       research—rather than positioning the study academically. The first-person narrative ("I was
       carrying out field research...") gives it an autobiographical quality that, while engaging, doesn't
       serve the purpose of an introduction.

    Missing critical elements:
        1. No substantive literature review: You cite relevant work, but don't engage with it
           systematically. There's no synthesis of key debates in return migration studies, no mapping
           of where your study fits, and no clear articulation of what gap you're addressing.
        2. Limited and dated citations: The literature cited is relatively thin and, in places, dated.
           Return migration, transnational entrepreneurship, and diaspora studies are well-
           established, dynamic fields with substantial recent scholarship that should be engaged.
        3. No research aim or questions: You never explicitly state what you're trying to find out. While
           readers can infer your interests, an academic article needs a clear statement of aims or
           research questions.
    Your empirical material touches on several theoretical areas that you don't explicitly address, such
    as:
        ○  Diaspora studies and the diaspora effect: Your returnees maintain transnational
           connections and identities—this needs theoretical grounding
        ○  Circular migration theory: The back-and-forth patterns you describe align with circular
           migration literature
        ○  Entrepreneurship theories:
               ○  Entrepreneurial typologies and motivations
               ○   Mixed embeddedness theory (entrepreneurial agency in constrained contexts)
               ○   Push versus pull entrepreneurship
               ○   Transnational entrepreneurship
        ○   Social and cultural capital: How returnees leverage their overseas experience
        ○   Cultural adaptation and clash: The tensions between "European" and "Cuban" business
            practices
        ○   Identity and belonging: Questions of who returnees become in relation to both contexts
        ○   Moral economy: You gesture toward this, but it needs fuller development
    Your Methods section is simultaneously too long (because it contains Results material) and too
    thin (because it lacks procedural detail).
    Specific problems:
        1. Sample ambiguity: You state "approximately 15–20 Cuban returnees" then later "mainly
           men." You need to specify:
               ○ Exact number of participants (was it 15? 18? 20?)
               ○   Why this sample size?
               ○   What were your inclusion/exclusion criteria?
        2. Case selection unexplained: Despite having 15-20 participants, you present only five in
           detail (Roberto, Humbe, Joel, Jorge, Pedro). Why these five? Are they representative?
           Exceptional? Purposefully selected to illustrate variation? The cases are interesting, but this
           could be a case study method—if so, you need to justify your case selection explicitly.
        3. Data collection procedures unclear:
               ○  How many conversations/observations with each participant?
               ○   What was the duration of these interactions?
               ○   In what contexts (homes, businesses, social settings)?
               ○   When did you write field notes (during, immediately after, later)?
               ○   Did you use any interview guides or was it entirely unstructured?

        4. Analytical approach absent: You don't describe how you analysed your data. Did you:
                ○Use any software?
                ○   Identify themes iteratively or deductively?
                ○   Reach saturation? How did you know?
                ○   Use any validation procedures (triangulation, member checking, peer debriefing)?
    An example of a field note would help readers assess validity.
         5. Participant observation underspecified: "The method of participant observation needs
    more details" including your role, how you built relationships, what you participated in, and how
    you maintained analytical distance.
         6. Ethical approval timing unclear: You state research began in 2005, but ethical approval was
    obtained in 2017. This 12-year gap needs explanation. Did you have earlier approval? Was the
    2005-2017 work exploratory? This needs clarification for ethical integrity.

    Your writing is engaging and evocative—you're a skilled storyteller. But academic writing requires
    something different: it needs to move from description to analysis, from particular cases to
    general patterns, from narrative to argument. You present stories without sufficient analytical
    scaffolding.

    Your material touches on numerous analytical concepts that remain implicit, such as:
        ○ Types of entrepreneurship and entrepreneurial identity formation
        ○   Entrepreneurial motivation ("or be entrepreneurial?" "this also could be linked to
            motivation")
        ○   Cultural clash between business cultures
        ○   Perceptions and institutionalisation of entrepreneurship
        ○   Identity and belonging negotiations
        ○   Material aspects of entrepreneurship and consumerism
        ○   Social and human capital development
        ○   Agency and constraint
        ○   Returnees as "agents of change"
    These connections could be made explicit and systematic, not left for readers to infer.

    Without a Results section, your findings remain scattered. Without a Discussion section, your
    analysis lacks depth and your contribution remains unclear. You could:
        ○ Interpret findings in light of existing literature
        ○   Show how your findings confirm, challenge, or extend current knowledge
        ○   Develop theoretical insights
        ○   Address implications (what does this mean for understanding return migration?
            Transnational entrepreneurship? Cuban economic transformation?)
        ○   Acknowledge what you can and cannot claim based on your data
    Your Conclusion is currently doing work that should have been done earlier. It's introducing new
    theoretical references, presenting fresh case material, and offering interpretations that belong in
    Discussion. Reviewers need to know what new knowledge your study generates. Is it:
        ○ Empirical? (New information about an understudied group—Cuban returnees post-2012)
        ○   Theoretical? (New concepts or frameworks for understanding return migration

        ○   Methodological? (Novel approaches to studying transnational lives)
