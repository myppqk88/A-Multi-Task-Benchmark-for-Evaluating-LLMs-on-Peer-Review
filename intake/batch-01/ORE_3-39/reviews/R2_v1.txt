    In this TRIPLE case study, researchers and training organisers from the Social Sciences and
    Humanities are provided with: 1) Open Educational Resources for Open Science competence-
    oriented training sessions and 2) the TRIPLE Training Toolkit (i.e., guidelines for organising
    training events).

    I am impressed about how thoroughly the authors organized their trainings. For example, the
    topics for the trainings were selected based on a survey, and a short post training survey was
    handed out at the end of each training session to measure its impact. The results were very
    positive (e.g., more than 90% enjoyed the sessions). All materials were licensed (CC-BY-4.0) shared
    on Zenodo and NAKALA. However, I think there is also some room for improvement. I formulated
    my three concerns below.

    First, I doubt whether this article is really helpful for researchers. Maybe such materials are handy
    for researchers who study meta-science, and who thus teach about open science, but that is not
    necessarily a SSH field. Instead of open science training organizers and researchers I would rather
    say that the article is relevant for starting/beginners open science training organizers (or just:
    early career open science trainers) and support staff. Indeed, many universities now want to offer
    training on open science but support staff is often ill equipped. This would really help them out,
    and also help them to organise trainings without having to rely so heavily on the knowledge of
    researchers who volunteer to help out with such trainings (e.g., events are now often organised
    coming from bottom-up communities or initiatives).

    Second, the authors mention an online calendar of trainings, but I do not see how that is helpful.
    The materials are, but I find it difficult to see how a calendar of events that have already been
    organized is helpful to others. Could the authors explain maybe what they mean by the online
    calendar of training sessions?

    Third, the authors claim that their training series is FAIR by design, but they were less thorough on
    the FAIR part. For example, Table 3. is really simple. FAIR is so much. more than how they specify
    it in their table.
